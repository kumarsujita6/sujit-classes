Line-by-Line Conceptual Notes

6.1 Perimeter: Measuring the Boundary

The Perimeter of any closed two-dimensional plane figure is the total continuous distance covered around its outer boundary.

📏 Fundamental Perimeter Formulas:
  • General Polygon: Sum of the lengths of all outer boundary sides.
  • Rectangle: Opposite sides are equal (Length = l, Breadth = b).
    Perimeter = l + b + l + b = 2 × (l + b).
  • Square: All four sides are equal (Side = s).
    Perimeter = s + s + s + s = 4 × s.
  • Equilateral Triangle: All 3 sides are equal.
    Perimeter = 3 × s.
  • Regular n-gon (Regular Polygon): A polygon with n equal sides.
    Perimeter = n × (Side Length s).
    • Regular Pentagon (5 sides): 5s
    • Regular Hexagon (6 sides): 6s
    • Regular Octagon (8 sides): 8s

Wire Reshaping & Conservation of Perimeter:

When a piece of wire or string of fixed length L is bent into different shapes (e.g. from a rectangle into a square, or into an equilateral triangle), the total boundary perimeter remains strictly conserved:
Length of Wire = Perimeter of Rectangle = Perimeter of Square = Perimeter of Triangle = L.

Multi-Strand Fencing:

When a farmer fences a rectangular field with multiple strands (rounds) of barbed wire:
Total Wire Needed = (Number of Rounds) × (Perimeter of Field)
Total Fencing Cost = (Total Wire Needed) × (Rate per Meter)

6.2 Area: Measuring Enclosed Flat Surfaces

Area is the measure of the flat surface region enclosed strictly within the boundary of a closed plane figure. While perimeter is a one-dimensional linear measure (cm, m), area is a two-dimensional surface measure expressed in square units (cm², m², km²).

🟩 The 1 cm² Grid Square Counting Method: When finding the area of irregular shapes on graph paper:
  1. Count fully enclosed unit squares → each counts as 1 sq unit.
  2. Count squares that are half or more than half enclosed → each counts as 1 sq unit.
  3. Ignore squares that are less than half enclosed → counts as 0.
  4. Add up all counted units to obtain the approximate area.

Standard Formulas for Elementary Polygons:

  • Area of a Rectangle: The surface consists of 'l' columns and 'b' rows of 1 cm² squares.
    Area = Length × Breadth = l × b.
  • Area of a Square: A rectangle with l = b = s.
    Area = Side × Side = s × s = s².
⚠️ Crucial Unit Conversion Traps in Area: Linear conversions DO NOT apply directly to area! Because area involves squaring two dimensions:
  • 1 m = 100 cm ⇒ 1 m² = 100 cm × 100 cm = 10,000 cm² (NOT 100 cm²!).
  • 1 km = 1,000 m ⇒ 1 km² = 1,000 m × 1,000 m = 1,000,000 m².
  • 1 Hectare = 100 m × 100 m = 10,000 m².

6.3 Area of Triangles & Composite Figures

1. Area of a Right-Angled Triangle:

Draw a diagonal across any rectangle of base b and height h. The diagonal divides the rectangle into two identical, congruent right-angled triangles.
Since the two triangles together equal the area of the rectangle (b × h):
Area of a Right Triangle = ½ × Base × Height = ½ × b × h.

2. Decomposing Composite Shapes:

Irregular 2D figures (such as L-shapes, T-shapes, cross-shapes, and floor blueprints) cannot be evaluated using a single formula. They must be partitioned into non-overlapping elementary rectangles:
Total Area = Area(Rectangle 1) + Area(Rectangle 2) + ...

3. The Shaded Region / Border Theorem:

When a pathway, lawn border, or flower bed surrounds or sits within a larger plot:
Area of Border / Pathway = (Outer Area) − (Inner Enclosed Area).

6.4 The Perimeter vs. Area Optimization Paradox

One of the deepest insights in Grade 6 geometry is that perimeter and area are independent properties:

The Isoperimetric Principle for Rectangles: Among all rectangles having the exact same fixed perimeter, the Square encloses the MAXIMUM area!

Proof by Example (Fixed Perimeter P = 24 cm):
Length × Breadth Perimeter = 2(l + b) Area = l × b
11 cm × 1 cm2(11 + 1) = 24 cm11 cm²
10 cm × 2 cm2(10 + 2) = 24 cm20 cm²
9 cm × 3 cm2(9 + 3) = 24 cm27 cm²
8 cm × 4 cm2(8 + 4) = 24 cm32 cm²
7 cm × 5 cm2(7 + 5) = 24 cm35 cm²
6 cm × 6 cm (Square)2(6 + 6) = 24 cm36 cm² (MAXIMUM!)

Notice that as the rectangle becomes more square-like, its area grows from 11 cm² up to 36 cm², even though the perimeter remains exactly 24 cm throughout!

Section 6.1: Perimeter of Polygons (NCERT Solutions)

Q1: Complete the missing terms in the perimeter table: (a) Rectangle with length 12 cm, breadth 8 cm, (b) Rectangle with length 15 cm and perimeter 50 cm, (c) Square with side 7 cm, (d) Square with perimeter 64 cm.
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Dimensions or perimeters of rectangles and squares.
To find: Missing breadths, side lengths, and perimeters.

Step 2: Formulas

- Perimeter of Rectangle = 2 × (Length + Breadth) → Breadth = (Perimeter ÷ 2) − Length.
- Perimeter of Square = 4 × Side → Side = Perimeter ÷ 4.

Step 3: Step-by-step calculations

Part (a): Length = 12 cm, Breadth = 8 cm.
Perimeter = 2 × (12 + 8) = 2 × 20 = 40 cm.

Part (b): Length = 15 cm, Perimeter = 50 cm.
Breadth = (50 ÷ 2) − 15 = 25 − 15 = 10 cm.

Part (c): Side of square = 7 cm.
Perimeter = 4 × 7 = 28 cm.

Part (d): Perimeter of square = 64 cm.
Side = 64 ÷ 4 = 16 cm.

Step 4: Final values
(a) P = 40 cm | (b) b = 10 cm | (c) P = 28 cm | (d) s = 16 cm.
Step 5: Concluding statement

All missing geometric dimensions are calculated with exact linear units.

Q2 (Wire Reshaping): A rectangle having side lengths 5 cm and 3 cm is made using a piece of wire. If the same wire is rebent to form a square, what will be the side length of the square?
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Rectangle length l = 5 cm, breadth b = 3 cm. Wire is rebent into a square.
To find: Side length s of the resulting square.

Step 2: Principle of Conservation of Perimeter

Total Length of Wire = Perimeter of Rectangle = Perimeter of Square.

Step 3: Step-by-step arithmetic

1. Find perimeter of the rectangle:
Prect = 2 × (l + b) = 2 × (5 + 3) = 2 × 8 = 16 cm.
Total wire length = 16 cm.

2. Equate to perimeter of square:
Psquare = 4 × s = 16 cm.
s = 16 ÷ 4 = 4 cm.

Step 4: Final value / result
Side of square = 4 cm.
Step 5: Concluding statement

The square formed from the rebent wire has a side length of exactly 4 cm.

Q3: Find the length of the third side of a triangle having a perimeter of 55 cm, if two of its sides measure 15 cm and 20 cm.
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Triangle perimeter P = 55 cm, side a = 15 cm, side b = 20 cm.
To find: Length of third side c.

Step 2: Formula for Perimeter of Triangle

Perimeter = a + b + c → c = Perimeter − (a + b).

Step 3: Calculation

Sum of two known sides = 15 + 20 = 35 cm.
c = 55 − 35 = 20 cm.

Step 4: Final value / result
Third side = 20 cm (The triangle is isosceles!).
Step 5: Concluding statement

The third side of the triangle measures 20 cm.

Q4: What would be the cost of fencing a rectangular park whose length is 150 m and breadth is 100 m at the rate of Rs 24 per meter?
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Length l = 150 m, Breadth b = 100 m, Fencing rate = Rs 24 / m.
To find: Total cost of fencing the park boundary.

Step 2: Formula

Perimeter = 2(l + b). Cost = Perimeter × Rate.

Step 3: Step-by-step arithmetic

1. Perimeter = 2 × (150 + 100) = 2 × 250 = 500 m.
2. Cost of fencing = 500 m × Rs 24/m = Rs 12,000.

Step 4: Final value / result
Total Fencing Cost = Rs 12,000.
Step 5: Concluding statement

The total expense to erect a fence around the park is Rs 12,000.

Q5: A piece of string is 36 cm long. What will be the length of each side if the string is used to form: (a) a square, (b) an equilateral triangle, (c) a regular hexagon?
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: String length = 36 cm (Perimeter for all three shapes).
To find: Side length s for: (a) Square (4 sides), (b) Equilateral triangle (3 sides), (c) Regular hexagon (6 sides).

Step 2: Formula

Side length s = (Total Perimeter) ÷ (Number of Equal Sides n).

Step 3: Arithmetic calculations

(a) Square (n = 4):
s = 36 ÷ 4 = 9 cm.

(b) Equilateral Triangle (n = 3):
s = 36 ÷ 3 = 12 cm.

(c) Regular Hexagon (n = 6):
s = 36 ÷ 6 = 6 cm.

Step 4: Final value / result
(a) Square: 9 cm | (b) Triangle: 12 cm | (c) Hexagon: 6 cm.
Step 5: Concluding statement

The side length scales inversely with the number of equal polygon edges.

Q6 (Farmer's Multi-Round Fence): A farmer has a rectangular field of length 230 m and breadth 160 m. He wants to fence it with 5 rounds of barbed wire. What is the total length of wire required?
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Field length l = 230 m, breadth b = 160 m. Number of rounds = 5.
To find: Total length of barbed wire needed.

Step 2: Formula

Perimeter = 2(l + b). Total Wire = (Number of Rounds) × Perimeter.

Step 3: Calculations

1. Perimeter for 1 round = 2 × (230 + 160) = 2 × 390 = 780 m.
2. Wire needed for 5 rounds = 5 × 780 = 3,900 m.

Step 4: Final value / result
Total Wire Required = 3,900 m (or 3.9 km).
Step 5: Concluding statement

The farmer must purchase exactly 3,900 meters of barbed wire.

Q7 (Akshi vs. Toshi Running Race): Akshi runs along an outer track (perimeter = 220 m) and covers 5 rounds. Toshi runs along an inner track (perimeter = 180 m) and covers 7 rounds. Who ran a longer distance, and by how much?
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Distance = 5 × 220 = 1,100 m.

2. Toshi's Distance:
Distance = 7 × 180 = 1,260 m.

3. Comparison:
1,260 m > 1,100 m → Toshi ran a longer distance.
Difference = 1,260 − 1,100 = 160 m.

Step 4: Final value / result
Toshi ran further by 160 meters (Toshi: 1,260 m vs. Akshi: 1,100 m).
Step 5: Concluding statement

Toshi's higher lap count allowed her to out-distance Akshi by 160 meters.

Q10: A rectangular coconut grove is 100 m long and 50 m wide. If each coconut tree requires 20 sq m of land area to grow properly, how many coconut trees can be planted in the grove?

To find: Total number of coconut trees that can be planted.

Step 2: Capacity Formula

Total Trees = (Total Area of Grove) ÷ (Area per Tree).

1. Total Area = 100 m × 50 m = 5,000 m².

Section 6.3: Composite Shapes & Uncarpeted Floor Problems

Q11: The floor of a room is 5 m long and 4 m wide. A square carpet whose sides are 3 m long is laid on the floor. Find the area of the floor that is NOT carpeted.
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Room floor: 5 m × 4 m. Carpet: 3 m × 3 m.
To find: Uncarpeted floor area.

Step 2: Area Difference Principle

Uncarpeted Area = Area of Room Floor − Area of Carpet.

Step 3: Step-by-step arithmetic

1. Area of room floor = 5 m × 4 m = 20 m².
2. Area of square carpet = 3 m × 3 m = 9 m².
3. Uncarpeted area = 20 m² − 9 m² = 11 m².

Step 4: Final value / result
Uncarpeted Area = 11 m².
Step 5: Concluding statement

11 square meters of the room floor remain bare.

Q12 (The Perimeter vs. Area Paradox): Shape A has an area of 18 square units and Shape B has an area of 20 square units. Is it possible for Shape A to have a LARGER perimeter than Shape B? Explain with an example.
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Area(A) = 18 sq units, Area(B) = 20 sq units.
To find: Can Perimeter(A) > Perimeter(B)?

Step 2: Independence of Perimeter and Area

Perimeter depends on boundary shape and elongation; area depends on surface enclosed. A long thin rectangle has a huge perimeter despite small area.

Step 3: Concrete Counterexample

- Let Shape A be a very long, thin rectangle of dimensions 18 units × 1 unit:
Area(A) = 18 × 1 = 18 sq units.
Perimeter(A) = 2 × (18 + 1) = 2 × 19 = 38 units.

- Let Shape B be a compact rectangle of dimensions 5 units × 4 units:
Area(B) = 5 × 4 = 20 sq units.
Perimeter(B) = 2 × (5 + 4) = 2 × 9 = 18 units.

Comparison:
Area(A) = 18 < Area(B) = 20.
Yet Perimeter(A) = 38 > Perimeter(B) = 18!

Step 4: Final value / result
Yes, completely possible! (Perimeter 38 vs 18).
Step 5: Concluding statement

Larger area does NOT imply larger perimeter; shape geometry dictates boundary length.

Q1. A wire of length 32 cm is rebent to form a square. The area of the square formed is:

(a) 32 cm²
(b) 64 cm²
(c) 16 cm²
(d) 128 cm²

💡 View Correct Option & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: (b) 64 cm²

Mathematical Explanation: Wire length = Perimeter of square = 32 cm. Side s = 32 ÷ 4 = 8 cm. Area = s × s = 8 × 8 = 64 cm².

Conceptual Trap Alert: Option (a) confuses perimeter with area. Option (c) is the result of dividing 32 by 2 and thinking side is 4.

Q2. How many square centimeters are there in 1 square meter?

(a) 100 cm²
(b) 1,000 cm²
(c) 10,000 cm²
(d) 100,000 cm²

💡 View Correct Option & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: (c) 10,000 cm²

Mathematical Explanation: 1 m = 100 cm ⇒ 1 m² = 100 cm × 100 cm = 10,000 cm².

Conceptual Trap Alert: 100 cm² is the most common mistake made by students applying linear conversion to 2D area.

Q3. The length of a rectangle is doubled, and its breadth is halved. Its new area will:

(a) Double
(b) Be halved
(c) Remain unchanged
(d) Quadruple

💡 View Correct Option & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: (c) Remain unchanged.

Mathematical Explanation: New Area = (2l) × (b / 2) = l × b = Original Area.

Conceptual Trap Alert: The perimeter, however, changes! Only the area is preserved.

Q4. What is the area of a right-angled triangle whose base is 8 cm and perpendicular height is 5 cm?

(a) 40 cm²
(b) 20 cm²
(c) 26 cm²
(d) 13 cm²

💡 View Correct Option & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: (b) 20 cm²

Mathematical Explanation: Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm².

Conceptual Trap Alert: 40 cm² is the area of the enclosing rectangle (forgetting to divide by 2).

Q5. Among all rectangles having a fixed perimeter of 20 cm, which shape has the maximum area?

(a) A rectangle of 9 cm × 1 cm
(b) A rectangle of 8 cm × 2 cm
(c) A square of 5 cm × 5 cm
(d) A rectangle of 7 cm × 3 cm

💡 View Correct Option & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: (c) A square of 5 cm × 5 cm

Mathematical Explanation: Areas: 9×1 = 9 cm², 8×2 = 16 cm², 7×3 = 21 cm², 5×5 = 25 cm² (Max!). By the isoperimetric theorem, a square maximizes area for a given perimeter.

Conceptual Trap Alert: Long thin shapes look "bigger" to intuition, but enclose far less surface.

Part D: Long Answer Multi-Step Problems (5M)

Q15 (5 Marks): A rectangular park is 90 m long and 75 m broad. A path 5 m wide is to be built along and inside its boundary. (a) Find the area of the path. (b) Find the cost of gravelling the path at the rate of Rs 40 per square meter.
Step 1: Given data & What to find

Given: Outer dimensions: L1 = 90 m, B1 = 75 m. Path width w = 5 m inside.
To find: Area of path and cost of gravelling.

Step 2: Border Area Principle

Area of Path = Outer Area − Inner Area. Inner Length = L1 − 2w; Inner Breadth = B1 − 2w.

Step 3: Step-by-step arithmetic

Part (a): Area of Path [3 Marks]
1. Outer Area = 90 × 75 = 6,750 m².
2. Inner Length = 90 − (2 × 5) = 90 − 10 = 80 m.
3. Inner Breadth = 75 − (2 × 5) = 75 − 10 = 65 m.
4. Inner Area = 80 × 65 = 5,200 m².
5. Area of Path = 6,750 − 5,200 = 1,550 m².

Part (b): Cost of Gravelling [2 Marks]
Cost = 1,550 m² × Rs 40/m² = Rs 62,000.

Step 4: Final value / result
Area of Path = 1,550 m² | Total Cost = Rs 62,000.
Step 5: Concluding statement

The path covers 1,550 square meters with a total gravelling cost of Rs 62,000.

Exam Hall Trap Alerts & 15 Rapid Revision One-Liners

Top 5 Exam Hall Traps in Perimeter and Area

⚠️ Trap 1: Writing Linear Units for Area or Square Units for Perimeter

A classic loss of marks occurs when a student calculates an area as 50 cm instead of 50 cm², or a perimeter as 24 m² instead of 24 m. Always remember: Perimeter is a 1D line (cm, m), while Area is a 2D surface tile count (cm², m²).

⚠️ Trap 2: Converting Area Units Linearly (The 1 m² Trap)

Because 1 m = 100 cm, students frequently make the catastrophic mistake of writing 1 m² = 100 cm². In reality, area squares both dimensions: 1 m² = 100 cm × 100 cm = 10,000 cm²! Using 100 instead of 10,000 throws tile calculations off by a factor of 100.

⚠️ Trap 3: Forgetting to Subtract 2w on BOTH Sides for Internal Paths

When a border or pathway of width 'w' runs inside a rectangular park, the inner length is L − 2w and the inner breadth is B − 2w. Students often subtract 'w' only once (writing L − w), cutting the path width in half and yielding incorrect inner areas.

⚠️ Trap 4: Confusing Wire Reshaping Invariants

When a wire is bent from a rectangle into a square, its perimeter remains constant, but its area changes (the square encloses more area than the rectangle). Do not equate the area of the wire shapes!

⚠️ Trap 5: Omitting Number of Strands in Fencing Problems

When a problem asks for "4 rounds of barbed wire", calculating only the single perimeter P and stopping forfeits 2 marks. Always multiply: Total Wire = 4 × P.

15 Rapid Revision One-Liners (Pre-Exam Checklist)

  1. Perimeter is the total linear distance around the outer boundary of a closed plane figure.
  2. Perimeter of a rectangle is given by 2 × (Length + Breadth).
  3. Perimeter of a square is 4 × Side; perimeter of an equilateral triangle is 3 × Side.
  4. The perimeter of a regular polygon with n sides is n × (Side Length).
  5. When a wire of fixed length is bent into different polygons, its perimeter is conserved.
  6. Area measures the flat 2D surface enclosed within a closed boundary.
  7. Area of a rectangle is Length × Breadth; area of a square is Side × Side.
  8. Area of a right-angled triangle is ½ × Base × Height.
  9. 1 square meter equals 10,000 square centimeters (1 m² = 10,000 cm²).
  10. 1 hectare is the area of a 100 m × 100 m square field, equal to 10,000 m².
  11. Two figures having the same perimeter do NOT necessarily have the same area.
  12. Among all rectangles of a given fixed perimeter, a square encloses the maximum area.
  13. The area of a pathway around a rectangular plot equals (Outer Area) − (Inner Area).
  14. Cutting small squares out of the four corners of a large square reduces the area but leaves the perimeter unchanged.
  15. Cost of boundary fencing uses perimeter; cost of flooring, tiling, or turfing uses area.
