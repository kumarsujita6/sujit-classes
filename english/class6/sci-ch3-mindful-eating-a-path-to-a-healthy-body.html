1. Ancient Wisdom: The Three Principles of Healthy Living

Chapter 3 begins with an ancient Sanskrit Subhashita (wise saying):

The Great Ayurvedic Question:
"Ko’ruk? Ko’ruk? Ko’ruk?" (Who is healthy? Who is free from disease?)
"Hitabhuk, Mitabhuk, Ritubhuk."
Teacher's Simple 3-Rule Meaning:
  1. Hitabhuk: One who eats wholesome, nourishing food that is good for the body (not greasy junk).
  2. Mitabhuk: One who eats in moderate, controlled quantities (never overeating or starving).
  3. Ritubhuk: One who eats food appropriate to the season, climate, and local geography!

2. The Seven Essential Components of Food (Nutrients)

Food is the chemical fuel of the human machine. The substances present in food that our body needs to live, grow, and remain healthy are called nutrients.

1. Carbohydrates: The Primary Energy Source

  • Role: Main provider of instant energy for walking, running, studying, and breathing.
  • Forms: Mainly present in our food as Starch (complex carbohydrate in grains and tubers) and Sugars (simple sweet carbohydrates like glucose, fructose in fruits).
  • Rich Sources: Rice, wheat, maize, millets, potatoes, sweet potatoes, bananas, sugarcane, jaggery.

2. Fats: Concentrated Energy Reserves

  • Role: Provide more than DOUBLE the energy of carbohydrates gram-for-gram! Stored under the skin as an insulating layer to keep us warm and protect vital organs.
  • Rich Sources: Cooking oils (mustard, sunflower, groundnut), ghee, butter, coconut, almonds, walnuts, egg yolk.

3. Proteins: The Body Builders

  • Role: Essential for growth of bones and muscles, repair of worn-out cells, healing cuts and wounds, and making enzymes and hair. Growing Class 6 children need plenty of protein!
  • Rich Sources: Pulses (dal, chana, rajma, moong), soybeans (richest plant protein), milk, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken, nuts.

4. Vitamins: The Disease Defenders

Required in small quantities, but essential to defend against infections and maintain healthy eyes, teeth, and skin:

Vitamin Crucial Biological Function Best Food Sources
Vitamin A Keeps eyes and skin healthy; ensures clear vision in dim light. Carrots, papaya, mango, milk, spinach, fish liver oil.
Vitamin B Complex Helps release energy from food, keeps nerves and muscles healthy. Whole grains, pulses, green leafy vegetables, milk, eggs.
Vitamin C Builds immune system to fight off coughs and colds; keeps gums healthy. (Easily destroyed by cooking heat!). Amla (richest source), lemon, orange, guava, tomatoes.
Vitamin D Helps our body absorb Calcium from food to build strong bones and teeth. Morning sunlight (skin synthesizes it!), milk, butter, eggs, fish.

5. Minerals: Micronutrient Regulators

  • Calcium (Ca): Builds strong bones and teeth, enables blood clotting (Milk, cheese, ragi, green leafy veg).
  • Iron (Fe): Forms haemoglobin in red blood cells to transport oxygen (Spinach, apples, jaggery/gud, liver).
  • Iodine (I): Needed by the thyroid gland in the neck to control growth and brain development (Iodised salt, seafood).
  • Phosphorus (P): Works with calcium to build rigid bone structures (Milk, bananas, whole grains, nuts).

6. Dietary Fibre (Roughage): The Internal Broom

  • What is it? Indigestible plant material (mainly cellulose) that passes through our digestive tract without being broken down.
  • Why do we need it? It does NOT provide any energy or nutrients, but it adds bulk (roughage) to food. This bulk allows intestinal muscles to grip and push waste smoothly, preventing constipation and stomach ache!
  • Rich Sources: Whole grains, pulses, unpeeled fruits (apples, pears, guava), raw salad vegetables (cucumber, carrots, radishes).

7. Water: The Universal Solvent

  • Makes up roughly 70% of human body weight.
  • Dissolves nutrients so they can be absorbed into the bloodstream.
  • Flushes out toxic metabolic wastes through urine and sweat.
  • Regulates body temperature through perspiration.

4. Deficiency Diseases & Clinical Disorders

If our food lacks a particular nutrient for weeks or months, normal biochemical processes break down, resulting in deficiency diseases.

Missing Nutrient Deficiency Disease Visible Symptoms to Spot Dietary Cure & Rich Foods
Vitamin A Night Blindness (Loss of vision in dim light) Poor vision in darkness, sometimes complete blindness; dry eyes. Carrots, papaya, mangoes, milk, butter, green leafy vegetables.
Vitamin B₁ Beriberi Very weak muscles, extreme fatigue, low energy to work. Unpolished rice, whole wheat, pulses, peanuts, green vegetables.
Vitamin C Scurvy Bleeding and spongy gums, loose teeth, wounds take very long to heal. Amla, lemon, oranges, guava, fresh ripe tomatoes.
Vitamin D Rickets Bones become abnormally soft, fragile, and bent (bow legs). Morning sunlight exposure, fortified milk, butter, egg yolk.
Calcium Bone & Tooth Decay Weak fragile bones, frequent fractures, crumbling tooth enamel. Milk, curd, paneer, ragi (millet), green leafy vegetables.
Iodine Goitre Swollen thyroid gland in neck; mental disability in growing children. Iodised salt, marine fish, seaweed.
Iron Anaemia Extreme pale skin, pale tongue, severe weakness, shortness of breath. Spinach, jaggery (gud), beetroot, apples, pomegranate, dal.
Protein (Severe) Kwashiorkor Stunted growth, swollen belly (oedema), thinned limbs, discoloured hair. Pulses, milk, eggs, soybeans, groundnuts.

5. Millets (Shree Anna): Ancient Superfoods

The textbook strongly promotes Millets (known in India as Shree Anna or Nutri-cereals).

  • Examples: Ragi (Finger millet), Jowar (Sorghum), Bajra (Pearl millet), and Foxtail millet.
  • Nutritional Powerhouse: Millets are exceptionally rich in Calcium, Iron, and Dietary Fibre compared to polished white rice and refined maida. Ragi has 30 times more calcium than rice!
  • Climate-Resilient: They grow in dry, drought-prone soils with very little water and without heavy chemical pesticides, making them eco-friendly treasures.

6. Food Miles: Farm to Plate & Zero Wastage

Key Definition: Food Miles
Food miles is the total physical distance that food travels from the farm where it is grown to the plate of the consumer who eats it.

Why Should We Reduce Food Miles?

  1. Cuts Air Pollution & Carbon Emissions: Transporting exotic kiwis or apples thousands of kilometres by cargo planes and diesel trucks consumes enormous fossil fuel.
  2. Ensures Freshness: Food eaten fresh from local farms retains vitamins and antioxidants without needing chemical waxes or artificial preservatives.
  3. Supports Local Farmers: Buying from regional haats and farmers puts money straight into local rural households.

NCERT Q5 (Page 57): Medu eats only biscuits, noodles, white bread; suffers stomach ache and constipation. What changes should he make?

Model Answer:

  1. The Cause of Medu's Problem: Biscuits, instant noodles, and white bread are made of refined flour (maida) and sugar. They contain almost ZERO dietary fibre (roughage). Without fibre, digested waste becomes hard and dry, sticking to intestinal walls and causing painful constipation and stomach ache.
  2. Required Diet Changes:
    • Add Fresh Raw Salads: Eat sliced cucumbers, carrots, and tomatoes before meals to supply roughage.
    • Switch to Whole Grains & Millets: Replace white bread with whole wheat chapatis or ragi/jowar rotis.
    • Include Whole Fruits: Eat unpeeled apples, guavas, and papayas instead of processed sugary biscuits.
    • Drink 6-8 Glasses of Water: Water softens the dietary fibre, ensuring smooth and painless bowel movements.

NCERT Q7 (Page 57): Given: (i) Canned fruit juice, (ii) Fresh fruit juice, (iii) Fresh fruit. Which one would you prefer and why?

Preference: I would definitely prefer (iii) Fresh Whole Fruit.

Scientific Reasons:

  1. Preserves Intact Dietary Fibre: Whole fruits contain natural pulp and skin loaded with roughage that cleans the intestine and slows sugar absorption. Squeezing juice removes this vital fibre.
  2. No Harmful Preservatives or Added Sugars: Canned juice contains artificial preservatives (sodium benzoate), synthetic food colours, and large amounts of added white sugar (corn syrup), which cause weight gain and cavities.
  3. Retains Heat-Sensitive Vitamins: Fresh fruits retain 100% of their delicate Vitamin C and natural antioxidants, which degrade rapidly when fruit juice is pasteurised or canned.
NCERT Q8 (Page 57): Gourav leg fracture: (i) Why calcium? (ii) Why Vitamin D on 2nd visit? (iii) Thoughtful question on doctor's choices?
  • (i) Why Calcium Tablets? Calcium is the chief mineral building block of bones. It provides the hardness and structural density required to knit fractured bone fragments back together.
  • (ii) Why Vitamin D Syrup on Second Visit? Calcium cannot be absorbed into the bloodstream on its own! Vitamin D is biologically essential to absorb calcium from the digestive intestines and deposit it into the repairing bone tissue. Without Vitamin D, all the calcium tablets would pass out unabsorbed!
  • (iii) Question on Doctor's Choices: "Could the doctor have recommended 20 minutes of daily morning sunlight exposure and a glass of milk alongside calcium from Day 1, so Gourav's skin could naturally synthesize Vitamin D and speed up bone healing from the very beginning?"
NCERT Q9 (Page 58): Sugar is a carbohydrate, but does not turn blue-black with iodine. Possible reason?

Scientific Reason:

  • Iodine specifically binds to Starch: Starch is a large, complex helical carbohydrate polymer (amylose and amylopectin). Iodine molecules slip inside this molecular coil, forming an intensely dark blue-black chemical complex.
  • Sugar is a Simple Carbohydrate: Table sugar (sucrose) or glucose consists of tiny, simple single or double sugar rings without helical coils. The iodine molecules cannot get trapped, so no colour change occurs and the solution remains yellow-brown.
NCERT Q10 (Page 58): "All starches are carbohydrates, but not all carbohydrates are starches." Design an activity to test this.

Evaluation: Raman's statement is 100% scientifically true! Carbohydrates include simple sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose) as well as complex starches.

Activity Design:

  1. Take two clean test tubes labeled A and B.
  2. In Test Tube A, add 5 mL of starch solution (boiled potato/rice water).
  3. In Test Tube B, add 5 mL of sugar solution (dissolved table sugar in water).
  4. Add 2 drops of dilute iodine solution to both tubes.
  5. Observation: Tube A turns deep blue-black (confirming starch). Tube B remains yellow-brown with no colour change!
  6. Conclusion: Both are carbohydrates, but only the complex polymer in Tube A is starch. Hence, not all carbohydrates are starches!
NCERT Q11 (Page 58): Iodine dropped on Mishti's socks (no change) vs teacher's saree (turned blue-black). Why?

Scientific Explanation:

  • Why the Saree Turned Blue-Black: Cotton sarees in India are traditionally stiffened during washing using starch (kalaf / rice starch water). When iodine droplets fell on the saree, the iodine reacted with the starch on the cotton fabric, instantly producing a blue-black stain.
  • Why the Socks Did Not Change: Mishti's school socks were made of synthetic fibers (nylon/polyester) or unstarched wool/cotton, which contain zero starch. Without starch, the iodine remained its natural reddish-yellow colour.
NCERT Q12 (Page 58): Why are millets healthy? Can eating just millets suffice for all nutritional requirements?
  • Why Millets are Healthy: Millets (ragi, bajra, jowar) are rich in minerals (calcium and iron), complex carbohydrates that release energy slowly, and high dietary fibre that keeps digestion healthy.
  • Can Eating Just Millets Suffice? No, absolutely not! While millets are superfoods, no single food can provide all required nutrients. Millets lack complete proteins, Vitamin C (found in citrus fruits), Vitamin A (found in carrots/papaya), and Vitamin B12. A healthy body requires a balanced combination of millets with pulses (dal), green leafy vegetables, fruits, and milk!
NCERT Q13 (Page 58): Given an unknown liquid sample. How would you check if it is an iodine solution?

Test Procedure:

  1. Take a known food item that is guaranteed to contain starch, such as a freshly cut slice of raw potato or a small spoonful of boiled rice.
  2. Using a dropper, place 2 drops of the unknown liquid onto the potato slice.
  3. Verification: If the contacted surface immediately turns a distinct, deep Blue-Black colour, the unknown liquid is confirmed to be an Iodine solution! If there is no colour change, it is not iodine.

Part C: 5 Short Answer Questions (2M & 3M)

Q1 (2 Marks): Why are fats called "concentrated energy foods"?

Answer:

  • Double Energy Yield: Gram for gram, fats supply more than twice the amount of chemical energy as carbohydrates (approx. 9 kcal/g for fats vs 4 kcal/g for carbs).
  • Storage Form: Surplus energy is converted into adipose fat tissue, serving as long-term energy reserves and physical thermal insulation.
Q2 (2 Marks): State two visible symptoms of "Anaemia" and name two iron-rich foods to cure it.

Answer:

  • Symptoms: Extreme bodily fatigue/weakness, pale skin, and pale inner eyelids/tongue due to reduced haemoglobin.
  • Iron-Rich Foods: Spinach (palak) and jaggery (gud) [also beetroot, apples, pomegranate].
Q3 (3 Marks): Describe the chemical test to detect the presence of proteins in boiled egg white.

Answer:

  1. Mash a small piece of boiled egg white into a smooth paste with 10 drops of water in a clean test tube.
  2. Add 2 drops of Copper Sulphate (CuSO₄) solution using a dropper.
  3. Add 10 drops of Caustic Soda (Sodium Hydroxide, NaOH) solution and shake the test tube thoroughly.
  4. Observation: The mixture turns a distinct violet / purple colour after a few minutes, confirming the presence of proteins.
Q4 (3 Marks): What is Goitre? What causes it and how can it be prevented in families?

Answer:

  • What is Goitre? A deficiency disease characterized by an abnormal swelling of the thyroid gland located in the front of the neck.
  • Cause: Severe deficiency of the mineral Iodine in the diet, which the thyroid requires to produce metabolic hormones.
  • Prevention: Use Iodised table salt in daily cooking and consume seafood or milk.
Q5 (3 Marks): State three distinct environmental and health benefits of reducing "Food Miles".

Answer:

  1. Cuts Transportation Pollution: Reduces fossil fuel burnt by freight trucks, reducing greenhouse gas emissions.
  2. Fresher & More Nutritious Food: Locally sourced produce doesn't require chemical preservatives or wax coatings.
  3. Strengthens Local Farm Economy: Keeps financial resources within local farming communities.

Part D: 3 Long Answer Questions (5 Marks Each)

Q1 (5 Marks): Define a "Balanced Diet". Explain why nutritional requirements vary between a 12-year-old active student, an elderly grandparent, and an athlete.

Model Answer (Structured 5-Mark Marking Breakdown):

1. Definition of Balanced Diet (1.5 Marks):
A balanced diet is one that provides all the essential nutrients (carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, and minerals) in the correct, healthy proportions suited to an individual's body, along with adequate amounts of dietary fibre (roughage) and clean water.

2. Variation in Nutritional Requirements (3.5 Marks):

  • Active 12-Year-Old Student: Needs high amounts of Proteins for muscle and bone growth, plenty of Calcium & Vitamin D for lengthening skeleton, and moderate carbohydrates for study and active sports.
  • Elderly Grandparent: Basal metabolic rate slows down; requires fewer carbohydrates and very low saturated fats. Needs easily digestible, soft foods rich in Calcium & Vitamin D to prevent osteoporosis and high Dietary Fibre to prevent sluggish bowel movements.
  • Strenuous Athlete / Manual Labourer: Burns thousands of calories daily. Requires a significantly higher proportion of Carbohydrates for sustained muscular stamina, high Proteins for rapid muscle repair, and abundant Water & Electrolytes to replace sweat loss.
Q2 (5 Marks): Describe the 3 Chemical Food Tests (Starch, Fat, Protein) in detail. State the sample, reagent, method, and conclusive observation for each.

Model Answer:

Component Food Sample Used Chemical Reagents Experimental Procedure Conclusive Color / Result

Q3 (5 Marks): Why are Millets designated as "Nutri-Cereals" (Shree Anna)? Detail their nutritional superiority over polished grains and their role in sustainable agriculture.

Model Answer:

1. Introduction (1 Mark):
Millets (such as Ragi, Jowar, and Bajra) are ancient small-seeded grasses cultivated in India for over 5,000 years. The Government of India and the United Nations celebrate them as Nutri-cereals.

2. Nutritional Superiority over Polished Rice and Maida (2 Marks):

  • Exceptional Mineral Content: Ragi (finger millet) has over 340 mg of Calcium per 100g, building dense bones in children and preventing osteoporosis in women.
  • High Dietary Fibre: Millets have a low glycaemic index, releasing glucose slowly into the bloodstream, which prevents diabetes and cures chronic constipation.
  • Rich in Iron & Micro-minerals: Bajra is rich in iron and zinc, combating anaemia in rural adolescent girls and children.

3. Ecological & Agricultural Benefits (2 Marks):

  • Drought-Resistant: Millets need only 25% of the water required by thirsty paddy crops, surviving in arid desert and tribal terrains.
  • Minimal Chemical Fertilizers: They naturally resist pests, reducing synthetic chemical contamination of soil and groundwater.

Case Study 1: The Fast-Food Dilemma

"Medu studies in Class 6. During lunch breaks, he avoids eating the home-cooked vegetable paratha and sprouted salad packed by his mother. Instead, he spends his pocket money buying salted potato wafers, cream biscuits, and instant noodles from the school canteen. Over the past four months, Medu has gained 5 kg of excess body weight, feels lethargic during sports, and frequently complains of severe abdominal cramps and chronic constipation. His doctor noticed pale eyes and recommended a complete overhaul of his diet."

Sub-Questions & Detailed Answers:

  1. Identify two major dietary deficiencies in Medu's canteen food.
    Answer: Medu's food is deficient in Dietary Fibre (Roughage), Proteins, and Essential Vitamins/Minerals.
  2. What scientific reason explains Medu's chronic constipation?
    Answer: Refined flour (maida) and processed biscuits contain zero roughage to form digestive bulk, resulting in hard, sluggish stools that cannot move easily through the large intestine.
  3. Suggest two healthy, tasty substitutes Medu's mother can pack in his tiffin.
    Answer: Ragi/sprouted moong vegetable cutlets and roasted chana with a whole sweet apple or orange.

Case Study 2: The Mystery of the Saree Stain

"In the school chemistry laboratory, Mishti and her science teacher Mrs. Sharma were preparing dilute iodine solution for a plant experiment. While filling a dropper bottle, two drops accidentally splashed onto Mishti's synthetic nylon socks, and two drops landed on Mrs. Sharma's handloom cotton saree. To Mishti's surprise, the spots on Mrs. Sharma's saree immediately turned a dark navy blue-black colour, whereas the drops on her socks remained dull yellowish-brown."

Sub-Questions & Detailed Answers:

  1. Why did the iodine drops on the cotton saree turn blue-black?
    Answer: The cotton saree was treated with laundry starch (rice/maize starch kalaf) for stiffness. Starch reacts with iodine to produce a deep blue-black complex.
  2. Why was there no colour change on Mishti's socks?
    Answer: Mishti's socks were made of synthetic nylon fibers containing zero starch.
  3. If Mishti applies the same iodine to a piece of boiled potato, what will happen?
    Answer: The potato will turn intensely blue-black because potatoes store rich reserves of plant starch.

Exam Hall Traps & 15 High-Yield Revision Points

Common mistakes made by Class 6 students in exams, how to avoid them, and a crisp 15-point memory checklist.

5 Common Student Mistakes to Avoid

Common Student Mistake Why Students Make This Mistake Correct Scientific Understanding to Write
Mistake 1: Writing that table sugar turns blue-black with iodine. Assuming all carbohydrates give the blue-black iodine reaction. Only STARCH turns blue-black with iodine. Simple sugars (glucose, sucrose) do NOT give a blue-black colour!
Mistake 2: Believing Roughage provides energy. Thinking everything we eat gets digested for energy. Roughage (cellulose) is indigestible. It provides ZERO calories, but is essential for bowel motility and preventing constipation.
Mistake 3: Confusing Scurvy with Rickets. Mixing up Vitamin C and Vitamin D symptoms. Vitamin C → Scurvy (bleeding gums). Vitamin D → Rickets (soft, bent bones / bow legs).
Mistake 4: Assuming water patch on paper is fat. A wet paper spot looks dark initially. Water evaporates, leaving the paper dry and opaque. A fat patch remains permanently greasy and translucent!
Mistake 5: Thinking boiling preserves Vitamin C. Assuming cooking retains all vitamins. Vitamin C is extremely heat-sensitive and is easily destroyed by cooking heat. Always eat citrus fruits raw!
