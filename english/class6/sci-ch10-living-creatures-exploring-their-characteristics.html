10.1 What Sets the Living Apart from the Non-Living?

Is a cloud that grows in the sky living? Is a car that moves along the road living? Science establishes that an entity is classified as living only if it exhibits ALL of the following fundamental life processes simultaneously:

  1. Nutrition (Need for Food & Water):
    • Every organism requires nutrients and energy to fuel cellular activities, maintain tissues, and drive growth.
    • Green plants are autotrophs that synthesize their own food through photosynthesis using sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water.
    • Animals are heterotrophs that depend directly or indirectly on plants for nutrition.
  2. Respiration:
    • Respiration is the biochemical breakdown of food inside living cells to release usable energy.
    • Breathing is the physical gas exchange: taking in oxygen (O₂) and releasing carbon dioxide (CO₂). Living beings respire continuously day and night — stopping respiration means biological death.
  3. Growth (Irreversible Internal Increase):
    • Living growth occurs from the inside out through cellular division and cellular expansion (e.g. a seed grows into a giant banyan tree; an infant grows into an adult).
    • Non-living things can only "grow" externally by accumulation of matter on the outside (e.g. a rolling snowball or a shifting sand dune).
  4. Response to Stimuli (Sensitivity):
    • A stimulus is any change in the external or internal environment (light, touch, temperature, sound, smell).
    • When you accidentally touch a hot cooking pan, you instantly pull your hand back (stimulus = heat, response = withdrawal).
    • The Mimosa pudica (Touch-me-not / Chhui-mui) plant folds its leaflets inward within seconds when touched!
  5. Excretion:
    • Metabolic chemical reactions produce toxic waste by-products (urea, excess salts, carbon dioxide).
    • Excretion is the biological process of removing these toxic wastes from the body. Animals excrete via urine, sweat, and exhaled breath; plants excrete through leaf fall, gums, and resins.
  6. Reproduction:
    • The biological process by which organisms produce new individuals of their own kind, ensuring the continuation of the species across generations.
  7. Definite Life Span and Death:
    • Every living being is born, undergoes growth, reproduces, ages, and ultimately dies.

⚗️ Living vs Dead vs Non-Living: The Critical Scientific Distinction

  • Living: Actively shows life processes (e.g. dog, mango tree, germinating bean).
  • Dead: Material that was once part of a living organism, but its cells have permanently stopped respiring and functioning (e.g. a fallen dried leaf, a wooden dining table, leather shoes, a dry bone).
  • Non-Living: Matter that was NEVER alive and has no cellular biological origin (e.g. stone pebble, pure water, iron nail, plastic toy).

10.2 Essential Conditions for Seed Germination

Inside every dry bean or gram seed lies a dormant baby plant called an embryo. What awakens this sleeping embryo into a growing seedling?

Setup: Moong or gram seeds are tested under four distinct environmental conditions:

Batch 1: Complete Dry Kept in dry dish with air and warmth, zero water. NO Germination. Enzymes inside seed embryo remain completely dormant without water.

10.3 Growth and Directional Movement in Plants (Tropisms)

Even though plants are rooted in one spot and cannot walk, they exhibit precise directional growth movements in response to environmental cues:

  1. Radicle vs Plumule:
    • When a seed germinates, the first structure that emerges downwards is the radicle, which develops into the primary root system.
    • Next, the plumule emerges upwards, developing into the stem, branches, and leaves (the shoot system).
  2. Geotropism (Response to Gravity):
    • Roots show Positive Geotropism: Roots always grow downwards towards the center of Earth in the direction of gravity, anchoring the plant and seeking groundwater and minerals.
    • Shoots show Negative Geotropism: Shoots always grow upwards away from gravity.
    • Pot on its side experiment (Fig 10.9): If a potted seedling is tipped on its side horizontally, within one week the shoot bends upwards towards the sky, while the root bends downwards into the soil!
  3. Phototropism (Response to Light):
    • Plant shoots always bend and grow towards the source of sunlight (Positive Phototropism).
    • Plant hormones (auxins) accumulate on the shaded side of the stem, causing those cells to elongate faster, which forces the stem to curve directly towards the light!

10.4 Metamorphosis and Animal Life Cycles

Many animals do not simply grow larger versions of their baby bodies. Instead, they hatch as an immature larva that undergoes a radical, dramatic bodily transformation called metamorphosis to become an adult.

1. Life Cycle of the Frog (Amphibian Metamorphosis)

  • Stage 1: Eggs (Spawn): The female frog lays thousands of jelly-covered eggs in tranquil fresh pond water.
  • Stage 2: Tadpole (Larva):
    • Hatches from the egg looking like a small fish!
    • Has external gills for breathing underwater, a long propelling tail with swimming fins, and NO legs.
    • Eats algae and aquatic vegetation (herbivore).
  • Stage 3: Metamorphosis:
    • Hind legs emerge first, followed by front legs.
    • Lungs develop inside for breathing air; gills disappear.
    • The long tail is gradually digested and reabsorbed by the body (autophagy).
  • Stage 4: Adult Frog:
    • A land-and-water amphibian with four strong legs for hopping and swimming, no tail, and breathes through lungs on land and moist skin underwater!

2. Life Cycle of the Mosquito (Insect Complete Metamorphosis)

  • Stage 1: Eggs: Female mosquitoes lay rafts of dark eggs on the surface of stagnant water (coolers, puddles, open drains).
  • Stage 2: Larva (Wriggler):
    • Hatches in 24–48 hours. Swims with a wriggling motion.
    • Hangs upside down from the water surface, breathing air through a tail siphon tube. Feeds on microscopic organic matter.
  • Stage 3: Pupa (Tumbler):
    • Comma-shaped non-feeding stage. Tumbles actively when disturbed. Breathes via two trumpet tubes at the surface. Inside, the body is completely reconstructed!
  • Stage 4: Adult Mosquito:
    • Pupa case splits; the winged adult emerges, rests on the surface to dry its wings, and flies away.
    • Epidemic Prevention: Emptying stagnant water from flowerpots, air coolers, and puddles breaks the mosquito life cycle at the larval stage, eradicating dengue, malaria, and chikungunya!

Exercise 1 List the similarities and differences in the life cycles of plants and animals

Similarities:

  1. Both begin life from a single fertilized unit (a fertilized egg/zygote in animals; an ovule inside a seed in plants).
  2. Both undergo cellular division, growth, and structural differentiation.
  3. Both require nutrition, respiration, and water to sustain life.
  4. Both reach reproductive maturity, produce offspring to propagate the species, age, and eventually die.

Differences:

Growth Pattern Indeterminate growth throughout life at root and shoot meristem tips. Determinate growth; stops when adult size is reached.
Metamorphosis No metamorphosis (seedling directly resembles adult tree). Many species undergo radical metamorphosis (tadpole → frog; wriggler → mosquito).
Mobility Fixed in soil; movements are directional growth tropisms. Free locomotion to search for food, mates, and shelter.

Exercise 3 How to use seed germination knowledge for proper storage of grains and pulses?

We know that seed germination strictly requires three conditions: Moisture (water) + Air (oxygen) + Warmth. To prevent seeds from sprouting, rotting, or being destroyed by pests during long-term storage, we deprive them of these conditions:

  1. Thorough Sun-Drying: Before storage, harvest grains are spread under the hot sun for several days to reduce moisture content below 10%. Without moisture, seeds cannot germinate and fungal mould cannot grow.
  2. Airtight Storage Containers: Storing dry pulses in sealed metal bins or hermetic bags prevents damp air and moisture ingress.
  3. Cool and Dry Storage Rooms: Warehouses are kept cool and well-ventilated to avoid high warmth that triggers premature sprouting.
Exercise 4 Advantage of a tail in the tadpole stage and why it disappears in adult frogs

Advantage in Tadpole Stage:

  • The tadpole lives exclusively underwater like a fish and has no legs. Its muscular, finned tail provides powerful swimming propulsion to navigate water currents, forage for algae, and escape aquatic predators (fish and water beetles).

Why it Disappears in Adult Frogs:

  • The adult frog transitions to a semi-terrestrial lifestyle. It develops powerful, muscular hind legs adapted for jumping on land and swimming with webbed feet.
  • A long heavy tail would be an impediment on land, interfering with jumping and hopping. During metamorphosis, the tail tissue is digested and reabsorbed internally (autophagy), supplying nutrients for developing adult organs!
Exercise 5 Wooden log debate: Charan (non-living) vs Charu (living) — who is right?

Evaluation of Both Statements:

  • Charan's argument: "Non-living because it cannot move."
    Flawed logic: Lack of locomotion does not prove non-living status — living banyan trees and rose bushes also cannot move from place to place! However, his conclusion that the log is currently non-living is correct.
  • Charu's argument: "Living because it is made of wood obtained from trees."
    Flawed logic: Just because something came from a living organism does not mean it is currently alive. A bone from a dead dinosaur or leather from a cow is not alive.

Scientific Verdict:

A cut wooden log is dead organic matter. While it was once part of a living tree with active cellular metabolism, the cut log no longer respires, cannot grow, cannot reproduce, cannot respond to stimuli, and cannot repair damaged cells. Therefore, it is scientifically classified as non-living (dead).

Exercise 6 Similarities and distinguishing features in the life cycles of a mosquito and a frog

Similarities:

  • Both lay their eggs in stagnant fresh water.
  • Both have an aquatic larval stage (tadpole in frog; wriggler in mosquito) that lives in water and looks totally different from the adult.
  • Both undergo dramatic metamorphosis to develop into adults.

Distinguishing Features:

Feature Frog Life Cycle Mosquito Life Cycle
Biological Class Amphibian (Vertebrate with backbone). Insect (Invertebrate with exoskeleton).
Number of Stages 3 main stages: Egg → Tadpole → Adult Frog. 4 distinct stages: Egg → Larva → Pupa → Adult Mosquito.
Pupal Stage Absent (direct metamorphosis from tadpole to frog). Present (active non-feeding pupa called a tumbler).

Exercise 7 Potted plant kept horizontally on the ground (Fig 10.9) — predict shoot and root growth after 1 week

Expected Observation After One Week:

  • The Shoot: Bends sharply and curves upwards away from the ground towards the sky.
  • The Root: Bends sharply and grows downwards into the soil towards the center of Earth.

Scientific Reasons:

  1. Negative Geotropism of the Shoot: Plant stems perceive gravity and respond by growing in the opposite direction (upwards). This ensures leaves reach upward into sunlight for photosynthesis.
  2. Positive Geotropism of the Root: Roots perceive gravity through specialized gravity-sensing cells (statocytes) in their root caps and grow downwards into the earth to secure mechanical anchorage and locate underground water.

Exercise 9 Design an experiment to check if temperature has an effect on seed germination

Aim: To investigate how temperature affects the germination of gram (chana) seeds.

Materials Required: 30 healthy soaked gram seeds, 3 petri dishes, cotton wool, water, refrigerator, room, and a warm incubator.

Step-by-Step Procedure:

  1. Soak 30 gram seeds in water overnight.
  2. Line three petri dishes (A, B, C) with moist cotton wool. Place 10 soaked seeds in each dish.
  3. Dish A (Cold Condition): Place inside a refrigerator at 4 °C.
  4. Dish B (Moderate Warmth): Keep in a normal room at 25 °C with indirect light.
  5. Dish C (Excessive Heat): Place in a warm incubator or near a heat radiator at 50 °C.
  6. Keep the cotton wool moist in all three dishes by adding a few drops of water daily.
  7. Record observations daily for 4 days.

Observations:

  • Dish B (25 °C): Over 90% of seeds germinate with healthy white radicles and green plumules.
  • Dish A (4 °C): No germination; seeds remain swollen but dormant.
  • Dish C (50 °C): No germination; seeds soften, decompose, and develop foul mould.

Scientific Conclusion: Temperature has a profound effect on seed germination. Seeds require an optimal moderate temperature (20 °C to 30 °C) for metabolic enzymes to become active. Extreme cold suppresses enzyme activity, while extreme heat destroys seed proteins.

Learning Further 1 & 2 Growing plants without germinating seeds (Vegetative Propagation)

Many plants reproduce asexually through vegetative propagation without needing seeds:

  • Stem Cuttings: Rose, sugarcane, and money plant grow new roots when a stem cutting with a node is planted in moist soil or water.
  • Underground Tubers: Potatoes grow new sprouts from their "eyes" (buds).
  • Rhizomes: Ginger and turmeric propagate from fleshy underground rhizomes.
  • Leaves: Bryophyllum produces tiny plantlets with roots along the margins of its leaves that drop to the soil and grow into independent plants!

SA 3 Differentiate between the wriggler and tumbler stages of a mosquito. (2 Marks)
  • Wriggler (Larva): An active, feeding worm-like stage that moves with a wriggling motion and breathes through a posterior breathing siphon at the surface.
  • Tumbler (Pupa): A comma-shaped, non-feeding stage that tumbles when disturbed. It undergoes internal metamorphosis into an adult winged mosquito while breathing via two head trumpets.
SA 4 Why do roots always grow downwards even if a seed is planted upside down? (2 Marks)

The emerging radicle (young root) has gravity-sensing starch granules (statoliths) in its root cap cells that perceive Earth's gravitational pull. It responds through positive geotropism, triggering growth hormones that cause the root to bend and burrow downwards into the soil regardless of seed orientation.

SA 5 State two reasons why excretion is essential for living organisms. (2 Marks)
  1. Prevents Toxic Poisoning: Biochemical reactions produce harmful waste substances (urea, uric acid, ammonia) that are toxic if accumulated in the bloodstream.
  2. Maintains Water and Salt Balance: Excretion regulates proper water levels, pH, and electrolyte balance inside the body.

Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)

LA 1 Explain the complete life cycle of a frog with the help of a neat stage-by-stage description. (5 Marks)

The life cycle of a frog represents classic amphibian metamorphosis comprising four key phases:

  1. Egg Mass (Spawn): Female frogs deposit clusters of thousands of eggs encased in protective jelly in ponds. Fertilization occurs externally in water.
  2. Tadpole (Aquatic Larva):
    • After 1 to 2 weeks, fish-like tadpoles hatch.
    • They possess external gills to extract dissolved oxygen from water, a long swimming tail with a dorsal fin, and a round mouth to graze on algae. They have zero limbs.
  3. Tadpole with Legs (Metamorphosis Begins):
    • At 6 to 9 weeks, tiny hind legs appear, followed by front limbs.
    • Internal lungs develop to enable breathing atmospheric air, while external gills wither away. The eyes bulge, and the mouth widens.
  4. Froglet & Adult Frog:
    • The long tail is gradually reabsorbed by the body, recycling its proteins.
    • The adult frog emerges onto land. It has strong leaping hind legs, webbed feet, no tail, and breathes using lungs on land and moist skin in water. Adult frogs return to water only to breed!
LA 2 Describe the four stages in the life cycle of a mosquito and explain how community hygiene breaks this cycle. (5 Marks)

The Four Stages of Mosquito Life Cycle (Complete Metamorphosis):

  1. Egg: Female mosquitoes lay 100–300 tiny dark eggs in rafts on the surface of standing stagnant water.
  2. Larva (Wriggler): Hatches within 24–48 hours. Swims with vigorous wriggling movements, feeding on aquatic microbes. Breathes atmospheric air by hanging upside down from a breathing siphon tube at the water surface.
  3. Pupa (Tumbler): A comma-shaped non-feeding transitional pupa that floats near the surface and breathes via two respiratory trumpets. Tumbles downwards when shadowed. Inside, larval organs dissolve and reconstruct into adult mosquito structures.
  4. Adult Mosquito: The pupal skin splits on the water surface; the winged adult emerges, pumps air into its wings to harden them, and flies away. Females require blood meals for egg development, transmitting malaria and dengue.

How Community Hygiene Breaks the Cycle:

  • Because eggs, larvae, and pupae are 100% aquatic, mosquitoes cannot complete their life cycle without standing water!
  • Emptying water from air coolers, discarded tyres, and flowerpot trays once a week kills larvae before they can pupate.
  • Pouring a few drops of kerosene or eco-friendly larvicide on drains creates a thin surface film that blocks the larval siphon tubes, suffocating the wrigglers.
LA 3 "Movement is not a definitive criterion for life." Elaborate this statement using examples from both plant and animal kingdoms. (5 Marks)

Why Movement Across Space (Locomotion) Fails as a Sole Criterion for Life:

  1. Living Organisms That Do NOT Show Locomotion:
    • Plants are rooted permanently in one place. A banyan tree or rose bush never moves from its position, yet it is fully alive — it photosynthesizes, respires, grows, transports sap, responds to light, and reproduces!
    • Many sedentary aquatic animals like adult sea sponges, corals, and sea anemones remain anchored permanently to rocks on the ocean floor throughout their adult lives.
  2. Non-Living Objects That DO Show Movement:
    • Motor cars, aeroplanes, and trains travel hundreds of kilometers across the land and sky.
    • Clouds drift across the horizon, and river water flows continuously down mountains.
    • Yet cars and clouds are non-living because their movement is caused by external mechanical combustion or wind, and they possess zero cellular biology, cannot reproduce, and cannot respire!
  3. Subtle Internal Living Movements:
    • While plants don't walk, they exhibit internal cellular streaming (cyclosis), stomatal opening/closing, and directional growth movements (tropisms towards light and gravity). Thus, life is defined by cellular metabolism and biological functions, not mere physical displacement!

🚨 Section 6: Exam Hall Mistakes & 15 Rapid Revision Points

❌ 5 Common Exam Hall Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Believing seeds need soil and sunlight to germinate: Seeds do NOT need soil or sunlight to germinate! They only need Water, Air (Oxygen), and Warmth. Soil and sunlight are needed later by the green seedling.
  • Mistake 2: Stating that plants do not respire: Plants respire 24 hours a day, taking in O₂ and releasing CO₂. Photosynthesis happens only during daytime sunlight.
  • Mistake 3: Calling a car or cloud "living" because it moves: Non-living things move due to external forces; living things move and grow via internal cellular metabolism.
  • Mistake 4: Claiming the radicle becomes the stem: Radicle becomes the ROOT; Plumule becomes the SHOOT.
  • Mistake 5: Assuming tadpoles breathe with lungs: Young tadpoles have gills, not lungs. Lungs develop only during metamorphosis.
