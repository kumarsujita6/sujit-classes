9.1 Why Do We Need to Separate Substances?

In nature and everyday life, substances rarely occur in a pure state. We encounter mixtures constantly. We separate components of a mixture for two primary objectives:

  1. To remove undesirable or harmful impurities: Removing tiny stone pebbles and insect-damaged grains from wheat before milling, or filtering muddy river water to make it safe for drinking.
  2. To separate two different but highly useful components: Churning fermented curd to produce butter and buttermilk (both are valuable food items!), or refining crude petroleum oil into petrol, diesel, and paraffin wax.

9.2 Methods for Separating Solid-Solid Mixtures

1. Handpicking

  • Principle: Exploits visible differences in size, colour, and shape of solid particles.
  • Conditions for Use:
    • The impurities must be visually distinguishable from the useful grain.
    • The total quantity of impurities must be relatively small.
  • Everyday Examples: Handpicking green chillies from a bowl of poha or vegetable pulao; picking black pebbles from a plate of raw rice or pulses before washing.

2. Threshing

  • Principle: After harvesting a wheat or paddy crop, hundreds of grain seeds are attached firmly to dry stalks. Handpicking individual grains from thousands of stalks would take years!
  • Technique: Stalks are gathered into bundles and beaten firmly against a hard rock, wooden log, or concrete threshing floor. The mechanical impact causes the dry, ripe grains to detach from the stalks.
  • Modern Mechanization: Farmers today use tractor-driven machines called threshers or giant combine harvesters that cut, thresh, and clean grains in a single automated pass.

3. Winnowing

🌾 Kabir's Dohā on Winnowing (The Soop)

“Sadhu aisa chahiye jaisa soop subhay | Saar saar ko gahi rahai thotha dayi uday ||”
— Saint Kabir observed that just as a traditional bamboo winnowing tray (soop) blows away the hollow, worthless husk while retaining the nourishing heavy grain, a truly wise person absorbs good virtues while discarding flaws.

  • Principle: Separates components based on the difference in weight (mass/density) using wind or blowing air.
  • Technique: The mixture of heavy grains and lightweight dry husk is placed in a flat bamboo tray (soop). The farmer stands on an elevated platform and tilts the tray, gently shaking it at shoulder height in the direction of the blowing wind.
    • The lighter husk particles are carried away by the wind and form a separate heap at a distance.
    • The heavier grain seeds fall almost vertically downwards under gravity to form a clean grain heap right next to the farmer's feet!

4. Sieving

  • Principle: Separates components of a solid-solid mixture based on the difference in particle size using a mesh screen called a sieve.
  • How it works: Particles smaller than the sieve holes pass freely through the mesh, while larger coarse particles are retained on top of the sieve.
  • Real-Life Examples:
    • Kitchen: Sieving wheat flour to remove coarse wheat bran and husk fragments before kneading dough.
    • Construction Sites: Workers pour sand onto large, inclined wire-mesh sieves to separate fine plastering sand from coarse gravel and round stones.

5. Magnetic Separation

  • Principle: Exploits the magnetic properties of certain substances (ferromagnetic materials like iron, cobalt, and nickel).
  • Application: When an iron key or nails fall accidentally into a pile of sawdust, handpicking takes forever. Moving a bar magnet through the sawdust causes all iron nails to leap onto the magnet instantly, leaving non-magnetic sawdust behind.
  • Industrial Scrap Yards: Massive electromagnets attached to cranes lift tonnes of discarded iron and steel from mounds of municipal junk!

9.3 Methods for Separating Insoluble Solids from Liquids

1. Sedimentation and Decantation

  • Sedimentation: When a mixture of an insoluble heavy solid and a liquid (such as soil/sand in water) is left undisturbed, the denser, heavier solid particles settle to the bottom of the container under gravity. This settling down is called sedimentation, and the settled layer is called the sediment.
  • Decantation: Once the solid has fully settled, the clean liquid above (called the supernatant) is poured out gently into another container by carefully tilting the vessel without disturbing the settled sediment. This process is called decantation.
  • Decanting Immiscible Liquids: Decantation is also used to separate two immiscible liquids of different densities — for example, oil floating on water. The lighter oil layer can be poured off the top!

2. Filtration

  • Principle: Passing a mixture through a porous medium whose pores are smaller than the solid particles. The solid particles are trapped as residue on the filter, while the clean liquid passes through as filtrate.
  • Filter Paper Cone Folding: A circular filter paper is folded in half, then folded again into quarters, and opened into a hollow cone. The cone is fitted into a glass funnel.
  • Biological Filters:
    • Nasal Hairs: Hairs inside human nostrils act as a natural mechanical air filter, trapping dust, pollen, and microbes.
    • Face Masks (N95): Microscopic fiber meshes that filter airborne pathogenic respiratory droplets.
    • Tea Strainer: Wire mesh separating tea leaves from brewed tea.

9.4 Evaporation and Churning

  • Evaporation for Soluble Solids: Common salt is completely dissolved in seawater. It cannot be separated by filtration (salt molecules pass straight through filter pores!). Instead, seawater is collected in shallow pits (salt pans) exposed to sunlight and wind. Water evaporates into the atmosphere, leaving dry crude sea salt crystals behind.
  • Churning (Centrifugation): Used to separate butter from curd or milk. When curd is churned rapidly using a wooden mathni or mechanical blender, the lighter fat globules (butter) clump together and float to the surface, while the denser liquid buttermilk (chhach) remains at the bottom.

Exercise 1 What purpose does handpicking serve in the process of separation?

Options: (i) Filtration   (ii) Sorting   (iii) Evaporation   (iv) Decantation

Correct Answer: (ii) Sorting

Explanation: Handpicking is a manual sorting method used to pick out and separate undesirable or different particles based on observable physical characteristics like size, color, or shape.

Exercise 2 Which of the following substances are commonly separated using the churning method?

Options: (i) Oil from water   (ii) Sand from water   (iii) Cream from milk   (iv) Oxygen from air

Correct Answer: (iii) Cream from milk (or butter from curd)

Explanation: Churning involves rapid spinning that causes the less dense butterfat globules to coalesce and float to the surface, leaving liquid buttermilk behind.

Exercise 3 Which factor is usually essential for filtration?

Options: (i) Apparatus size   (ii) Presence of air   (iii) Pore size   (iv) Temperature of the mixture

Correct Answer: (iii) Pore size

Explanation: Filtration relies strictly on the size of the filter's pores — the pores must be smaller than the insoluble solid particles to trap them as residue while allowing liquid filtrate to flow through.

Exercise 4 True or False with reasons & corrections

(i) Salt can be separated from salt solution by keeping it under the Sun.
Answer: TRUE [T]
Reason: Solar thermal energy evaporates the water into the atmosphere, leaving dry common salt crystals behind. This is the exact method used in coastal salt pans across India.

(ii) Handpicking should be used only when the quantity of one component is less.
Answer: TRUE [T]
Reason: If the quantity of impurities is enormous (e.g. millions of husks in wheat), handpicking becomes practically impossible and winnowing/sieving must be used instead.

(iii) A mixture of puffed rice and rice grains can be separated by threshing.
Answer: FALSE [F]
Correction: Threshing is beating stalks to detach grains. Puffed rice and rice grains are already detached; they are separated by winnowing because puffed rice is much lighter and is blown away by wind while heavy rice grains drop straight down.

(iv) A mixture of mustard oil and lemon water can be separated by decantation.
Answer: TRUE [T]
Reason: Mustard oil is immiscible with lemon water and is less dense, forming a distinct separate floating layer on top that can be carefully poured off (decanted).

(v) Sieving is used to separate a mixture of rice flour and water.
Answer: FALSE [F]
Correction: Sieving is used only for solid-solid mixtures of different particle sizes. A mixture of rice flour and water is a liquid suspension separated by filtration or sedimentation and decantation.

Exercise 6 In what situations would you use decantation instead of filtration to separate solids from liquids?

Decantation is preferred over filtration in the following situations:

  1. When solid particles are heavy, coarse, and settle rapidly: For example, washing rice grains or pulses with water before cooking. The heavy grains sink to the bottom immediately, allowing the dirty wash water to be poured off effortlessly without needing filter paper.
  2. When immediate separation is needed without laboratory equipment: Decantation requires only a single vessel and zero specialized consumables (filter paper, funnels).
  3. When separating coarse sand/gravel from muddy construction slurry: Filtration through paper would instantly clog and tear the paper under the weight of coarse stones.
Exercise 7 Can you relate the presence of nasal hair to any separation process?

Answer: Yes, nasal hair acts as a biological Filtration system!

Explanation:

  • The dense lining of fine hairs inside our nasal passages forms a porous mesh filter.
  • When we inhale air, the pore spaces between the hairs allow tiny gaseous oxygen and nitrogen molecules to pass into the lungs freely.
  • However, larger suspended solid particles — dust, pollen grains, soot, insect fragments, and airborne dirt — are trapped on the hairs and sticky mucus membrane (residue), protecting our delicate lungs from infection and damage.
Exercise 8 During COVID-19, all of us wore masks. What material are they made of and what is their role?

Materials Used: Surgical and N95 masks are constructed from multi-layered non-woven melt-blown polypropylene polymer fabric, while washable masks use dense multi-layered woven cotton.

Role & Mechanism of Separation:

  • Masks function as high-efficiency micro-filtration devices.
  • The ultrafine fiber mesh has microscopic pore spaces that allow breathable gases (oxygen and carbon dioxide) to flow freely while physically intercepting and blocking airborne respiratory droplets, spit droplets, and aerosol particles carrying the coronavirus.
  • This prevents an infected wearer from dispersing virus droplets into the air and shields healthy people from inhaling them.
Exercise 9 Procedure to separate a mixture of potatoes, salt, and sawdust

Step-by-Step Separation Scheme:

  1. Step 1: Handpicking: The potatoes are much larger and solid. Simply handpick all the whole potatoes out of the dry mixture. (Component 1 separated: Potatoes).
  2. Step 2: Dissolution in Water: Add 200 mL of water to the remaining mixture of salt and sawdust and stir thoroughly with a spoon. Salt dissolves completely in water to form a clear salt solution, while sawdust is insoluble and floats/suspends in water.
  3. Step 3: Filtration: Pour the liquid mixture through a funnel fitted with filter paper. The insoluble sawdust is trapped on the filter paper as residue. Wash it with a little clean water and let it dry in the sun. (Component 2 separated: Sawdust).
  4. Step 4: Evaporation: Take the clear salt-water filtrate in a china dish and heat it gently over a burner (or leave it in an open shallow dish under the hot sun). The water evaporates completely as steam, leaving dry crystalline salt behind. (Component 3 separated: Salt).

Short Answer Questions (2 & 3 Marks)

SA 1 What is threshing? How is it performed traditionally and modernly? (3 Marks)

Definition: Threshing is the process of beating harvested agricultural stalks to detach and separate the grain seeds from the dry straw.

  • Traditional Method: Farmers tie dried stalks into bundles and strike them repeatedly against a hard rock, heavy wooden log, or bullocks are made to trample over the spread stalks.
  • Modern Method: Farmers use motorized threshers or combine harvesters powered by tractors or diesel engines, which process tonnes of grain in hours.
SA 2 Explain the principle of winnowing with a neat explanation. (2 Marks)

Principle: Winnowing is based on the difference in weights (mass/density) of components in a solid-solid mixture using moving air.

When the mixture of grain and husk is dropped from a height in a breeze, the lighter husk is blown away by the wind, forming a heap at a distance, while the heavier grains fall straight down under gravity, forming a separate heap.

SA 3 Differentiate between sedimentation and decantation. (2 Marks)
Sedimentation Decantation
The process where heavier, insoluble particles settle to the bottom of a liquid under gravity. The process of carefully pouring off the clear liquid (supernatant) without disturbing the settled sediment.
Occurs passively when the mixture is left undisturbed. An active manual pouring step performed after sedimentation is complete.
SA 4 Why does a carpenter use a magnet to find iron nails dropped in sawdust? (2 Marks)

Iron is a ferromagnetic material, whereas sawdust is organic, non-magnetic wood. Sweeping a magnet through the sawdust causes the magnetic iron nails to leap onto the magnet instantly, saving time and preventing finger injuries from sharp nails.

SA 5 How is cottage cheese (paneer) separated from liquid whey at home? (2 Marks)

When lemon juice or vinegar is added to boiling milk, milk curdles into solid white casein protein curdles and liquid whey. The mixture is poured through a clean fine muslin cloth (filtration). The solid paneer curdles are trapped on the cloth as residue, while the clear liquid whey drains out as filtrate.

Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)

LA 1 Explain the complete process of obtaining common salt from seawater in coastal areas. (5 Marks)

Principle: Separation of a dissolved solid from a liquid solvent using solar evaporation.

Step-by-Step Commercial Process:

  1. Trapping Seawater: During high tide, seawater containing dissolved salts (predominantly sodium chloride, ~3.5% salinity) is channeled into large, shallow open earthen embankments called salt pans (salt beds).
  2. Solar Heating & Evaporation: The shallow depth maximizes the exposed surface area. Under the intense heat of the sun and blowing coastal winds, liquid water slowly evaporates into invisible water vapour.
  3. Crystallization: Over several days to weeks, as water evaporates, the solution becomes super-saturated. White crystals of crude salt precipitate and accumulate on the bed of the pan.
  4. Harvesting & Purification: Workers scrape the crust of crude salt into heaps. The crude salt contains sand and other magnesium salts, so it is sent to refineries for washing, recrystallization, iodization, and packaging as edible table salt.
LA 2 Describe the method of folding a circular filter paper and assembling a laboratory filtration apparatus. (5 Marks)

Step-by-Step Cone Folding Technique:

  1. Take a clean, circular disc of laboratory filter paper.
  2. Fold the circular disc exactly in half along its diameter to form a semi-circle.
  3. Fold the semi-circle in half once more along its radius to form a quarter-circle wedge.
  4. Open out one of the four paper layers while keeping the other three layers together on the opposite side. This creates a hollow, conical funnel-shaped paper cone.

Apparatus Assembly & Operation:

  1. Insert the paper cone snugly into a glass funnel. Moisten the paper with a few drops of water so it adheres tightly to the glass funnel walls without air pockets.
  2. Mount the funnel in a ring clamp on an iron stand, placing the stem of the funnel inside a clean conical flask or beaker below.
  3. Pour the muddy liquid slowly into the funnel along a glass stirring rod, ensuring the liquid level never overflows the top rim of the paper cone.
  4. The clear liquid passes through as filtrate, leaving all solid particles trapped on the paper as residue.
LA 3 You are given a complex mixture containing iron pins, sand, salt, and camphor. Outline a stepwise scientific protocol to separate every single component. (5 Marks)

Protocol:

  1. Step 1: Magnetic Separation: Pass a strong bar magnet through the dry mixture. The iron pins are magnetic and will cling to the magnet. (Component 1 separated: Iron pins).
  2. Step 2: Sublimation: Place the remaining mixture (camphor, sand, salt) in a china dish covered with an inverted glass funnel whose neck is plugged with cotton. Heat the dish gently. Camphor sublimes directly from solid to vapour, rises, and crystallizes on the cool upper walls of the funnel. Scrape off the pure solid camphor. (Component 2 separated: Camphor).
  3. Step 3: Dissolution & Filtration: Add water to the remaining sand and salt mixture. Salt dissolves completely; sand does not. Pour through a filter paper. Insoluble sand is trapped on the filter paper as residue. Dry it. (Component 3 separated: Sand).
  4. Step 4: Evaporation: Heat the salt-water filtrate in a china dish. Water evaporates away, leaving pure common salt crystals behind. (Component 4 separated: Salt).

📜 Case Study 2: Clean Drinking Water in a Flood Relief Camp

Context: Severe monsoon floods in Assam submerged local tubewells, contaminating village drinking water with mud, sewage, and decaying leaves. A volunteer relief team set up an emergency multi-stage water purification station using 200-liter plastic barrels, alum (phitkari), sand, charcoal, and chlorine tablets.

Questions:

  1. What separation stage occurs when muddy floodwater is allowed to stand for 2 hours after stirring in a piece of alum?
  2. Why did the volunteers pass the decanted water through a multi-layer barrel containing gravel, fine sand, and charcoal?
  3. Why is boiling or adding chlorine tablets necessary even after the water looks crystal clear?
View Model Answers
  1. Sedimentation (Coagulation). Alum dissolves and neutralizes the electric charges on fine suspended clay particles, making them clump into heavier flocs that settle rapidly to the bottom.
  2. It acts as a multi-stage depth filter (Filtration). Gravel and sand trap remaining suspended particles, while activated charcoal adsorbs dissolved organic foul odours, pesticides, and toxins.
  3. Filtration only removes physical suspended particles; it cannot trap microscopic bacteria, cholera microbes, or viruses. Boiling or chlorination is required to disinfect and kill pathogens, making water biologically safe to drink.

🚨 Section 6: Exam Hall Mistakes & 15 Rapid Revision Points

❌ 5 Common Exam Hall Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Trying to separate dissolved salt by filtration: Salt is completely dissolved in water. Passing it through filter paper yields 100% of the salt in the filtrate! Dissolved solids must be separated by evaporation.
  • Mistake 2: Confusing threshing with winnowing: Threshing detaches grains from stalks by beating; winnowing separates detached husk from grains using wind.
  • Mistake 3: Confusing residue and filtrate: Residue is the solid that stays ON the filter paper; filtrate is the clear liquid that drips INTO the beaker.
  • Mistake 4: Claiming decantation produces 100% pure water: Decantation only separates coarse settled sediment; fine colloidal particles remain suspended. Filtration is needed for clarity.
  • Mistake 5: Using handpicking for industrial batches: Handpicking is valid only for small amounts of visibly distinct impurities.
