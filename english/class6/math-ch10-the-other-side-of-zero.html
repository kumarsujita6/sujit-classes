10.1 Bela’s Building of Fun & The Number Line

To make sense of numbers below zero, imagine Bela visiting the “Building of Fun”, a tall shopping complex with both upper floors and subterranean basements connected by an elevator.

The Elevator Floor Model

  • Welcome Hall (Ground Floor): Serves as the origin or reference datum: Floor 0.
  • Upper Floors: Above ground: +1 (Food Court), +2 (Art Centre), +3 (Book Store), +4... (Positive integers).
  • Basement Floors: Below ground: −1 (Toy Store), −2 (Video Games), −3 (Bowling Alley)... (Negative integers).
  • Elevator Button Presses: Pressing + moves the lift UP; pressing − moves the lift DOWN.
The Movement Equation: Starting Floor + Movement = Final Floor

- Start at Floor +2 and press −3: (+2) + (−3) = −1 (arrives at Toy Store).
- Start at Floor −1 and press +2: (−1) + (+2) = +1 (arrives at Food Court).
- Start at Floor +4 and press −3 and then −2: (+4) + (−3) + (−2) = −1.

Additive Inverses (Opposite Numbers)

Every positive integer has an exact opposite on the other side of zero:

  • Inverse of +4 is −4, because (+4) + (−4) = 0.
  • Inverse of −3 is +3, because (−3) + (+3) = 0.
  • The additive inverse of 0 is 0 itself (0 is neutral: neither positive nor negative).

The Integer Number Line & Comparison Law

On a horizontal number line, integers (ℤ) are laid out linearly:

The Directional Ordering Rule: Right is Always Greater

For any two integers a and b on the number line, if a lies to the right of b, then a > b:
• Positive numbers are greater than zero: +5 > 0.
• Zero is greater than every negative number: 0 > −3.
• For two negative numbers, the one closer to zero is greater: −2 > −7 (because −2 lies to the right of −7).

10.2 The Token Model & Zero Pairs

The Token Model provides a concrete manipulative method for mastering integer addition and subtraction without mechanical memorization.

The Two Counter Tokens

  • Positive Token (+): Represents +1 unit (green / yellow chip).
  • Negative Token (−): Represents −1 unit (red chip).
The Zero Pair Axiom

One positive token and one negative token neutralize each other completely:
(+1) + (−1) = 0
This combination is called a Zero Pair. Adding or removing any number of zero pairs leaves the total value completely unchanged!

Addition Using Tokens

  1. Same Signs: Simply combine all tokens.
    Example: (−3) + (−4) = 7 negative tokens = −7.
  2. Different Signs: Pair up positive and negative tokens into zero pairs and discard them. The un-cancelled tokens provide the final answer.
    Example: (+6) + (−2) = 2 zero pairs + 4 positive tokens left = +4.

Subtraction Using Tokens & Introducing Zero Pairs

Subtraction means “taking away”. When we lack the necessary tokens to take away, we introduce neutral zero pairs!

Worked Demonstration: Subtracting (−3) − (+5)

1. Start with 3 negative tokens: [−−−].
2. You need to take away 5 POSITIVE tokens, but there are no positive tokens present!
3. Introduce 5 neutral zero pairs: 5 × [(+) + (−)]. (Total value is still −3).
4. Now take away the 5 positive tokens: − (+5).
5. What remains? The original 3 negative tokens PLUS the 5 negative tokens from the zero pairs = 8 negative tokens = −8!

This concrete action proves the algebraic identity: Subtracting a number is identical to adding its opposite: a − b = a + (−b) and a − (−b) = a + b.

10.3 Integers in the Real World

1. Banking & Personal Finance

  • Credit / Deposit (+): Money added to account (e.g., deposited ₹100 ⇒ +₹100).
  • Debit / Withdrawal (−): Money taken out or spent (e.g., withdrew ₹40 ⇒ −₹40).
  • Overdraft / Negative Balance (−): If an account has ₹130 and ₹150 is spent, balance becomes 130 − 150 = −₹20 (owing ₹20 to the bank).

2. Geography & Sea Level Elevation

  • Sea Level = 0 meters datum.
  • Above Sea Level (+): Land elevation (Mount Everest = +8,848 m).
  • Below Sea Level (−): Ocean trenches and sub-sea depressions (Mariana Trench / Challenger Deep = −10,994 m; Dead Sea shore = −430 m).

3. Thermometry & Weather

  • 0°C: Freezing point of pure water.
  • Above 0°C (+): Warmth (e.g., Leh at 2:00 p.m. = +14°C).
  • Below 0°C (−): Freezing conditions (e.g., Leh at 2:00 a.m. = −4°C).

4. Historical Timeline (BCE and CE)

The Calendar Zero Fallacy

In the historical Gregorian calendar system, there is NO Year 0!
The year 1 BCE is followed immediately by the year 1 CE. When calculating time spans spanning across the BCE/CE boundary, you must account for the missing year 0:
• A span of 320 years after 680 BCE: −680 + 320 = −360 ⇒ 360 BCE.

10.4 Explorations with Integers & Sign Rules

The universal sign rules for arithmetic operations with signed numbers:

Arithmetic Expression Resulting Sign Conceptual Reason
(Positive) + (Positive) Always Positive Accumulating positive credits
(Negative) + (Negative) Always Negative Accumulating debts: −a + (−b) = −(a + b)
(Positive) + (Negative) Positive OR Negative Depends on which has the larger absolute magnitude
(Positive) − (Negative) Always Positive Subtracting a negative removes a debt: a − (−b) = a + b
(Negative) − (Positive) Always Negative Adding to existing debt: −a − b = −(a + b)
(Negative) − (Negative) Positive OR Negative −a − (−b) = −a + b (e.g. −2 − (−7) = +5; −7 − (−2) = −5)

10.5 A Pinch of History — Brahmagupta’s 628 CE Treatises

Negative numbers were met with skepticism in Western mathematics for centuries, but in ancient India, the astronomer-mathematician Brahmagupta formalized them completely in his masterpiece Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE).

  • Dhana (Fortune): Positive numbers (+).
  • Rina (Debt): Negative numbers (−).
  • Khasama / Sunya (Zero / Cypher): 0.

Brahmagupta wrote:

“The sum of two fortunes is a fortune. The sum of two debts is a debt. The sum of a fortune and a debt is their difference. A debt subtracted from zero is a fortune. A fortune subtracted from zero is a debt.”

4.2 Section 10.2 Solutions (The Token Model & Subtractions)

Exercise 10.2 — Q1: Subtracting Using Zero Pairs: (−3) − (+5)

Question: Try to subtract: −3 − (+5). How many zero pairs will you have to put in? What is the final result?

💡 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Given:
Expression: (−3) − (+5). Initial state: 3 negative tokens [−−−].
Target removal: 5 positive tokens.

Step 2: Principle used:
When tokens to be subtracted are absent, add equal numbers of positive and negative tokens (zero pairs) to leave total value invariant.

Step 3: Step-by-step token working:
- We need to remove 5 positive tokens, so we add 5 zero pairs: 5 × [(+1) + (−1)].
- Current collection: 3 negative tokens + 5 positive tokens + 5 negative tokens.
- Take away the 5 positive tokens.
- Remaining tokens = 3 negatives + 5 negatives = 8 negative tokens (−8).

Step 4: Final value:
5 zero pairs must be inserted; Result = −8.

Step 5: Concluding statement:
We have to put in five zero pairs, and the final result is −8.

Exercise 10.4 — Q2: The Signed Dice Roll Challenge

Question: Two dice have faces numbered: −1, 2, −3, 4, −5, 6. The minimum sum is −10 [(−5)+(−5)] and maximum sum is +12 [(+6)+(+6)]. Which integer sums between −10 and +12 are IMPOSSIBLE to obtain?

💡 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Face values:
Odd numbers are negative: {−1, −3, −5}.
Even numbers are positive: {+2, +4, +6}.

Step 2: Analyzing parity of sums:
- Odd + Odd = (Negative Odd) + (Negative Odd) = Even Negative (−2, −4, −6, −8, −10).
- Even + Even = (Positive Even) + (Positive Even) = Even Positive (+4, +6, +8, +10, +12).
- Odd + Even = (Negative Odd) + (Positive Even) = Odd Signed Number (e.g. −5+2 = −3, −1+4 = +3, etc.).
Notice that zero is even, but the only way to get 0 would be a face plus its opposite, which do not exist in the set (we have −1 but no +1; +2 but no −2).

Step 3: Checking all integers:
The sums that can NEVER be formed are: −9, −7, −5, 0, 2, 7, 9, 11.

Step 4: Final value:
Impossible sums: −9, −7, −5, 0, 2, 7, 9, 11.

Step 5: Concluding statement:
These 8 numbers cannot be formed by adding any pair of faces from the two dice.

5.1 Multiple Choice Questions (5 MCQs with Trap Analysis)

MCQ 1: Integer Comparison Trap

Question: Which of the following comparison statements is TRUE?

A) −8 > −3     B) −15 > 0     C) −10 < −2     D) 0 < −5

💡 Step-by-Step Solution & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: C) −10 < −2

Conceptual Trap Decoded: Students familiar with positive magnitudes know 10 > 2 and often transfer this intuition to negatives, incorrectly choosing Option A (−8 > −3). On the number line, numbers further to the left are smaller. Since −10 lies to the left of −2, −10 is strictly less than −2.

MCQ 2: Double Negative Subtraction

Question: The value of (−14) − (−20) is:

A) −34     B) +34     C) −6     D) +6

💡 Step-by-Step Solution & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: D) +6

Conceptual Trap Decoded: Option A (−34) is the trap where students ignore the double negative and add the magnitudes with a negative sign: −14 − 20 = −34. In reality, subtracting a negative equals adding a positive: −14 − (−20) = −14 + 20 = +6.

MCQ 3: Elevation Difference

Question: A submarine is cruising at −250 meters below sea level, while an aircraft flies directly above it at +1,200 meters above sea level. What is the vertical distance between them?

A) 950 m     B) 1,450 m     C) −1,450 m     D) 1,200 m

💡 Step-by-Step Solution & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: B) 1,450 m

Conceptual Trap Decoded: Option A (950 m) is obtained by subtracting the numbers without regard to sign: 1,200 − 250 = 950 m. The true vertical distance is the total span from +1,200 m down through sea level (0 m) to −250 m: 1,200 − (−250) = 1,200 + 250 = 1,450 meters.

MCQ 4: Additive Inverse of an Expression

Question: What is the additive inverse of the expression: (−7) + (+3)?

A) −4     B) +4     C) −10     D) +10

💡 Step-by-Step Solution & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: B) +4

Conceptual Trap Decoded: First evaluate the expression: (−7) + (+3) = −4. Many students stop here and pick Option A (−4), forgetting that the question asks for the additive inverse of this result. The additive inverse of −4 is +4.

MCQ 5: The Calendar Calculation

Question: How many total calendar years elapsed from 50 BCE to 50 CE?

A) 100 years     B) 101 years     C) 99 years     D) 50 years

💡 Step-by-Step Solution & Conceptual Trap

Correct Answer: C) 99 years

Conceptual Trap Decoded: Option A (100) is the classic trap! In standard integer arithmetic, 50 − (−50) = 100. However, there was no Year 0 in the calendar. The transition goes directly from 1 BCE to 1 CE (only 1 year elapsed, not 2). Thus, the actual elapsed time is 100 − 1 = 99 years.

5.2 Assertion & Reasoning Questions (4 Questions)

Directions: Choose (A) if both A and R are true and R is correct explanation of A; (B) if both are true but R is NOT correct explanation; (C) if A is true but R is false; (D) if A is false but R is true.

A&R 1: Subtraction of Integers

Assertion (A): For any two integers a and b, a − (−b) = a + b.
Reason (R): Subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding its additive inverse.

💡 Step-by-Step Solution

Answer: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Explanation: The additive inverse of −b is +b. By definition of subtraction, a − x = a + (−x). Substituting x = −b yields a + (−(−b)) = a + b.

A&R 2: Sum of Negative Integers

Assertion (A): The sum of two negative integers is always a negative integer.
Reason (R): Adding two debts results in an accumulated total debt.

💡 Step-by-Step Solution

Answer: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Explanation: In token notation, combining red tokens with more red tokens can only produce red tokens: (−a) + (−b) = −(a + b). Reason captures Brahmagupta's ancient debt rule.

A&R 3: Zero and Positive Integers

Assertion (A): Zero is the smallest positive integer.
Reason (R): Zero is greater than every negative integer.

💡 Step-by-Step Solution

Answer: (D) Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.

Explanation: Zero is NOT a positive integer! Zero is neutral (neither positive nor negative). The smallest positive integer is +1. Reason (R) is mathematically true (0 > −x for all positive x), but Assertion is false.

A&R 4: Integer Subtraction Non-Commutativity

Assertion (A): Integer subtraction is NOT commutative: a − b ≠ b − a (for a ≠ b).
Reason (R): 5 − 3 = +2, whereas 3 − 5 = −2.

💡 Step-by-Step Solution

Answer: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is a valid counterexample illustrating A.

Explanation: In general, a − b = −(b − a). Unless a = b, swapping terms changes the sign of the result from positive to negative.

Exam Hall Trap Alerts & 15 Rapid Revision One-Liners

Top 5 Exam Hall Traps in Integers

⚠️ Trap 1: Transferring Positive Magnitude Ordering to Negatives

A classic blunder is writing −10 > −2 simply because 10 > 2. On the number line, numbers further to the left are strictly smaller. Since −10 is 10 steps to the left of zero while −2 is only 2 steps left, −2 > −10. Think of temperature: −2°C is much warmer than −10°C!

⚠️ Trap 2: The “Two Negatives Make a Positive” Addition Fallacy

Students memorize the catchphrase “two negatives make a positive” and catastrophically apply it to addition: (−5) + (−3) = +8 ✗. Adding debts can only accumulate more debt! (−5) + (−3) = −8. The rule applies ONLY to multiplication or double negative subtraction: −(−3) = +3.

⚠️ Trap 3: Treating Zero as a Positive Integer

In multiple choice tests, students often classify 0 as the “smallest positive integer”. Zero is neutral (neither positive nor negative)! The smallest positive integer is +1.

⚠️ Trap 4: The Calendar Year 0 Trap in BCE/CE Calculations

When calculating the number of years between 50 BCE and 50 CE, writing 50 − (−50) = 100 years loses marks. Because there was NO Year 0 in the calendar (1 BCE transitioned directly to 1 CE), the true elapsed duration is 100 − 1 = 99 years.

⚠️ Trap 5: Misinterpreting Subtraction of a Larger Number

When computing 7 − 12, students frequently flip the numbers to 12 − 7 = 5. Always remember that subtracting a larger positive number pulls you across zero into negative territory: 7 − 12 = −5.

15 Rapid Revision One-Liners (Pre-Exam Checklist)

  1. Integers (ℤ) comprise positive natural numbers, zero, and negative whole numbers: {..., −3, −2, −1, 0, +1, +2, +3, ...}.
  2. Zero is the neutral origin on the number line; it is neither positive nor negative.
  3. On the integer number line, any number situated to the right is strictly greater than any number to its left.
  4. Every negative integer is strictly less than zero: −n < 0.
  5. Between two negative numbers, the one with the smaller absolute value is greater (e.g., −3 > −8).
  6. The additive inverse of integer a is −a, such that a + (−a) = 0.
  7. A zero pair consists of one positive token and one negative token: (+1) + (−1) = 0.
  8. The sum of two positive integers is always positive; the sum of two negative integers is always negative.
  9. To add integers with opposite signs, subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger and attach the sign of the larger magnitude.
  10. Subtracting an integer is identical to adding its additive inverse: a − b = a + (−b).
  11. Subtracting a negative integer is identical to adding a positive integer: a − (−b) = a + b.
  12. In finance, deposits/credits are positive (+) and withdrawals/debits/overdrafts are negative (−).
  13. In geography, elevations above sea level are positive (+) and ocean depths below sea level are negative (−).
  14. There is no Year 0 in the historical Gregorian calendar: 1 BCE was followed immediately by 1 CE.
  15. In 628 CE, Indian mathematician Brahmagupta formalized the universal arithmetic of fortunes (dhana), debts (rina), and zero (khasama).
