Mastering Mirrors, Lenses, and Ray Optics

From the shiny curves of a kitchen spoon to telescope lenses, explained with joyful teacher clarity.

10.1 Spherical Mirrors: The Kitchen Spoon Discovery

Pick up a shiny, clean stainless steel tablespoon from your kitchen right now. Look at your reflection on both sides:

  • Inner Curved Surface (Concave side): If you hold it close, your face looks huge (magnified) and upright! If you slowly pull it away from your eyes, your image suddenly flips completely upside down (inverted)!
  • Outer Bulging Surface (Convex side): No matter how close or far you hold the back of the spoon, your face is always upright (erect) and tiny (diminished), and you can see a wide view of your entire room behind you!
Teacher's Definitions • Spherical Mirrors:
A mirror whose reflecting surface is a part of a hollow sphere of glass or metal.
• Concave Mirror: A spherical mirror whose reflecting surface curves inwards (towards the center of the sphere, like a hollow cave). It converges parallel light rays to a single point.
• Convex Mirror: A spherical mirror whose reflecting surface bulges outwards. It diverges light rays outward over a wide area.

10.2 The Laws of Reflection & Angle Geometry

Whenever light hits any smooth, shiny surface (whether flat or curved), it obeys two inviolable laws of physics:

  1. First Law of Reflection: The angle of incidence (∠ i) is always equal to the angle of reflection (∠ r). Both angles are measured between the light rays and the Normal (an imaginary perpendicular line drawn at 90° to the mirror surface at the point of incidence).
    ∠ i = ∠ r
  2. Second Law of Reflection: The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same geometric plane.
Crucial Board Exam Trap • Normal Incidence (∠ i = 0°):
What happens when a ray of light hits a mirror straight along the normal (perpendicularly)?
Students often wrongly answer: "Angle of incidence is 90°!"
Correct Answer: The ray is parallel to the normal, so the Angle of Incidence ∠ i = 0°! By the law of reflection, the Angle of Reflection ∠ r = 0°. The ray bounces straight back along its own path!

10.3 Why Vehicle Mirrors Warn: "Objects are Closer Than They Appear"

Have you ever read the printed warning on the side-view mirror of a car or motorcycle? Why do automakers use convex mirrors instead of plane mirrors?

  • Wide Field of View: Because a convex mirror curves outward, it captures light from a much wider angle than a flat plane mirror. The driver can see several lanes of traffic behind them!
  • Always Erect and Diminished: A convex mirror always produces an upright image, compressed into a smaller size.
  • The Optical Catch: Because the cars appear smaller (diminished), our brain naturally assumes they are far away. In reality, that trailing truck is right behind you! Hence, the vital safety warning: "Objects in mirror are closer than they appear."
Why Dentists & ENT Specialists Use Concave Mirrors:
When an object is placed very close to a concave mirror (closer than its focal point), the mirror forms a virtual, erect, and highly magnified image. A dentist places a tiny concave mirror inside your mouth right beside a molar tooth to see a giant, crystal-clear view of small cavities and plaque!

10.4 What Is a Lens? Convex vs Concave Lenses

A lens is a piece of transparent glass or plastic bounded by one or two curved spherical surfaces. While mirrors reflect light backwards, lenses let light pass through them, bending (refracting) the rays:

Feature Convex Lens (Converging) Concave Lens (Diverging)
Physical Shape Thick in the middle, thin and tapered at the outer edges. Thin in the middle, thicker at the outer edges.
Action on Light Rays Converges parallel rays inward towards a focal point. Diverges parallel rays outward away from each other.
Nature of Image Can form both real (inverted) and virtual (magnified erect) images. Always forms virtual, erect, and diminished images.
Everyday Uses Magnifying glass, microscope, telescope, reading glasses for hypermetropia. Spectacles for myopia (nearsightedness), peepholes in hotel doors.

10.5 The Bent Pencil: The Magic of Refraction

In NCERT Activity 10.11, place a pencil diagonally inside an empty glass tumbler: it looks completely straight. Now pour water until the tumbler is half-full:

  • The pencil appears broken or sharply bent at the water's surface!
  • The submerged part of the pencil looks significantly thicker and closer to the viewer!

Why? Light travels faster in air (approx 300,000 km/s) than in water (approx 225,000 km/s). When light rays reflected from the submerged pencil travel from dense water into rare air, they speed up and bend away from the normal. Our brain assumes light travels in dead-straight lines, tracing the rays back to an apparent higher position, creating the optical illusion of a bent pencil!

10.7 Indian Optical Heritage: Bhaskara II & Water-Mirror Astronomy

Over 800 years ago in 12th-century India, the renowned mathematician and astronomer Bhāskara II (author of Siddhanta Shiromani) developed sophisticated methods to study stars and planetary orbits.

Because direct viewing of bright celestial bodies can strain the eyes and introduce parallax errors, ancient Indian observatories used perfectly calm, shallow black bowls filled with clear water or mercury as horizontal plane mirrors. By sighting the sharp reflected starlight through calibrated brass tubes (Nalika Yantra) set at precise angles, astronomers accurately mapped astronomical coordinates centuries before modern telescopes were invented!

Q1. Light ray incident on mirror. Angle with normal is 40°. What is angle made by reflected ray with the mirror? Angle Geometry

Correct Option: (ii) 50°

Step-by-Step Teacher's Solution:

  • Angle of incidence (∠ i) with normal = 40°.
  • By the first law of reflection: Angle of reflection (∠ r) = ∠ i = 40° (with the normal).
  • The normal is perpendicular to the mirror surface (makes 90° with the mirror).
  • Therefore, the angle made by the reflected ray with the mirror surface = 90° - ∠ r = 90° - 40° = 50°.
Q2. Fig. 10.22 ray falling on mirror: (i) along normal; (ii) tilted mirror along normal; (iii) tilted at 20° from normal. What is angle of reflection in each case? Ray Tracing

Analysis for each case:

  • Case (i): Light ray falls along the normal. Since the incident ray coincides with the normal, the angle of incidence is ∠ i = 0°. Hence, Angle of reflection ∠ r = 0°. The reflected ray bounces straight back along the same line!
  • Case (ii): Mirror is tilted, but the light ray still falls along the normal to the tilted surface. Angle of incidence is ∠ i = 0°. Hence, Angle of reflection ∠ r = 0°. The ray retraces its path straight back.
  • Case (iii): Mirror is tilted, and the light ray falls at an angle of 20° from the normal. Here, ∠ i = 20°. By the law of reflection, Angle of reflection ∠ r = 20° on the opposite side of the normal.
Q3. Match cap of sketch pen placed in front of three mirrors (Fig. 10.23): Plane, Convex, Concave. Matching

Matching Table:

  • Image (i) — Same Size: Plane mirror (A plane mirror always produces an image of identical size to the object).
  • Image (ii) — Diminished (Smaller): Convex mirror (A convex mirror always produces an erect, smaller image).
  • Image (iii) — Magnified (Larger): Concave mirror (When placed close to a concave mirror, it forms an enlarged erect image).
Q4. Match pen cap behind convex lens, concave lens, and flat glass piece (Fig. 10.24). Matching

Matching Table:

  • Image (i) — Same Size (Unaltered): Flat transparent glass piece (Does not converge or diverge rays; no magnification).
  • Image (ii) — Magnified (Enlarged): Convex lens (Acts as a magnifying glass when the object is near).
  • Image (iii) — Diminished (Smaller): Concave lens (Always produces a diminished, upright image).
Q5. When light is incident along the normal on the mirror, which statement is true? Concept

Correct Option: (ii) Angle of incidence is 0°

Explanation: The angle of incidence is defined as the angle between the incident ray and the normal. When the incident ray travels directly along the normal, there is zero angular separation between them, so ∠ i = 0°.

Q6. Three mirrors in Fig. 10.25 showing images of a graph sheet. Identify the mirrors. Image Identification

Identification based on grid square size:

  • Mirror 1 (Squares are same size as real sheet): Plane mirror.
  • Mirror 2 (Squares are visibly smaller and compressed): Convex mirror (Diminishing effect).
  • Mirror 3 (Squares are enlarged and stretched out): Concave mirror (Magnifying effect).
Q7. In a museum, a woman walks towards a large concave mirror (Fig. 10.26). What will she see? Concave Mirror Dynamics

Correct Option: (iii) her inverted image keeps increasing in size and eventually it becomes erect and magnified.

Teacher's Detailed Optical Journey:

  • When she is far beyond the center of curvature (C), she sees a small, inverted image.
  • As she walks closer towards the focus (F), her inverted image gets bigger and bigger (increasing in size).
  • The moment she steps inside the focal point (between Focus and Mirror), the image flips upright and becomes a giant, erect, and highly magnified virtual image!
Q8. Hold a magnifying glass over text. What happens as you move it away? What type of lens is it? Hands-on Inquiry

Answer & Observations:

  • A magnifying glass is a Convex lens (converging lens).
  • When held close to the text (within its focal length), the letters appear upright (erect) and significantly larger (magnified).
  • As you pull the magnifying glass farther away beyond its focus, the letters become blurry, and suddenly they flip completely upside-down (inverted) and become smaller!
Q9. Match Column I with Column II (Concave/Convex mirrors and lenses). Column Match

Correct Matches:

  • (i) Concave mirror → (a) Spherical mirror with a reflecting surface that curves inwards.
  • (ii) Convex mirror → (b) It forms an image which is always erect and diminished in size.
  • (iii) Convex lens → (c) Object placed behind it may appear inverted at some distance.
  • (iv) Concave lens → (d) Object placed behind it always appears diminished in size.
Q10. Assertion: Convex mirrors are preferred for observing traffic behind us. Reason: Convex mirrors provide a significantly larger view area than plane mirrors. Assertion-Reason

Correct Option: (i) Both Assertion and Reason are correct and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Explanation: Convex mirrors curve outward, creating a diverging field of view that enables the driver to see a much broader panoramic view of traffic than a flat mirror could ever provide, while keeping images upright.

Q11. In Fig. 10.27 (O = object, M = mirror, I = image), identify mirrors in (a) and (b). Diagram Analysis

Correct Option: (iii) Figure (a) indicates a concave mirror and Figure (b) indicates a convex mirror.

Reasoning: In Fig (a), the image I is erect and larger than object O (magnified), which is characteristic of a concave mirror at close range. In Fig (b), the image I is erect and smaller than object O (diminished), which is the signature characteristic of a convex mirror.

Q12. Pencil behind transparent glass tumbler filled halfway with water (Fig. 10.28). How does it appear and why? Refraction

Observations: The pencil appears broken, bent, and displaced sideways at the boundary between air and water, and the submerged lower portion appears thicker and magnified.

Scientific Explanation (Refraction):

  • Light rays reflected from the submerged pencil travel from water into air.
  • Because water is optically denser than air, the light rays speed up and bend away from the normal as they cross the water-glass-air interface.
  • When these bent rays reach our eyes, our brain traces them straight back, making the submerged part of the pencil appear shifted upwards and sideways, creating the illusion of a bent pencil.
  • Additionally, the cylindrical curvature of the water-filled glass tumbler acts like a convex lens, magnifying the submerged pencil!

Section 6 • Exam Traps & Key Takeaways

Board Exam Traps, Common Mistakes & Memory Shortcuts

Guard against these frequent optical mistakes to ensure full marks.

Exam Trap #1: 'Normal vs Surface Angles'
Mistake: If a question states "the ray strikes the mirror at an angle of 30° to the mirror," students immediately assume ∠ i = 30°.
Full-Marks Correction: The angle of incidence is always measured with the NORMAL, never the mirror surface! Here, ∠ i = 90° - 30° = 60°, so ∠ r = 60°.
Exam Trap #2: 'Convex Mirror vs Convex Lens'
Mistake: Confusing curved mirrors with lenses.
Full-Marks Correction: Remember: Convex Mirror = Diverging (pushes light rays away), whereas Convex Lens = Converging (brings light rays together to a focus)!
Teacher's Memory Hack • C-A-V-E Rule:
• Concave: Looks like a dark hollow CAVE that traps light inward (Converging).
• Convex: VEXES (scatters) light outwards in all directions (Diverging)!
