Deep Mechanics: Push, Pull & Equilibrium

Learn how forces interact, calculate resultant forces, and classify contact vs non-contact interactions.

1. What is Force? Interactions & Direction

In everyday speech, we use many words: pushing a supermarket trolley, pulling open a refrigerator door, hitting a tennis ball, kicking a football, or lifting a heavy school bag. In science, all these actions are grouped into a single unified concept: a push or a pull exerted upon an object.

Fundamental Law of Interaction:
A force does not exist in isolation! A force comes into play only when at least two objects interact with each other. A book lying on a shelf cannot push itself; your hand must physically touch or push the book to make it slide. The SI unit of force is the Newton (N).

2. Combining Forces: The Net Force

When multiple forces act on an object simultaneously, their overall combined effect is known as the Net Force (or Resultant Force).

Figure 5.2: Calculating Net Force — Forces add up when aligned, but subtract when acting in opposition.

Balanced vs. Unbalanced Forces:

  • Balanced Forces: If two teams in a tug-of-war pull with exactly equal force (e.g. 500 N vs 500 N), the net force is zero ($500 - 500 = 0 ext{ N}$). The central rope does not budge! Balanced forces cannot change the state of motion of an object.
  • Unbalanced Forces: If Team A pulls with 600 N while Team B pulls with 450 N, the net force is $150 ext{ N}$ towards Team A. The rope immediately accelerates in the direction of Team A! Only an unbalanced force produces a change in motion.

3. The Four Core Effects of Force

1. Start or Stop Motion

A kick sets a stationary football in motion; a goalkeeper applies force to stop the rolling ball.

2. Change Speed

Pedaling harder applies force to accelerate a bicycle; pressing brake levers exerts force to slow it down.

3. Change Direction

A batsman hits a straight delivery towards the boundary covers, changing the ball's travel trajectory.

4. Change Shape / Size

Squeezing an inflated balloon, rolling dough with a rolling pin, or stretching an elastic rubber band.

4. Contact Forces vs. Non-Contact Forces

Classification Type of Force How It Operates & Everyday Example
Contact Forces
(Requires physical touching)		 Muscular Force Force produced by bodily muscles. Lifting a bucket of water, a bullock pulling a cart, chewing food.
Frictional Force Opposes motion between two surfaces in contact. A rolling ball stops on grass due to friction between ball and blades.
Non-Contact Forces
(Action-at-a-distance)		 Magnetic Force Magnets exert attraction or repulsion without touching (like poles repel, opposite attract).
Electrostatic Force Force exerted by an electrically charged body. A plastic scale rubbed on dry hair attracts tiny paper scraps without contact.
Gravitational Force Universal attractive pull of the Earth (and all masses). Ripe mangoes falling down from trees, water flowing downward in rivers.
Section 3 • High-Yield Fact & Term Bank

Physics Concepts & Key Definitions

Quick-memorization cards for exams.

Force

A push or pull upon an object resulting from an interaction between two bodies. SI unit: Newton (N).

Net Force

The single resultant force that represents the combined vector sum of all individual forces acting upon a body.

Friction

A contact force that always acts parallel to touching surfaces and in the direction opposite to relative motion.

Electrostatic Force

The non-contact attractive or repulsive force exerted by an object possessing static electric charge.

Gravity

The non-contact universal attractive force with which the Earth and all celestial bodies pull masses toward their centers.

Balanced Forces

Equal and opposite forces acting on an object whose net force equals zero, preserving its state of rest or uniform motion.

Section 4 • 100% Complete NCERT Solutions

Textbook In-Text & Chapter Exercises

Step-by-step teacher solutions with complete explanations.

Q1: Give two examples each of situations in which you push or pull to change the state of motion of objects. NCERT Core
  • Push Examples:
    • Pushing a stationary swing to start it oscillating.
    • Pushing a stalled car from behind to make it move forward.
  • Pull Examples:
    • Pulling a bucket of water up from a deep well.
    • Pulling open a heavy classroom drawer to fetch a book.
Q2: Two people push a heavy wooden box in the same direction with forces of 25 N and 35 N respectively. Find the resultant net force acting on the box. Numerical Calculation

Step-by-Step Calculation:

  • Force applied by person 1 ($F_1$) = $25 ext{ N}$
  • Force applied by person 2 ($F_2$) = $35 ext{ N}$
  • Since both forces act along the same direction, they add together:
  • $ ext{Net Force} = F_1 + F_2 = 25 ext{ N} + 35 ext{ N} = 60 ext{ N}$
  • Conclusion: The resultant net force is 60 N acting in the direction of the push.
Q3: An archer stretches her bow while taking aim at the target. She then releases the arrow, which begins to move towards the target. Identify the forces acting at each stage. Real-Life Application
  1. To stretch the bow, the archer applies muscular force, which causes a change in the bow's shape.
  2. When released, the arrow shoots forward due to the stored elastic energy transferring into kinetic motion.
  3. While flying in the air, two forces act upon the arrow:
    • Frictional force of air (air resistance): Opposes its forward motion.
    • Gravitational force: Pulls the arrow downward toward the Earth.
Q4: In a tug-of-war, team A exerts a force of 1200 N towards the east, while team B exerts a force of 1200 N towards the west. What is the net force, and will the central mark move? Equilibrium
  • $ ext{Net Force} = 1200 ext{ N (East)} - 1200 ext{ N (West)} = 0 ext{ N}$
  • Because the forces are equal in magnitude and directly opposite in direction, they represent balanced forces.
  • The net force is exactly zero, and therefore the central ribbon will remain completely stationary.
Q5: Why does a ball rolling on a horizontal floor eventually slow down and stop even when no one is touching it? Friction Physics

Explanation:

  • Even though no human is touching the ball, an invisible contact force called friction acts between the bottom surface of the rolling ball and the floor.
  • Friction always acts in the direction opposite to the direction of motion.
  • This opposing force continuously resists the forward momentum of the ball, gradually reducing its speed until it comes to a complete halt.

Q1: Which of the following is a non-contact force that operates across empty space?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) Gravitational Force
Gravity pulls objects toward the Earth without requiring physical contact. Muscular force, friction, and rope tension all require physical contact.

Q2: Two boys push a cart in opposite directions. Boy A pushes with 40 N towards the left, while Boy B pushes with 60 N towards the right. The cart will:

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a)
Net force = $60 ext{ N} - 40 ext{ N} = 20 ext{ N}$ towards the right (in the direction of the larger force).

Q3: A plastic comb rubbed vigorously through dry hair attracts bits of dry paper. This phenomenon demonstrates:

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) Electrostatic force
Friction transfers electrons to the plastic comb, giving it an electric charge that attracts neutral paper scraps without physical touching.

Q4: Which of the following situations demonstrates a force changing the SHAPE of an object rather than its speed?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c)
Molding clay alters its geometric shape and dimensions rather than driving translational motion.

Q5: When two balanced forces act on a stationary box from opposite sides, the net force is:

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (d) Exactly Zero
Balanced forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction ($F - F = 0$), resulting in zero acceleration.

Exam Traps & Key Takeaways

Quick revision pearls to prevent loss of marks.

Common Student Traps to Avoid:
  • Trap 1: Forgetting direction when stating a force: Force is a vector! Never write just "Force = 15 N"; always write magnitude AND direction (e.g. "15 N towards the East").
  • Trap 2: Saying a moving object must have an unbalanced force: An object moving at a constant speed in a straight line has a NET FORCE OF ZERO (balanced forces)! Unbalanced forces are only needed to change speed or direction.
  • Trap 3: Classifying Gravity as contact: Gravity acts through empty space, vacuum, and air—it is strictly a non-contact force.
Final 1-Minute Master Checklist:
• Force: Push or pull arising from interaction (Unit: Newton, N).
• Net Force: Same direction add ($F_1 + F_2$); opposite direction subtract ($F_1 - F_2$).
• Balanced Forces: Net force = 0; no change in motion.
• Unbalanced Forces: Net force > 0; causes acceleration/turning.
• Contact Forces: Muscular, Friction.
• Non-Contact Forces: Magnetic, Electrostatic, Gravitational.
