Section 02

Deep-Dive Core Concepts & Teacher Explanations

1. The Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

Draw any four-sided shape ABCD. Draw a single diagonal AC. What happens?

Teacher's Proof: Diagonal AC splits the quadrilateral into 2 triangles: ΔABC and ΔADC.
• The sum of angles of ΔABC = 180°.
• The sum of angles of ΔADC = 180°.
• Total sum of all 4 interior angles = 180° + 180° = 360°!
∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D = 360°

2. Master Table of Quadrilateral Properties

Quadrilateral Opposite Sides Opposite Angles Adjacent Angles Diagonal Properties
Parallelogram Parallel and Equal Equal (∠A = ∠C, ∠B = ∠D) Supplementary (add to 180°) Bisect each other
Rhombus All 4 sides equal; opposite sides parallel Equal Supplementary Perpendicular bisectors of each other
Rectangle Opposite sides parallel and equal All angles are 90° Each 90° (sum = 180°) Equal in length and bisect each other
Square All 4 sides equal and opposite parallel All angles are 90° Each 90° Equal in length AND perpendicular bisectors
Trapezium Exactly 1 pair parallel Not necessarily equal Consecutive angles along parallel sides sum to 180° Intersect, but do not bisect
Kite 2 pairs adjacent equal sides One pair of opposite angles equal Not supplementary Diagonals are perpendicular; one bisects the other

3. Constructing a Quadrilateral

How many independent measurements are needed to construct a quadrilateral uniquely?

The Golden Rule of Construction: While 3 measurements suffice for a triangle, a quadrilateral has 4 sides + 4 angles + 2 diagonals = 10 elements!
To construct a unique quadrilateral, you need at least 5 independent measurements:
• 4 sides and 1 diagonal
• 2 adjacent sides and 3 angles
• 3 sides and 2 included angles
• 3 sides and 2 diagonals
Section 03

In-Text Activities & Classroom Explorations (Fully Solved)

Activity 1 (Page 3): If ABCD is a quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other, what can we deduce about ABCD?

Proof: Let diagonals AC and BD intersect at point O such that OA = OC and OB = OD.

In ΔAOB and ΔCOD:

  • OA = OC (given)
  • OB = OD (given)
  • ∠AOB = ∠COD (vertically opposite angles)

Therefore, by SAS Congruence, ΔAOB ≅ ΔCOD.
⇒ AB = CD and ∠OAB = ∠OCD.
Since alternate interior angles are equal, AB || CD.

Similarly, ΔAOD ≅ ΔCOB ⇒ AD = BC and AD || BC.

Conclusion: A quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other is always a Parallelogram!

Activity 2 (Page 15): Is every rectangle a parallelogram? Is every parallelogram a rectangle?

Part 1: Yes, every rectangle is a parallelogram.
In a rectangle, opposite sides are equal and parallel, and opposite angles are equal (both are 90°). Hence it satisfies all conditions of a parallelogram.

Part 2: No, not every parallelogram is a rectangle.
A general parallelogram does not need to have 90° angles. Only when the angles of a parallelogram are right angles does it become a rectangle.

Activity 3 (Page 21): Why do the diagonals of a rhombus intersect at right angles (90°)?

Proof: Let rhombus ABCD have diagonals intersecting at O.
Since a rhombus is a parallelogram, OA = OC and OB = OD.
Now consider ΔAOB and ΔCOB:

  • OA = OC (diagonals bisect)
  • AB = BC (all sides of a rhombus are equal!)
  • OB = OB (common side)
By SSS Congruence, ΔAOB ≅ ΔCOB.
⇒ ∠AOB = ∠COB.
Since AC is a straight line, ∠AOB + ∠COB = 180° (linear pair).
⇒ 2∠AOB = 180° ⇒ ∠AOB = 90°.
Conclusion: The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors!

Section 04

Complete End-of-Chapter 'Figure it Out' Exercises (Solved Step-by-Step)

Exercise 1: Two adjacent angles of a parallelogram are in the ratio 3 : 2. Find the measure of each of the four angles.
Concept: Adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary (sum = 180°).
Assume: Let the angles be 3x and 2x.
Equation: 3x + 2x = 180° ⇒ 5x = 180° ⇒ x = 36°.
Angles: First angle = 3 × 36° = 108°. Second angle = 2 × 36° = 72°.
Opposites: Opposite angles are equal, so the other two angles are also 108° and 72°.
Result: The four angles are 108°, 72°, 108°, and 72°.
Exercise 2: The diagonals of a rhombus are 16 cm and 12 cm. Find the length of each side of the rhombus.
Concept: Diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at 90°.
Half-diagonals: d₁/2 = 16/2 = 8 cm, and d₂/2 = 12/2 = 6 cm.
Right Triangle: Each side 's' forms the hypotenuse: s² = 8² + 6².
Calculation: s² = 64 + 36 = 100 ⇒ s = √100 = 10 cm.
Result: The length of each side of the rhombus is 10 cm.
Exercise 3: In a trapezium ABCD, AB || CD. If ∠A = 55° and ∠B = 70°, find ∠D and ∠C.
Parallel Rule: Consecutive interior angles on the same side of a transversal add up to 180°.
Find ∠D: ∠A + ∠D = 180° ⇒ 55° + ∠D = 180° ⇒ ∠D = 180° - 55° = 125°.
Find ∠C: ∠B + ∠C = 180° ⇒ 70° + ∠C = 180° ⇒ ∠C = 180° - 70° = 110°.
Result: ∠D = 125° and ∠C = 110°.

Interactive Practice Quiz Engine

Score: 0 / 5 Answer the questions below to test your mastery!

1. What is the sum of the four interior angles of any quadrilateral?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) 360°.
Any quadrilateral can be split into 2 triangles. Since each triangle has an angle sum of 180°, 180° × 2 = 360°.

2. In which quadrilateral are the diagonals BOTH equal in length and perpendicular to each other?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (d) Square.
A rectangle has equal diagonals (not perpendicular). A rhombus has perpendicular diagonals (not equal). Only a square combines both!

3. If one angle of a parallelogram is 65°, what is the measure of its adjacent angle?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a) 115°.
Adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary: 180° - 65° = 115°.

4. How many independent measurements are generally required to construct a unique quadrilateral?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) 5.
To fix the position and rigid shape of a 4-sided figure, exactly 5 independent pieces of data are needed.

5. Which of the following statements is FALSE?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c).
A kite does NOT have opposite sides parallel or equal; its adjacent sides are equal. Hence a kite is not a parallelogram.

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Pitfall: Thinking diagonals of a parallelogram are equal!
In a general parallelogram, one diagonal is longer and one is shorter! They only become equal in a Rectangle or Square.
