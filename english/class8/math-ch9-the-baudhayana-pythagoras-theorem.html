Section 02

Deep-Dive Core Concepts & Teacher Explanations

1. Ancient Origins: Doubling a Square in the Śulba-Sūtras

Baudhāyana's Question (c. 800 BCE): "How can one construct a square having exactly double the area of a given square?"
Baudhāyana's Answer: Construct a square on the diagonal of the given square!
• Let original square side = a. Area = a².
• Draw diagonal d. By the triangle rule, d² = a² + a² = 2a².
• The square on the diagonal has area exactly 2a² (Double!).

2. The Theorem Statement

The Baudhāyana-Pythagoras Theorem: In any right-angled triangle, the area of the square built on the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the areas of the squares built on the other two legs:
a² + b² = c²      (or Base² + Perpendicular² = Hypotenuse²) • Hypotenuse (c): The side opposite the 90° right angle. It is ALWAYS the longest side.

3. Bhāskara II's Celebrated Dissection Proof ("Behold!")

In the 12th century, Indian mathematician Bhāskara II in his treatise Bijaganita drew four identical right triangles (sides a, b, c) inside a square on the hypotenuse c and simply wrote: "Pashya!" (Behold!)

Area of large outer square = c²
Outer area = Area of 4 triangles + Area of central square
c² = 4 × (1/2 × a × b) + (b - a)²
c² = 2ab + (b² - 2ab + a²)
c² = a² + b²!

4. Generating Pythagorean Triplets

A set of three natural numbers (a, b, c) that satisfy a² + b² = c² is called a Pythagorean Triplet.

General Formula for Any Integer m > 1:
(2m, m² - 1, m² + 1) Check:
(2m)² + (m² - 1)² = 4m² + (m⁴ - 2m² + 1) = m⁴ + 2m² + 1 = (m² + 1)²!
• For m = 2: (4, 3, 5) ⇒ 3² + 4² = 5² (9 + 16 = 25).
• For m = 3: (6, 8, 10) ⇒ 6² + 8² = 10² (36 + 64 = 100).
• For m = 4: (8, 15, 17) ⇒ 8² + 15² = 17² (64 + 225 = 289).
Section 03

In-Text Activities & Classroom Explorations (Fully Solved)

Activity 1 (Page 8): The hypotenuse of an isosceles right triangle is 10 cm. Find the length of each of its equal sides.

Step 1: In an isosceles right triangle, both legs are equal: a = b.

Step 2: By theorem: a² + a² = c² ⇒ 2a² = 10² = 100.

Step 3: a² = 100 ÷ 2 = 50.

Step 4: a = √50 = √(25 × 2) = 5√2 cm ≈ 7.07 cm.

Result: Each side measures 5√2 cm.

Activity 2 (Page 16): Write a Pythagorean triplet whose smallest member is 6.

Using formula: (2m, m² - 1, m² + 1).

Let 2m = 6 ⇒ m = 3.

  • First member = 2m = 6.
  • Second member = m² - 1 = 3² - 1 = 9 - 1 = 8.
  • Third member = m² + 1 = 3² + 1 = 9 + 1 = 10.

Check: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10².

Result: The triplet is (6, 8, 10).

Section 04

Complete End-of-Chapter 'Figure it Out' Exercises (Solved Step-by-Step)

Exercise 1: A 13 m long ladder reaches a window 12 m high from the ground on placed against a wall. Find the distance of the foot of the ladder from the wall.
Identify: The ladder forms the hypotenuse c = 13 m. Height of window b = 12 m. Base distance = a.
Formula: a² + b² = c² ⇒ a² + 12² = 13².
Compute: a² + 144 = 169 ⇒ a² = 169 - 144 = 25.
Square Root: a = √25 = 5 m.
Result: The foot of the ladder is 5 metres away from the wall.
Exercise 2: The lengths of the sides of a triangle are 7 cm, 24 cm, and 25 cm. Determine whether it is a right-angled triangle.
Check Hypotenuse: Longest side = 25. 25² = 625.
Sum of squares of other two sides: 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625.
Compare: 7² + 24² = 25² (625 = 625).
Result: By the converse of the Baudhāyana-Pythagoras theorem, this IS a right-angled triangle.
Exercise 3: Find the perimeter of a rectangle whose length is 40 cm and diagonal is 41 cm.
Find Breadth: In right triangle: b² + 40² = 41² ⇒ b² + 1600 = 1681.
Solve b: b² = 1681 - 1600 = 81 ⇒ b = √81 = 9 cm.
Perimeter: 2 × (Length + Breadth) = 2 × (40 + 9) = 2 × 49 = 98 cm.
Result: Perimeter is 98 cm.

1. Which side of a right-angled triangle is the hypotenuse?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b).
The hypotenuse is the side opposite the 90° angle, and it is always the longest side in a right triangle.

2. What ancient Indian text first recorded the principle of doubling a square using its diagonal?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) Baudhāyana Śulba-Sūtras (c. 800 BCE).
The Sulba-Sutras provided precise geometric rope-construction methods for sacrificial altars.

3. Which of the following sets of numbers forms a Pythagorean Triplet?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a) (5, 12, 13).
5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13².

4. If one member of a Pythagorean triplet generated by (2m, m²-1, m²+1) is 14 (where 2m = 14), what is the largest member?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) 50.
2m = 14 ⇒ m = 7. Largest member = m² + 1 = 7² + 1 = 49 + 1 = 50. (The other is 7² - 1 = 48).

5. A boy walks 8 m due North and then 6 m due East. How far is he from his starting point?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) 10 m.
North and East are at 90°. Distance d = √(8² + 6²) = √(64 + 36) = √100 = 10 m.

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Pitfall: Applying a² + b² = c² to triangles that are NOT right-angled!
The Baudhāyana-Pythagoras theorem ONLY holds for right-angled triangles (where one angle is exactly 90°).
