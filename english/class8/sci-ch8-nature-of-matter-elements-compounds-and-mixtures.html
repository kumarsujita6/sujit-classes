8.1 What Are Mixtures?

Imagine making a tasty bowl of bhelpuri or fruit salad. You toss together puffed rice, roasted peanuts, sev, chopped onions, and lemon juice. Notice three critical facts about this bowl:

  1. No fixed recipe: You can add more peanuts or less lemon juice; it is still bhelpuri!
  2. Original tastes remain: The peanuts still taste nutty, and the lemon still tastes sour. No new taste or chemical is born.
  3. Easy separation: If you don't like peanuts, you can pick them out easily with your fingers!

This is exactly what a mixture is in science!

Teacher's Definition • Mixture:
A mixture consists of two or more substances (elements or compounds) physically mixed together in any proportion, where each component retains its own unique chemical properties and no chemical reaction takes place.

8.1.1 Is Air a Mixture?

Yes, air is a classic example of a mixture! It consists of several gases physically mixed together without reacting under normal conditions:

  • Nitrogen (~78%): An inert, unreactive gas that moderates burning.
  • Oxygen (~21%): Essential for respiration in animals and supports combustion.
  • Argon (~0.9%), Carbon Dioxide (~0.04%), Water Vapour, and Dust particles.

Proof that air is a mixture:

  1. The composition of air changes slightly from place to place (e.g., more water vapour near the Puri beach, more CO2 in busy city traffic).
  2. Gases in air retain their individual chemical properties (oxygen still supports fire; CO2 still turns limewater milky).
  3. Air can be separated into its individual liquid gases by physical methods (fractional distillation of liquid air).

8.1.2 Types of Mixtures: Uniform vs Non-Uniform

Mixtures are classified into two broad families depending on how thoroughly their particles are mixed:

Feature Uniform (Homogeneous) Mixture Non-Uniform (Heterogeneous) Mixture
Appearance Completely uniform throughout. Looks like a single uniform phase. Non-uniform. You can see different parts or layers with naked eyes or a lens.
Particle Distribution Components are evenly distributed down to the molecular level. Components are unevenly distributed in clumps or separate patches.
Boundary of Separation No visible boundary between components. Clear visible boundaries between different phases.
Everyday Examples Salt dissolved in water, sugar syrup, clean air, brass (copper + zinc), bronze. Muddy pond water, sand in water, oil and water, smoke, chalk in water.

8.2 & 8.3 What Are Pure Substances? Elements & Compounds

In everyday language, when a shopkeeper says "pure milk" or "pure ghee", they mean it has no adulteration or water added. But in chemistry, milk is NOT a pure substance—it is a complex mixture of water, fats, proteins, lactose, and minerals!

Scientific Definition • Pure Substance:
A substance that consists of only one type of particle throughout and has a definite, sharp chemical composition with fixed physical constants (like precise melting point and boiling point).
Pure substances are subdivided into two classes: Elements and Compounds.

1. Elements (The Fundamental Building Blocks)

An element is the purest, simplest form of matter that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by any chemical or physical reaction (such as heating, light, or electricity).

  • An element is made up of only one kind of atom. For example, a pure bar of 24-carat gold contains only gold (Au) atoms; pure iron contains only iron (Fe) atoms.
  • There are 118 known chemical elements (94 occur naturally on Earth, such as hydrogen, oxygen, carbon, iron, copper, sulfur).

2. Compounds (Chemical Marriage of Elements)

A compound is a pure substance formed when two or more different elements chemically combine in a fixed ratio by mass.

  • The elements lose their original identities completely, and the resulting compound has totally new chemical and physical properties!
  • A compound can only be broken down into its constituent elements by chemical reactions or electrical energy, never by physical methods (like filtering or magnets).

8.3.2 The Classic Showdown: Mixture of Fe + S vs Compound FeS

NCERT Activities 8.1, 8.4, and 8.5 present one of the most famous and illuminating experiments in all of chemistry:

Test / Property Mixture of Iron Filings & Sulfur Powder Compound: Iron Sulfide (FeS) [Heated Mixture]
Preparation 5.6 g iron filings and 3.2 g sulfur powder crushed and mixed cold without heating. The mixture is heated strongly in a hard glass test tube until it glows red hot with a black mass.
Visual Appearance Yellowish-grey powder. Grey iron particles and yellow sulfur specks are clearly visible. Uniform, dull black solid lump. No yellow sulfur or grey iron can be seen.
Action of a Bar Magnet All the grey iron filings get pulled and cling to the magnet, leaving yellow sulfur behind! Not attracted to the magnet! The iron has chemically bonded and lost its individual magnetism.
Action with Dilute Acid (HCl) Iron reacts to release Hydrogen gas (H2), which is colorless, odorless, and burns with a pop sound. Sulfur does not react. Releases Hydrogen Sulfide gas (H2S), which has a foul, suffocating smell of rotten eggs!
Chemical Equation Fe + S (Physical blend — no equation) Fe + S → FeS (Iron Sulfide)

8.3.3 Why Water (H2O) is a Compound, Not a Mixture

Consider the amazing transformation when hydrogen and oxygen combine to form water:

  • Hydrogen (H2): A highly combustible gas that burns with an explosive pop.
  • Oxygen (O2): A gas that violently supports combustion and makes fires rage.
  • Water (H2O): When these two gases combine chemically in a fixed 1:8 mass ratio (2:1 volume ratio), they form water — a cool, life-giving liquid that is used to extinguish fires!

This dramatic change of properties proves beyond doubt that water is a compound, not a mere mixture.

8.5 Minerals & Odisha's Ancient Dhokra Metalcraft

What Are Minerals?
Minerals are naturally occurring, solid inorganic substances found in the Earth's crust that have a definite chemical composition and a regular crystal structure (e.g., quartz, bauxite, haematite, native gold). Most minerals are compounds, but native metals like gold and platinum occur as pure elements!

Our Scientific Heritage • Dhokra Metalcraft of Odisha & Bihar:
For over 4,000 years (dating back to the Mohenjo-daro 'Dancing Girl'), traditional tribal artisans in Odisha and Bihar have practiced Dhokra art using the lost-wax casting technique (cire perdue):
  1. An artist crafts a detailed model using pure beeswax (organic compound).
  2. The wax model is coated with layers of clay paste and dried to form a hardened mould.
  3. The mould is heated in a furnace, causing the wax to melt and drain out through small channels.
  4. Molten brass or bronze (homogeneous metallic mixtures/alloys of copper, zinc, and tin) is poured into the hollow cavity.
  5. When cooled, the clay shell is cracked open to reveal a stunning, intricately detailed golden metal sculpture!

Section 4 • Step-by-Step NCERT Solutions

Complete Textbook 'Keep the Curiosity Alive' Exercises

Q1. Reaction A + B → C, where A and B cannot be broken down by chemical reactions. Which statement is correct? NCERT MCQ

Correct Option: (iv) A and B are elements, C is a compound, and has a fixed composition.

Teacher's Justification:

  • By definition, substances that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical reactions are elements. Therefore, A and B are elements.
  • When two elements chemically combine to form a new product C, C is a compound. Every chemical compound has a definite, fixed chemical composition by mass (Law of Definite Proportions).
Q2. Assertion: Air is a mixture. Reason: A mixture is formed when two or more substances mix without undergoing any chemical change. Assertion-Reason

Correct Option: (i) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Explanation: In air, nitrogen, oxygen, argon, and carbon dioxide coexist physically without reacting with one another. Each gas retains its chemical identity and can be separated by physical condensation and fractional distillation. Hence, the Reason correctly explains why air is a mixture.

Q3. Water, a compound, has different properties compared to those of the elements oxygen and hydrogen from which it is formed. Justify this statement. 3 Marks Core

Teacher's Point-Wise Justification:

  • State of Matter: Hydrogen and oxygen are both invisible gases at room temperature, whereas water is a liquid.
  • Combustion Behavior:
    • Hydrogen (H2) is highly inflammable and burns vigorously with a pop sound.
    • Oxygen (O2) is a supporter of combustion (fires cannot burn without oxygen).
    • Water (H2O) is neither combustible nor a supporter of combustion; in fact, it is widely used as a fire extinguisher!
  • Conclusion: When a compound is formed, chemical bonds rearrange the electrons, creating a totally new chemical substance whose properties are completely independent of its constituent elements.
Q4. In which of the following cases are all the examples correctly matched? Give reasons. Classification

Correctly Matched Case: (iii) Pure substances — carbon dioxide, iron, oxygen, sugar.

Reasons for each option:

  • Option (i) Incorrect: Lists water (compound) and air (mixture) under Elements. Only nitrogen and iron are elements.
  • Option (ii) Incorrect: Minerals can be heterogeneous rocks, and seawater contains suspended particles.
  • Option (iii) CORRECT: Carbon dioxide is a pure compound, iron is a pure element, oxygen is a pure element, and sugar (sucrose) is a pure compound. All consist of only one type of particle with fixed composition!
  • Option (iv) Incorrect: Lists air (uniform mixture) and brass (uniform alloy) under non-uniform mixtures.
Q5. Classify all substances involved: (a) Iron reacts with moist air to form iron oxide; (b) Magnesium burns in oxygen to form magnesium oxide. Reaction Analysis

Reaction (a): Iron + Moist Air → Iron Oxide (Rust)

  • Iron: Element — Pure metal composed of single Fe atoms.
  • Moist Air: Mixture — Gaseous mixture of nitrogen, oxygen, water vapour, and other gases.
  • Iron Oxide: Compound — Chemical substance formed by fixed chemical bonding of iron and oxygen atoms.

Reaction (b): Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium Oxide

  • Magnesium: Element — Pure metal composed of single Mg atoms.
  • Oxygen: Element — Pure non-metal composed of diatomic O2 molecules.
  • Magnesium Oxide: Compound — White ionic solid composed of magnesium and oxygen chemically combined in fixed ratio.

Q7. What new substance is formed when a mixture of iron filings and sulfur powder is heated? Write the word equation. Experiment

New Substance Formed: Iron Sulfide (FeS)

Word Equation:
Iron + Sulfur —(Heat)→ Iron Sulfide

How it differs from the original mixture:

  1. Appearance: The original mixture is a loose yellowish-grey powder. The new compound is a hard, uniform black solid mass.
  2. Magnetic Behavior: In the mixture, iron filings are easily pulled away by a bar magnet. In the iron sulfide compound, the iron has chemically reacted and is not attracted by a magnet.
  3. Chemical Behavior: The mixture reacts with dilute HCl to release odorless hydrogen gas. Iron sulfide reacts with dilute HCl to release toxic hydrogen sulfide gas with a rotten egg smell.
Q8. Is it possible for a substance to be classified as both an element and a compound? Explain why or why not. Conceptual Logic

Answer: NO, it is strictly IMPOSSIBLE.

Reasoning:

  • Elements and compounds have mutually exclusive scientific definitions.
  • An element contains only one kind of atom and cannot be split into simpler chemical components.
  • A compound must contain two or more different kinds of atoms chemically bonded together.
  • A substance cannot simultaneously consist of only one type of atom and multiple different types of atoms. Therefore, a substance can never be both an element and a compound.
Q9. How would our daily lives be changed if water were not a compound but a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen? HOTS Thought Experiment

Catastrophic Consequences:

  1. No Liquid Water on Earth: A mixture of hydrogen and oxygen gases at room temperature would remain an invisible, explosive gas! There would be no oceans, rivers, rain, or drinking water.
  2. Violent Explosions Everywhere: Hydrogen and oxygen gas mixtures ignite violently in the presence of a single spark or static shock, creating catastrophic explosions.
  3. Extinction of All Life: Living cells are made of over 70% liquid water. Without the solvent and biological medium of liquid water, life could never have originated or survived on Earth!
Q10. Analyse Fig. 8.24 (Iron filings + Dilute Hydrochloric Acid). Identify Gas A and write the word equation. NCERT Fig 8.24

Identification of Gas (A): Hydrogen Gas (H2)

Word Equation:
Iron + Dilute Hydrochloric Acid → Iron Chloride (Ferrous Chloride) + Hydrogen Gas

Confirmation Test: When a burning splinter or matchstick is brought near the mouth of the delivery tube, the escaping gas burns with a characteristic 'pop' sound, proving it is hydrogen gas.

Q11. Write the names of two compounds made only from non-metals, and mention two uses of each. Application

Compound 1: Carbon Dioxide (CO2) [Formed from non-metals Carbon and Oxygen]

  • Use 1: Used by green plants during photosynthesis to manufacture food for the entire planet.
  • Use 2: Used in fire extinguishers because it is denser than air and blankets flames, cutting off oxygen.

Compound 2: Water (H2O) [Formed from non-metals Hydrogen and Oxygen]

  • Use 1: Essential for drinking, biological digestion, blood circulation, and hydration in living organisms.
  • Use 2: Acts as the universal solvent in cooking, agriculture, and industrial chemical processing.
Q12. How can gold be classified as both a mineral and a metal? Dual Classification

Teacher's Clear Explanation:

  • Why Gold is a Mineral: A mineral is defined geologically as a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a definite crystalline structure. Gold occurs naturally in veins and riverbeds of the Earth's crust as a pure native substance without human intervention. Hence, it meets every geological criterion of a mineral.
  • Why Gold is a Metal: From a chemical and physical perspective, gold possesses high metallic luster (shine), high density, excellent thermal and electrical conductivity, and is the most malleable (can be beaten into ultra-thin foil) and ductile (can be drawn into microscopic thin wires) substance known. Hence, it is a metal.

Section 6 • Exam Traps & Key Takeaways

Common Board Exam Traps & Memory Shortcuts

Critical tips so you avoid frequent misconceptions on test day.

Exam Trap #1: 'Is tap water a pure substance?'
Mistake: Writing "Yes, because it looks transparent and clean."
Full-Marks Correction: Tap water is a homogeneous mixture, not a pure substance! It contains dissolved minerals (calcium, magnesium, chloride salts) and gases. Only pure distilled water (H2O) is a chemical compound and pure substance.
Exam Trap #2: 'Can an element be decomposed by heat or electricity?'
Mistake: Confusing compounds (like water splitting into H2 and O2) with elements.
Full-Marks Correction: An element CANNOT be broken down into simpler substances by any chemical or physical reaction. Only compounds can be decomposed!
Teacher's Memory Hack • MIX vs CHEM Rule:
• M-I-X-T-U-R-E: Many ingredients, Individual identities kept, X-ternal (physical) separation possible!
• C-O-M-P-O-U-N-D: Chemically combined, Original properties lost, Unified fixed mass ratio, New substance born!
