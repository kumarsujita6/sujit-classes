Clear, step-by-step physics and chemistry explained with relatable everyday analogies.

9.1 What Are Solute, Solvent, and Solution?

Think about making a refreshing glass of lemonade (Shikanji) on a hot summer afternoon. You add a spoonful of sugar and a pinch of salt to a glass of water and stir it. Soon, the white granules vanish completely, and you have a clear, sweet liquid.

  • Solute: The substance that gets dissolved. It is usually present in the smaller quantity (e.g., sugar, common salt).
  • Solvent: The liquid medium in which the solute dissolves. It is usually present in the larger quantity (e.g., water).
  • Solution: The homogeneous (uniform) mixture formed when the solute dissolves completely in the solvent.
Teacher's Rule • Solutions of Two Liquids:
When two liquids mix (like milk and water, or alcohol and water), which one is the solute?
The liquid present in lesser quantity is called the solute, and the liquid present in greater quantity is called the solvent. If water is present, water is almost always recognized as the solvent because of its extraordinary dissolving power (Universal Solvent).

9.2 Saturated vs Unsaturated Solutions & Solubility

Can water dissolve an unlimited amount of sugar? Let's investigate:

  • You take 100 mL of water at room temperature (25 °C) and add 1 teaspoon of sugar. It dissolves easily. The solution is unsaturated (it has room for more solute).
  • You add a second, third, and fourth spoon. They dissolve with stirring.
  • By the fifth spoon, no matter how vigorously you stir, sugar granules begin to settle at the bottom of the beaker! The solution has reached its absolute capacity.
Key Definitions • Saturated Solution & Solubility:
Saturated Solution: A solution in which the maximum possible amount of solute has dissolved at a given temperature, such that no more solute can dissolve.
Unsaturated Solution: A solution that contains less than the maximum amount of solute, meaning more solute can still dissolve at that temperature.
Solubility: The maximum mass of a solute (in grams) that can dissolve in a fixed quantity (typically 100 g or 100 mL) of solvent at a specific temperature.

9.2.1 How Temperature Changes Solubility

What happens if you take that saturated sugar solution with undissolved crystals at the bottom and place it on a burner?

  • For Solids in Liquids (e.g., Sugar, Salt, Copper Sulphate): As temperature rises, thermal energy increases, expanding the interparticle spaces between liquid molecules. The undissolved sugar quickly dissolves! Solubility of solids increases as temperature increases.
  • For Gases in Liquids (e.g., Oxygen, Carbon Dioxide in Water): The exact opposite happens! When a liquid is heated, dissolved gas particles gain kinetic energy and escape into the air. Solubility of gases DECREASES as temperature increases.
Why Do Fish Prefer Cold Water Streams?
Because cold water can dissolve significantly more oxygen than warm water! During hot summer months, the temperature of shallow ponds rises, decreasing dissolved oxygen levels and causing aquatic organisms to struggle to breathe.

9.4 & 9.5 Mass, Volume, and Density

Why does a tiny steel pin sink straight to the bottom of a bucket, while a massive 50,000-ton aircraft carrier made of steel floats proudly on the ocean? The secret is density!

Scientific Formula • Density:
Density is the mass of a substance per unit volume. It measures how tightly matter is packed inside an object.
Density (d) = Mass (m) ÷ Volume (V)
• SI Unit: kg/m³ (kilogram per cubic metre)
• Common Laboratory Unit: g/cm³ (gram per cubic centimetre) or g/mL
Conversion: 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Pure water has a density of 1.0 g/cm³ at 4 °C.

9.5.1 The Water Displacement Method

How do we measure the volume of an irregular stone or sculpture that cannot be measured with a ruler? We use the ingenious method discovered by ancient Greek scientist Archimedes:

  1. Fill a graduated measuring cylinder partially with water and record the initial level: V₁ = 50 mL.
  2. Tie the irregular stone to a thin cotton thread and gently lower it completely into the water.
  3. The submerged stone pushes aside (displaces) an amount of water exactly equal to its own volume!
  4. Record the new, higher water level: V₂ = 72 mL.
  5. Volume of Stone: V = V₂ - V₁ = 72 - 50 = 22 mL = 22 cm³.
  6. Measure the stone's mass on a digital balance: m = 59.4 g.
  7. Density: d = 59.4 ÷ 22 = 2.7 g/cm³ (which matches aluminium/granite stone!).

9.5.2 The Floating Egg & The Surprising Orange Puzzle

The Orange Puzzle (NCERT Fig 9.25)

Drop a fresh unpeeled orange into a bowl of water: it floats like a buoyant little boat! Now peel the orange skin off completely and drop the bare orange slices back into the same water: it sinks like a stone to the bottom!

Why? The peel (rind) of the orange is filled with millions of microscopic air pockets. These air pockets dramatically inflate the total volume of the unpeeled orange without adding much weight. This lowers the orange's average overall density below that of water (1.0 g/cm³), allowing it to float. Peeling removes the air-filled lifejacket, leaving only dense fruit flesh that is denser than water!

The Magic Egg in Salt Water (NCERT Fig 9.24)

A raw whole egg placed in pure tap water sinks immediately because the egg is denser than fresh water (density of egg ≈ 1.08 g/cm³ > 1.00 g/cm³).

How to make it float? Dissolve 3 to 4 tablespoons of common salt into the water! Adding salt packs heavy sodium and chloride ions into the water, raising the liquid's density to 1.15 g/cm³ (dense brine). Now the water is denser than the egg, pushing the egg up to float on top!

9.6 Indian Heritage: Ningel Salt Wells of Manipur

In the picturesque village of Ningel in Manipur's Thoubal district, villagers still harvest natural salt from ancient brine wells. One famous well is lined with an intact 100-year-old hollowed tree trunk driven deep into saline aquifers.

  • Local women collect the saturated salt brine and boil it in huge shallow iron pans over firewood kilns.
  • As the water evaporates, pure salt crystals crystallize out.
  • The hot salt is shaped into smooth circular 'salt cakes' using banana leaves and wrapped in traditional handwoven Phanek textiles. These mineral-rich salt cakes have been used for centuries in traditional cuisine and indigenous medicine.

Section 4 • Step-by-Step NCERT Solutions

Complete Textbook 'Keep the Curiosity Alive' Exercises

Unabridged solutions for Questions 1 to 12 with full mathematical calculations and justifications.

Q1. State whether True or False. Correct the false statement(s). True / False

(i) Oxygen gas is more soluble in hot water rather than in cold water.
FALSE — Correction: The solubility of gases in liquids decreases with rising temperature. Oxygen gas is much more soluble in cold water than in hot water.

(ii) A mixture of sand and water is a solution.
FALSE — Correction: Sand does not dissolve in water; it forms an insoluble heterogeneous suspension, not a homogeneous solution.

(iii) The amount of space occupied by any object is called its mass.
FALSE — Correction: The space occupied by an object is called its volume. Mass is the amount of matter contained in it.

(iv) An unsaturated solution has more solute dissolved than a saturated solution.
FALSE — Correction: A saturated solution contains the maximum possible dissolved solute at that temperature; an unsaturated solution contains less solute.

(v) The presence of different gases in the atmosphere is also a uniform mixture.
TRUE — Clean atmospheric air is a homogeneous (uniform) gaseous solution of nitrogen, oxygen, argon, and trace gases.

Q2. Fill in the blanks. Fill in Blanks
  1. The volume of a solid can be measured by the method of displacement, where the solid is immersed / submerged in water and the rise / increase in water level is measured.
  2. The maximum amount of solute dissolved in a fixed amount of solvent (100 mL or 100 g) at a particular temperature is called solubility at that temperature.
  3. Generally, the density decreases with increase in temperature.
  4. The solution in which glucose has completely dissolved in water, and no more glucose can dissolve at a given temperature, is called a saturated solution of glucose.
Q3. You pour oil into a glass containing some water. The oil floats on top. What does this tell you? NCERT MCQ

Correct Option: (ii) Water is denser than oil (or oil is less dense than water).

Explanation: Any substance with a lower density than the surrounding liquid floats on top. Cooking oil has a density of approximately 0.90 g/cm³, which is less than water (1.00 g/cm³); therefore, oil floats on water.

Q4. A stone sculpture weighs 225 g and has a volume of 90 cm³. Calculate its density and predict whether it will float or sink in water. Calculation

Step-by-Step Calculation:

  • Given Mass (m): 225 g
  • Given Volume (V): 90 cm³
  • Formula: Density (d) = Mass ÷ Volume
  • Calculation: d = 225 ÷ 90 = 2.5 g/cm³

Prediction: The density of pure water is 1.0 g/cm³. Since the sculpture's density (2.5 g/cm³) is significantly greater than the density of water (2.5 > 1.0), the stone sculpture will SINK in water.

Q5. Which one of the statements is the most appropriate, and why are the others not appropriate? Conceptual Logic

Most Appropriate Statement: (iii) No more solute can be dissolved into the saturated solution at that temperature.

Critical evaluation of other statements:

  • Statement (i) is false: A saturated solution cannot dissolve more solute at that fixed temperature.
  • Statement (ii) is false: It describes a saturated solution, not an unsaturated solution.
  • Statement (iv) is false: Saturated solutions can be prepared at any temperature (low, room, or high).
Q6. You have a bottle with a volume of 2 litres. You pour 500 mL of water into it. How much more water can the bottle hold? Word Problem

Calculation:

  • Total capacity of bottle = 2 litres = 2 × 1000 mL = 2000 mL.
  • Current volume of water inside = 500 mL.
  • Remaining volume = 2000 mL - 500 mL = 1500 mL (or 1.5 litres).

The bottle can hold 1500 mL (1.5 L) more water.

Q7. An object has a mass of 400 g and a volume of 40 cm³. What is its density? Calculation

Formula: Density (d) = Mass (m) ÷ Volume (V)

Calculation: d = 400 g ÷ 40 cm³ = 10 g/cm³.

The density of the object is 10 g/cm³ (equivalent to 10,000 kg/m³, characteristic of silver metal).

Q8. Analyse Fig. 9.25a and 9.25b. Why does the unpeeled orange float, while the peeled one sinks? Explain. NCERT Fig 9.25

Teacher's Scientific Explanation:

  • Unpeeled Orange: The outer skin (rind) of an orange is thick, spongy, and filled with millions of microscopic air pockets. Even though the orange has slightly more mass with the skin on, the air pockets create a huge increase in total volume. This makes the average density of the whole unpeeled orange less than 1.0 g/cm³, so it floats easily!
  • Peeled Orange: Removing the peel strips away all those trapped air pockets. The remaining fruit flesh consists mostly of dense water, citric acid, and sugars without trapped air. Its density is slightly higher than 1.0 g/cm³, causing it to sink to the bottom.
Q9. Object A has mass 200 g and volume 40 cm³. Object B has mass 240 g and volume 60 cm³. Which object is denser? Comparative Math

Step-by-Step Comparison:

  • Density of Object A: dₐ = 200 g ÷ 40 cm³ = 5.0 g/cm³
  • Density of Object B: dṎ = 240 g ÷ 60 cm³ = 4.0 g/cm³

Conclusion: Since 5.0 g/cm³ > 4.0 g/cm³, Object A is denser than Object B.

Q10. Reema has modeling clay of mass 120 g. First moulded into a cube of volume 60 cm³, then flattened into a thin sheet. Predict what will happen to its density. Conceptual Trap

Prediction: The density will REMAIN EXACTLY THE SAME (UNCHANGED at 2.0 g/cm³)!

Scientific Reason:

  • Mass remains 120 g (no clay was added or removed).
  • Volume remains 60 cm³ (changing the physical shape from a 3D block to a flat sheet does not change the amount of space the clay occupies).
  • Since both mass and volume remain constant, Density = 120 g ÷ 60 cm³ = 2.0 g/cm³ remains completely unchanged. Density is an intrinsic material property that does not depend on shape.
Q11. A block of iron has a mass of 600 g and a density of 7.9 g/cm³. What is its volume? Calculation

Rearranging Formula: Volume (V) = Mass (m) ÷ Density (d)

Calculation: V = 600 g ÷ 7.9 g/cm³ ≈ 75.95 cm³ (or ~76.0 cm³).

The volume of the iron block is 75.95 cm³.

Q12. Experimental setup in Fig. 9.26: Test tube with glass tube placed in hot water (~70 °C). Water level rises. How does it affect density? NCERT Fig 9.26

Answer: The density of the water inside the test tube DECREASES.

Scientific Explanation:

  • When the test tube is heated by hot water, the water inside absorbs thermal energy. Its particles vibrate and move farther apart, causing thermal expansion.
  • Because the liquid expands, it pushes upward into the narrow glass tube, indicating an increase in total Volume (V).
  • The Mass (m) of water inside remains unchanged (no water entered or escaped).
  • From the formula Density = Mass ÷ Volume, as Volume increases while Mass stays constant, the density must decrease. This explains why hot water is less dense and rises above cold water!

1. Why does ice float on liquid water, unlike almost every other solid that sinks in its own liquid?

Teacher's Insight
Correct Answer: (b) When water freezes at 0 °C, hydrogen bonding locks water molecules into an open hexagonal crystalline cage that occupies ~9% more volume than liquid water, lowering its density to 0.92 g/cm³.

2. A student adds 40 grams of salt to 100 mL of water at 20 °C. Only 36 grams dissolve, and 4 grams settle at the bottom. The solution is:

Teacher's Insight
Correct Answer: (c) Because the water has dissolved its absolute maximum capacity of salt (36 g) and cannot dissolve the remaining 4 g at that temperature, it is a saturated solution.

3. When opening a chilled soda bottle, vigorous effervescence (bubbles) is observed because:

Teacher's Insight
Correct Answer: (a) Carbon dioxide is dissolved in soda under high pressure. When opened, pressure drops to atmospheric level; gas solubility drops sharply, and excess CO2 rushes out as fizzing bubbles.

4. A solid block has mass 500 g and volume 600 cm³. When dropped into a pond of fresh water (density 1.0 g/cm³), the block will:

Teacher's Insight
Correct Answer: (a) Density = 500 g / 600 cm³ = 0.83 g/cm³. Since 0.83 < 1.0, the block is lighter than water and will float!

5. Why can humans float effortlessly on the surface of the Dead Sea without swimming?

Teacher's Insight
Correct Answer: (d) High mineral and salt saturation increases the liquid density of the Dead Sea to ~1.24 g/cm³. Since the human body has an average density of ~0.98 g/cm³, swimmers cannot sink!
Section 6 • Exam Traps & Key Takeaways

Board Exam Traps, Common Mistakes & Memory Shortcuts

Master these golden rules to avoid losing easy marks in calculations and reasoning.

Exam Trap #1: 'Does heavy always mean sinking?'
Mistake: Thinking a 5,000 kg wooden log will sink because it is "so heavy."
Full-Marks Correction: Floating or sinking depends on density (mass per unit volume), NOT total mass alone! A 5,000 kg log occupies a massive volume such that its density is ~0.6 g/cm³ (< 1.0), so it floats effortlessly. Conversely, a 1-gram steel pin sinks because its density is ~7.9 g/cm³ (> 1.0).
Exam Trap #2: 'Temperature vs Gas Solubility'
Mistake: Writing that "heating makes gases dissolve more."
Full-Marks Correction: Heating drives gases OUT of solution! Gas solubility decreases with increasing temperature because heat increases particle kinetic energy, allowing gas molecules to break free into the air.
Teacher's Memory Triangle • The D-M-V Formula Trick:
Draw a triangle with M on top, and D and V at the bottom:
• Cover D → D = M ÷ V
• Cover M → M = D × V
• Cover V → V = M ÷ D
