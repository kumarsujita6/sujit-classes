The 6 Architectural Pillars of Chapter 7

Every concept in Chapter 7 connects directly into a unified conceptual framework. Explore each pillar below before diving into line-by-line theory:

1. Ratios & Simplest Forms

A ratio a : b compares two quantities of the same kind by division. The terms a (antecedent) and b (consequent) are reduced to simplest form by dividing by their HCF:

Simplest Form: a/HCF(a, b) : b/HCF(a, b)

Critical Insight: Ratios are pure dimensionless numbers. Adding a constant to both terms breaks the ratio!

2. The Law of Proportion

Two ratios are in proportion (a : b :: c : d) if they represent the same numerical value. By cross-multiplication, the fundamental invariance law states:

Product of Extremes = Product of Means ⇒ a × d = b × c

Any unknown fourth term can be computed directly as d = (b × c) / a.

3. Ancient Indian Trairāśika

Formalized by Āryabhaṭa I (499 CE) and Bhāskara II in Līlāvatī, the Rule of Three links three known quantities to find the desired yield:

Ichchhāphala = (Phala × Ichchhā) ÷ Pramāṇa

Solves commercial trading, saffron currency exchange, midday meal food allocation, and bricklaying.

4. Partitioning & Sharing (m : n)

To divide a quantity x in the ratio m : n, split the whole into (m + n) equal groups. The size of one group is x / (m + n):

Part 1 = m × [x / (m+n)]  |  Part 2 = n × [x / (m+n)]

Applied in partnership profits (Prashanti & Bhuvan), cement mixtures, and paint tinting.

5. Unit Conversions & Standards

Quantities must share identical units before proportional comparison. Key standard factors:

1 acre = 43,560 sq ft  |  1 L = 1,000 mL = 1,000 cc

Temperature: °F = (9/5)°C + 32 and °C = (5/9)(°F - 32).

6. Non-Proportional Boundaries

Not all relationships are directly proportional! Recognizing when proportional reasoning fails prevents critical calculation errors:

Speed × Time = Constant  (Inverse Proportion)

Market pricing (shampoo bottles vs sachets) is influenced by packaging, marketing, and volume tiers.

What is an Aspect Ratio?

The aspect ratio of a two-dimensional shape is the numerical relationship between its width (horizontal measurement) and height (vertical measurement), expressed as:

Aspect Ratio = Width : Height = Width ÷ Height

Two rectangular shapes are similar if and only if their aspect ratios are mathematically equivalent. When you scale an image by multiplying both the width and height by the same positive factor, the aspect ratio remains unchanged, and no distortion occurs.

2.2 What is a Ratio? Conceptual Mechanics

In mathematics, we compare two quantities using two fundamental methods:

  1. Comparison by Subtraction (Difference): Tells us how much more or how much less one quantity is compared to another. For example, if Ananya has 10 pencils and Rohit has 6 pencils, Ananya has 10 - 6 = 4 more pencils.
  2. Comparison by Division (Ratio): Tells us how many times one quantity contains or is contained in another. Using the same example, the ratio of Ananya's pencils to Rohit's pencils is 10 ÷ 6 = 5/3 or 5 : 3.
Formal Definition of a Ratio

A ratio is a mathematical comparison of two quantities of the same kind and expressed in the same units, obtained by dividing the first quantity by the second.

The ratio of a quantity a to a quantity b is written as a : b (read as "a is to b") or as a fraction a/b, where b ≠ 0.

In the ratio a : b:

  • a is called the First Term or Antecedent (ପୂର୍ବପଦ).
  • b is called the Second Term or Consequent (ଉତ୍ତରପଦ).
Three Non-Negotiable Rules of Ratios
  • Same Kind & Unit: We cannot form a ratio between 5 apples and 3 rupees, nor between 2 metres and 500 grams. To compare 2 metres and 50 centimetres, we must first convert metres to centimetres (200 cm : 50 cm = 4 : 1).
  • Order Matters: The ratio a : b is completely different from b : a unless a = b. For example, 3 : 2 (1.5) is not the same as 2 : 3 (0.67).
  • No Units: Because a ratio is a fraction of two quantities with the same units, the units cancel out completely! A ratio is a pure, dimensionless number.

2.3 Equivalent Ratios & Simplest Form

Just like equivalent fractions, we obtain equivalent ratios by multiplying or dividing both terms of the ratio by the same non-zero number:

a : b = (a × k) : (b × k)   and   a : b = (a ÷ k) : (b ÷ k)   (for any k ≠ 0)

Simplest Form (Lowest Terms): A ratio a : b is in its simplest form if the integers a and b are co-prime, meaning their Highest Common Factor (HCF / ଗ.ସା.ଗୁ.) is 1.

Teacher Memory Trick: The HCF Reduction Shortcut

To reduce any large ratio a : b to simplest form in one single step, compute HCF(a, b) and divide both terms by it!

Example: Reduce 72 : 96.
HCF(72, 96) = 24.
Dividing both by 24: (72 ÷ 24) : (96 ÷ 24) = 3 : 4.

The Critical Age Problem: Why Adding Breaks Proportion

One of the most dangerous blunders students make is assuming that adding or subtracting the same number from both terms preserves the ratio. It does not!

Let us examine Example 6 from the NCERT textbook:

Scenario: When Neelima was 3 years old, her mother's age was 10 times her age (30 years).

• Initial Ratio (Neelima : Mother) = 3 : 30 = 1 : 10.

Now, fast-forward 9 years later, when Neelima is 12 years old. Her mother is now 30 + 9 = 39 years old.

• New Ratio = 12 : 39 = 4 : 13.

Notice that 1 : 10 ≠ 4 : 13! Even though exactly 9 years were added to both ages, the ratio changed dramatically. Proportionality is preserved ONLY by multiplication or division by a factor, NEVER by addition or subtraction.

2.4 Proportion & Cross-Multiplication Law

When two ratios are equivalent in their simplest forms, we say they are in proportion or proportional.

We write this using the double-colon symbol '::' (or the equals sign '='):

a : b :: c : d   ⟺   a/b = c/d

(Read as: "a is to b as c is to d")

In the proportion a : b :: c : d:

  • a and d are the outer terms, called the Extremes (ପ୍ରାନ୍ତ ପଦ).
  • b and c are the middle terms, called the Means (ମଧ୍ୟ ପଦ).
The Fundamental Law of Proportion (Cross-Multiplication)

For any four quantities to be in proportion, the product of the extremes MUST equal the product of the means:

Product of Extremes = Product of Means  ⇒  a × d = b × c

Rigorous Proof:
1. If a : b :: c : d, then by definition a / b = c / d.
2. Multiply both sides of the equation by the common denominator (b × d):
    (a / b) × (b × d) = (c / d) × (b × d)
3. Cancelling b on the left and d on the right gives:
    a × d = b × c (Proved).

Pramāṇa (a): Known Standard Input   |   Phala (b): Known Standard Output • Ichchhā (c): Desired Inquiry Input   |   Ichchhāphala (d): Desired Unknown Result = (Phala × Ichchhā) ÷ Pramāṇa

2.5 Ancient Indian Trairāśika: The Rule of Three

The principle of proportionality has deep roots in ancient Indian mathematics. Long before modern algebraic notation was developed in Europe, Indian mathematicians mastered proportional problem-solving under the name Trairāśika (ତ୍ରୈରାଶିକ, literally "Rule of Three Quantities").

Master mathematicians such as Āryabhaṭa I (born 476 CE; composed the Āryabhaṭīya in 499 CE), Brahmagupta (628 CE; Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta), and Bhāskarāchārya II (1150 CE; Līlāvatī) defined the algorithm systematically:

Āryabhaṭa's Trairāśika Verse (Āryabhaṭīya, Gaṇitapāda 26)

"त्रैराशिक-फल-राशिं तमथेच्छया संहते कृत्वा ।
लब्धं प्रमाण-भजितं तस्मादिच्छा-फलं भवति ॥"

Translation: In the Rule of Three, multiply the Phala (fruit) by the Ichchhā (requisition), and divide the resulting product by the Pramāṇa (measure). The result obtained is the Ichchhāphala (yield).

Ichchhāphala = (Phala × Ichchhā) ÷ Pramāṇa

Let us map the Sanskrit terms to modern variables:

Sanskrit Term Literal English Meaning Mathematical Role Modern Variable
Pramāṇa (ପ୍ରମାଣ) Measure / Benchmark The standard known quantity or input a
Phala (ଫଳ) Fruit / Produce The known result or output produced by Pramāṇa b
Ichchhā (ଇଚ୍ଛା) Desire / Requisition The new query quantity or requirement c
Ichchhāphala (ଇଚ୍ଛାଫଳ) Desired Yield / Desired Fruit The unknown quantity to be found d = x

2.6 Sharing, but Not Equally! (Partitioning a Whole into m : n)

In daily life, resources, expenses, and profits are frequently shared in unequal proportions. For example:

  • Two business partners divide monthly profits in proportion to their initial capital investments.
  • A mason mixes cement, sand, and gravel in fixed ratios (e.g., 1 : 2 : 4) to prepare strong concrete.
  • A chef mixes rice and urad dal in the ratio 2 : 1 to prepare soft idli batter.
Textbook Counter Activity (Section 7.5)

Suppose you and your friend have 12 counters:

  • Equal Share: Each gets 6 counters. The ratio is 6 : 6, which simplifies to 1 : 1.
  • Partner gets 5: You get 12 - 5 = 7 counters. The ratio is 5 : 7.
  • Share in Ratio 3 : 1:
    • Round 1: Partner takes 3, you take 1 (4 taken, 8 left).
    • Round 2: Partner takes 3, you take 1 (4 taken, 4 left).
    • Round 3: Partner takes 3, you take 1 (4 taken, 0 left).
    Partner gets 3 + 3 + 3 = 9 counters, you get 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 counters.

What if you have to share 42 counters in the ratio 4 : 3? Repeating physical rounds would take forever! Instead, we use the Unit Group Method:

m + n (In this case, 4 + 3 = 7 parts). 2. Value of 1 Unit Part = Total Quantity ÷ (m + n) ⇒ 42 ÷ 7 = 6. 3. First Share = m × [x / (m + n)] = 4 × 6 = 24  |  Second Share = n × [x / (m + n)] = 3 × 6 = 18. Sum = 24 + 18 = 42 ✓.

2.7 Boundaries of Proportional Reasoning: When Rule of Three Fails!

A key objective of the NEP 2020 Ganita Prakash curriculum is developing critical thinking. Not every real-world phenomenon is directly proportional. Students must recognize when proportional models apply and when they do not.

Case 1: Inverse Variation (Speed vs Time)

Consider Puneeth's Father's Motorcycle Journey (NCERT Page 171):
Puneeth's father rode from Lucknow to Kanpur at 50 km/h and took 2 hours.
If he increases his speed to 75 km/h, will he take more time or less time?

  • If a student blindly writes: 50 : 2 :: 75 : x ⇒ x = (2 × 75) / 50 = 3 hours, they have committed a fatal logic error!
  • Riding faster must decrease the travel time, not increase it!
  • Here, Distance = Speed × Time = Constant (50 × 2 = 100 km).
  • At 75 km/h, the time is 100 ÷ 75 = 4/3 hours = 1 hour 20 minutes (80 minutes).
  • This is Inverse Proportion (ବ୍ୟସ୍ତ ସମାନୁପାତ), not Direct Proportion.

Case 2: Real-World Commercial Packaging (The Shampoo Mystery)

In Activity 2 (NCERT Page 171), students collect prices of different sizes of shampoo containers:

Container Size Volume (mL) Retail Price (₹) Unit Cost (₹ per mL) Proportionality Status
Sachet 6 mL ₹2 ₹0.33 / mL Baseline low-cost entry sachet
Small Bottle 180 mL ₹154 ₹0.86 / mL 2.6× more expensive per mL!
Medium Bottle 340 mL ₹276 ₹0.81 / mL Moderate volume discount
Large Bottle 1,000 mL ₹540 ₹0.54 / mL Best bottle value (economy of scale)

Why are prices not proportional to volumes?
1. Sachet Penetration Pricing: Companies price small sachets cheaply to make the product accessible to rural and low-income buyers.
2. Packaging & Dispenser Overheads: Plastic pump bottles, molds, caps, and branded printing cost significantly more to manufacture than simple plastic pouches.
3. Ecological Footprint: Although sachets have a cheaper upfront price, millions of discarded non-biodegradable multi-layer sachets create catastrophic plastic pollution. Environmentally, large refill packs are far superior.

2.9 Mathematical Logic in Proportion: The Binairo (Takuzu) Grid

At the conclusion of Chapter 7 (NCERT Page 178), students are introduced to Binairo (also known worldwide as Takuzu). Binairo is a grid logic puzzle governed by proportional parity rules:

  • Rule 1 (Equal Balance): Every single row and every column must contain an equal number of horizontal (—) and vertical (|) marks. (In a 6×6 grid, exactly 3 horizontal and 3 vertical lines per row and column).
  • Rule 2 (No Triplets): No more than two identical marks can be placed consecutively in any row or column (e.g., three horizontal lines in a row — — — is strictly forbidden).
  • Rule 3 (Uniqueness): All completed rows must be completely unique, and all completed columns must be completely unique.

Example 1: Are the ratios 3 : 4 and 72 : 96 proportional? (NCERT Page 162)
Step 1: Given Information & Goal

Two ratios are given: Ratio 1 = 3 : 4 and Ratio 2 = 72 : 96. We must determine whether these two ratios are in proportion.

Step 2: Proportionality Criterion

Two ratios are proportional if and only if their simplest forms are identical, or equivalently, if the product of extremes equals the product of means (a × d = b × c).

Step 3: Step-by-Step Simplification & Calculation

• Ratio 1: 3 : 4 is already in simplest form since HCF(3, 4) = 1.
• Ratio 2: To simplify 72 : 96, find the HCF of 72 and 96:
  Prime factors of 72 = 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9
  Prime factors of 96 = 2⁵ × 3¹ = 32 × 3
  HCF(72, 96) = 2³ × 3¹ = 24.
Dividing both terms by 24:
  72 ÷ 24 = 3
  96 ÷ 24 = 4
  Simplest Form of 72 : 96 = 3 : 4.

Step 4: Alternative Cross-Product Verification

Let a = 3, b = 4, c = 72, d = 96.
Extremes product = a × d = 3 × 96 = 288.
Means product = b × c = 4 × 72 = 288.
Since a × d = b × c = 288, the ratios are strictly proportional!

Step 5: Final Concluding Answer

Yes, the ratios 3 : 4 and 72 : 96 are proportional. We write: 3 : 4 :: 72 : 96.

Farmer Sanatan owns a 4-acre organic brinjal and tomato plantation in Mayurbhanj. Under the PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana, he installed a solar-powered drip irrigation system with calibrated drippers. The system delivers precisely 4 litres of water per hour per plant emitter. Sanatan mixes liquid organic bio-NPK fertilizer with water in a fertigation tank in the ratio 2 : 25.

Sub-question 1.1 (1M): If 500 emitters run continuously for 3.5 hours, calculate the total volume of water discharged in Litres.

Solution:
Total Discharge = 500 emitters × 4 L/h × 3.5 h = 7,000 Litres.

Sub-question 1.2 (1M): To prepare a fertigation solution containing 12.5 Litres of bio-NPK fertilizer in the ratio 2 : 25, how much water must Sanatan add?

Solution:
Bio-NPK : Water :: 2 : 25 ⇒ 12.5 : W :: 2 : 25
W = (12.5 × 25) ÷ 2 = 312.5 ÷ 2 = 156.25 Litres of water.

Sub-question 1.3 (1.5M): Sanatan's solar pump pumps water at a uniform rate of 2,400 litres per hour under direct sunlight. How long will the pump take to fill a 15,000-litre farm storage pond? Express answer in hours and minutes.

Solution:
Time = 15,000 ÷ 2,400 = 6.25 hours.
0.25 hours = 0.25 × 60 = 15 minutes.
Time = 6 hours 15 minutes.

Sub-question 1.4 (1.5M): Conventional flood irrigation previously consumed 28,000 litres per acre per week. The new drip system uses only 11,200 litres per acre per week. Calculate the ratio of water saved to conventional water used, and express the water conservation as a percentage.

Solution:
Water saved per acre = 28,000 - 11,200 = 16,800 Litres.
Ratio of Saved to Conventional = 16,800 : 28,000 = (168 ÷ 56) : (280 ÷ 56) = 3 : 5.
Percentage Conservation = (3/5) × 100% = 60% water conserved!

Case Study 2: Kalinga Metal Crafts & Bell Metal (Kansa) Casting (4 Sub-Questions)

Case Narrative: Heritage Bell Metal Utensil Casting in Balkati, Khordha

In the historic village of Balkati near Bhubaneswar, traditional coppersmiths (Kansari artisans) cast resonant Kansa (bell metal) dinner thalis and bowls. Standard bell metal is an alloy of copper and pure tin in the mass ratio 4 : 1. Raw copper ingots cost ₹920 per kg, while pure tin ingots cost ₹2,400 per kg. During crucible melting, 5% of the molten alloy is lost to oxidation slag.

Sub-question 2.1 (1M): An artisan casts a heavy ceremonial Kansa thali of final mass 3.2 kg. Find the mass of copper and tin contained in the finished thali.

Solution:
Total parts = 4 + 1 = 5 parts. Mass per part = 3.2 kg ÷ 5 = 0.64 kg.
• Copper = 4 × 0.64 kg = 2.56 kg (2,560 g).
• Tin = 1 × 0.64 kg = 0.64 kg (640 g).

Sub-question 2.2 (1M): Calculate the raw material cost of the metals in the 3.2 kg thali.

Solution:
Cost of Copper = 2.56 kg × ₹920 = ₹2,355.20.
Cost of Tin = 0.64 kg × ₹2,400 = ₹1,536.00.
Total Metal Cost = ₹2,355.20 + ₹1,536.00 = ₹3,891.20.

Sub-question 2.3 (1.5M): Because 5% of the total raw metal mass is lost as oxidation slag during furnace smelting, what total mass of raw metal ingots must be melted to obtain 3.2 kg of usable alloy?

Solution:
Let raw mass be M. Since 5% is lost, 95% remains:
0.95 × M = 3.2 kg ⇒ M = 3.2 ÷ 0.95 ≈ 3.368 kg.

Sub-question 2.4 (1.5M): The artisan adds a hand-carving labor fee that is proportional to the raw metal cost in the ratio 3 : 8. Calculate the artisan's labor fee and the final retail price of the carved thali.

Solution:
Labor Fee = (3/8) × Raw Metal Cost = (3/8) × ₹3,891.20 = 3 × ₹486.40 = ₹1,459.20.
Final Retail Price = Raw Cost + Labor = ₹3,891.20 + ₹1,459.20 = ₹5,350.40.

Part 5.6: Previous Years Questions (PYQs) & Most Probable Exam Questions

PYQ 1 (2M): Does the Ratio of Perimeter to Area of a Square Remain Constant?
Step 1: Formulas for Square

For a square of side length s: Perimeter P = 4s; Area A = s².

Step 2: Ratio Formation

Ratio P : A = (4s) / s² = 4 / s.

Step 3: Testing with Numerical Values

• For s = 2 cm: P = 8 cm, A = 4 cm² ⇒ Ratio = 8 : 4 = 2 : 1.
• For s = 4 cm: P = 16 cm, A = 16 cm² ⇒ Ratio = 16 : 16 = 1 : 1.
• For s = 8 cm: P = 32 cm, A = 64 cm² ⇒ Ratio = 32 : 64 = 1 : 2.

Step 4: Deduction

The ratio 4/s decreases as side length increases. It is not invariant.

Step 5: Final Concluding Answer

No, the ratio does NOT remain constant. Perimeter grows linearly (4s) while area grows quadratically (s²), so the ratio 4/s decreases as the square grows.

PYQ 2 (3M): Compound Trairāśika — Sambalpuri Fabric Dyeing
Step 1: Given Information & Variables

• 8 workers dye 240 m of fabric in 6 hours.
• Target: 14 workers working for 9 hours.
• Goal: Find total length of fabric dyed (L).

Step 2: Unitary Rate per Man-Hour

Total man-hours initially = 8 workers × 6 hours = 48 man-hours.
Dyeing rate = 240 m ÷ 48 man-hours = 5 metres per man-hour.

Step 3: Calculating for Target Group

Target man-hours = 14 workers × 9 hours = 126 man-hours.
Total fabric dyed = 126 man-hours × 5 m/man-hour = 630 metres.

Step 4: Compound Ratio Formula Verification

L = 240 × (14 / 8) × (9 / 6) = 240 × 1.75 × 1.5 = 240 × 2.625 = 630 m ✓.

Step 5: Final Concluding Answer

The 14 workers will dye 630 metres of fabric in 9 hours.

PYQ 3 (3M): Two-Era Age Ratio System
Step 1: Given Information

• 5 years ago: Ratio of ages (A : B) = 4 : 5.
• 10 years hence (from now): Ratio of ages (A : B) = 7 : 8.
• Goal: Find present ages of A and B.

Step 2: Setting up Algebraic Variables

Let ages 5 years ago be 4k and 5k.
Present ages: A = 4k + 5, B = 5k + 5.
Ages 10 years hence: A = 4k + 15, B = 5k + 15.

Step 3: Solving the Proportion

(4k + 15) / (5k + 15) = 7 / 8
Cross-multiply:
8(4k + 15) = 7(5k + 15)
32k + 120 = 35k + 105
35k - 32k = 120 - 105
3k = 15 ⇒ k = 5.

Step 4: Present Ages

• Present age of A = 4(5) + 5 = 20 + 5 = 25 years.
• Present age of B = 5(5) + 5 = 25 + 5 = 30 years.
Verification: 5 years ago: 20:25 = 4:5 ✓. 10 years hence: 35:40 = 7:8 ✓.

Step 5: Final Concluding Answer

A's present age is 25 years and B's present age is 30 years.

PYQ 4 (3M): Packaging Volume Discount & Percentage Savings
Step 1: Given Pricing Options

• Small tube: 50 mL for ₹85.
• Economy bottle: 200 mL for ₹260.
• Goal: Find percentage savings by purchasing the economy bottle instead of four 50 mL tubes.

Step 2: Equivalent Volume Cost

To get 200 mL using small tubes: 4 tubes × 50 mL = 200 mL.
Cost of four 50 mL tubes = 4 × ₹85 = ₹340.
Cost of one 200 mL bottle = ₹260.

Step 3: Calculating Absolute & Percentage Savings

• Absolute Savings = ₹340 - ₹260 = ₹80.
• Percentage Savings = (Savings ÷ Cost of Tubes) × 100%
= (80 ÷ 340) × 100% = (8 / 34) × 100% = (4 / 17) × 100% ≈ 23.53%.

Step 4: Unit Cost Check

Small tube: ₹85 / 50 mL = ₹1.70 per mL.
Bottle: ₹260 / 200 mL = ₹1.30 per mL.
Unit saving = ₹0.40/mL. Percentage = (0.40 / 1.70) × 100% ≈ 23.53% ✓.

Step 5: Final Concluding Answer

Purchasing the economy bottle provides an absolute saving of ₹80, representing a 23.53% discount.

PYQ 5 (3M): Temperature Equality Point (°C = °F Derivation)
Step 1: Formula Definition

The conversion between Celsius (°C) and Fahrenheit (°F) is: F = (9/5)C + 32.

Step 2: Condition for Numerical Equality

Set F = C = T:
T = (9/5)T + 32

Step 3: Solving the Algebraic Equation

Multiply the entire equation by 5:
5T = 9T + 160
Subtract 9T from both sides:
5T - 9T = 160
-4T = 160
T = 160 ÷ (-4) = -40.

Step 4: Verification

Substitute C = -40 into formula:
F = (9/5)(-40) + 32 = 9(-8) + 32 = -72 + 32 = -40 ✓.
Both thermometers read the exact same number at -40°!

Step 5: Final Concluding Answer

The unique temperature at which Celsius and Fahrenheit scales read the exact same number is -40° (-40 °C = -40 °F).

The 5 Most Fatal Student Blunders

Trap 1: Forming Ratios with Inconsistent Measurement Units
The Flawed Student Way

Writing the ratio of 200 grams of tea to 1 kg of tea as: 200 : 1.

Why it fails: The student compared grams directly with kilograms, resulting in an error by a factor of 1,000!

The 100% CBSE Topper Way

Always convert both quantities to the same unit first:
1 kg = 1,000 g ⇒ Ratio = 200 g : 1000 g = 1 : 5.

Rule: A ratio must be dimensionless. Units MUST match and cancel out completely.

Trap 2: Believing Adding a Constant Preserves Proportionality
The Flawed Student Way

Assuming that if father and son's ages are in ratio 4 : 1, their age ratio after 10 years will still be 4 : 1 or (4+10) : (1+10) = 14 : 11.

Why it fails: The student assumed adding 10 years preserves the ratio or simply added 10 to the ratio terms.

The 100% CBSE Topper Way

Set up an algebraic variable: let initial ages be 4k and 1k.
After 10 years, ages are (4k + 10) and (k + 10).
New Ratio = (4k + 10) / (k + 10), which strictly decreases towards 1.

Rule: Proportionality is preserved ONLY by multiplication or division by a non-zero factor, NEVER by addition or subtraction.

Trap 3: Applying Direct Proportion to Inverse Variation (Speed vs Time)
The Flawed Student Way

If speed increases from 50 km/h (taking 2 hours) to 75 km/h, writing:
50 : 2 :: 75 : x ⇒ x = (2 × 75) / 50 = 3 hours.

Why it fails: The student concluded that driving faster takes MORE time!

The 100% CBSE Topper Way

Recognize that speed and time are inversely proportional:
Speed₁ × Time₁ = Speed₂ × Time₂ = Distance
50 × 2 = 75 × t ⇒ t = 100 / 75 = 4/3 hours (1 h 20 min).

Rule: When an increase in one variable decreases the other, use x₁y₁ = x₂y₂, NOT the direct Rule of Three.

Trap 4: Dividing by the Ratio Term Instead of Total Parts (m + n)
The Flawed Student Way

To divide ₹4,500 in ratio 2 : 3, dividing 4,500 by 2 or by 3 directly.

Why it fails: The student confused an individual share with the total number of parts in the whole.

The 100% CBSE Topper Way

First find the total number of units: Total Parts = 2 + 3 = 5.
Value of 1 part = ₹4,500 ÷ 5 = ₹900.
Shares = 2 × 900 = ₹1,800 and 3 × 900 = ₹2,700.

Rule: The whole corresponds to the sum of parts: Whole = (m + n) units.

Trap 5: Inverting Āryabhaṭa's Trairāśika Division Order
The Flawed Student Way

Writing: Ichchhāphala = (Pramāṇa × Phala) ÷ Ichchhā or (Pramāṇa × Ichchhā) ÷ Phala.

Why it fails: Swapping numerator and denominator scrambles the cross-multiplication equation.

The 100% CBSE Topper Way

Always align the proportion cleanly:
Pramāṇa (a) : Phala (b) :: Ichchhā (c) : Ichchhāphala (d)
Extremes product = Means product ⇒ a × d = b × c
d = (b × c) ÷ a.

Rule: Multiply the "Fruit" by the "Desire" and divide by the "Measure"!

15 Rapid Revision Anchors (Teacher High-Yield Summary)

  1. Definition of Ratio: A ratio a : b compares two quantities of the same physical dimension by division. It is a pure, dimensionless number without any units.
  2. Antecedent & Consequent: In a : b, a is the antecedent (first term) and b is the consequent (second term). The order cannot be switched without altering the meaning.
  3. Simplest Form: A ratio is in its simplest (irreducible) form when the HCF of both terms is 1, meaning the terms are co-prime integers.
  4. Proportion: When two ratios represent the exact same quotient (a : b = c : d), they are in proportion, symbolized by a : b :: c : d.
  5. Fundamental Law of Proportion: In any proportion a : b :: c : d, the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: a × d = b × c.
  6. Cross-Multiplication Formula: Any unknown fourth term is computed as d = (b × c) ÷ a.
  7. Ancient Trairāśika: Established by Āryabhaṭa I (499 CE) and Bhāskara II, Trairāśika calculates Ichchhāphala = (Phala × Ichchhā) ÷ Pramāṇa.
  8. Addition Invariant Trap: Adding or subtracting a positive constant to both terms changes the ratio. Proportionality is preserved ONLY by non-zero multiplication or division.
  9. Partitioning Whole x in Ratio m : n: Total parts = (m + n). Unit part size = x / (m + n). First share = m × [x / (m+n)], Second share = n × [x / (m+n)].
  10. Partnership Profit Rule: Business partners distribute net profits in direct proportion to their initial capital investments (Profit₁ : Profit₂ = Investment₁ : Investment₂).
  11. Aspect Ratio & Visual Similarity: Two rectangles or digital images are geometrically similar if and only if their aspect ratio (Width ÷ Height) remains identical.
  12. Dimensional Scaling Laws: Scaling linear lengths by factor k scales perimeter by k, surface area by k², and volume capacity by k³.
  13. Critical Unit Anchors: Always memorize: 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft; 1 hectare = 10,000 m² = 2.471 acres; 1 L = 1,000 mL = 1,000 cc; 1 m = 3.281 ft.
  14. Temperature Conversion: Formulas are °F = (9/5)°C + 32 and °C = (5/9)(°F - 32). Both thermometers read the exact same value at -40°.
  15. Direct vs Inverse Distinction: In direct proportion, the ratio y/x is constant (straight line through origin). In inverse variation (e.g., speed vs time), the product x·y is constant (hyperbolic curve).
