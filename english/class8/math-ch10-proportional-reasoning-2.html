2.1 Proportionality — A Quick Recap

In our daily lives, quantities rarely exist in isolation; they change in harmony with one another. When two or more related quantities change by the exact same multiplying factor, we say that their relationship is a proportional relationship.

Mathematical Definition: Proportional Relationship

Two quantities x and y are said to be in direct proportion if the ratio of any value of x to its corresponding value of y remains constant. That is:

x / y = k   (where k is a positive constant called the Constant of Proportionality)

This implies that if x is multiplied by any factor m, the corresponding value of y must also be multiplied by the same factor m.

The Culinary Idli Batter Paradigm

Consider the traditional preparation of South Indian idlis. Idli batter is prepared by fermenting a mixture of rice and urad dal (black gram). The softness, fluffiness, and authentic taste of the idlis depend strictly on preserving the ratio of these two ingredients:

  • Standard Recipe: For every 2 cups of rice, exactly 1 cup of urad dal is added. We write this ratio as 2 : 1.
  • Viswanath's Batch: Viswanath prepares a family breakfast using 6 cups of rice and 3 cups of urad dal (Ratio 6 : 3).
  • Puneet's Batch: Puneet prepares a smaller breakfast using 4 cups of rice and 2 cups of urad dal (Ratio 4 : 2).

Will Viswanath's and Puneet's idlis taste identical?
Yes! If cooked in the same way, their idlis will taste exactly identical because both ingredient mixtures maintain the identical proportion. Let us prove this mathematically using two equivalent methods:

Method 1: Fraction Simplification

• Viswanath's ratio: 6 / 3 = 2 / 1 = 2
• Puneet's ratio: 4 / 2 = 2 / 1 = 2

Since both fractions simplify to the same irreducible fraction 2/1, the ratios are equivalent.

Method 2: Cross-Multiplication

Set the two ratios side by side: 6 : 3 and 4 : 2.
• Product of Extremes = 6 × 2 = 12
• Product of Means = 3 × 4 = 12

Since the cross-products are equal (12 = 12), the two ratios form a valid proportion: 6 : 3 :: 4 : 2.

Teacher's Algebraic Proof: Why Cross-Multiplication Works

Suppose we are testing if two ratios a : b and c : d are in proportion. That is, we test if:

a / b = c / d

Multiply both sides of the equation by the common denominator b × d:

(a / b) × (b × d) = (c / d) × (b × d)
⇒ a × d = b × c

Hence, Product of Extremes (a × d) = Product of Means (b × c) is a necessary and sufficient mathematical condition for any two ratios to be proportional!

2.2 Ratios in Maps & Cartographic Scales

Have you ever held an atlas or examined a printed geographic map and noticed a tiny ratio printed in the bottom corner, such as 1 : 60,00,000? This mathematical ratio is called the Representative Fraction (RF) or the Map Scale.

Definition: Representative Fraction (RF)

A Representative Fraction (RF) is a dimensionless ratio that expresses the relationship between a measured distance on a map and the corresponding actual geographical distance on the ground:

RF = Distance on the Map / Corresponding Actual Distance on the Ground

Crucial Rule of Cartography: Both the numerator (map distance) and the denominator (ground distance) must be expressed in the exact same unit of measurement before writing the ratio!

Deconstructing Scale RF 1 : 60,00,000

What does RF 1 : 60,00,000 physically mean?

  • It means that 1 centimeter on the map corresponds to 60,00,000 centimeters on the surface of the Earth.
  • It also means 1 millimeter on the map corresponds to 60,00,000 millimeters on the ground.
  • Because people do not measure driving distances in centimeters, we must bridge metric units using proportional conversion factors!
Step-by-Step Metric Unit Conversion Proof

Let us convert 60,00,000 cm into kilometers:

  1. Step 1 (Centimeters to Metres): Since 1 metre = 100 centimetres, divide by 100:
    60,00,000 cm ÷ 100 = 60,000 metres
  2. Step 2 (Metres to Kilometres): Since 1 kilometre = 1,000 metres, divide by 1,000:
    60,000 metres ÷ 1,000 = 60 kilometres

Master Conversion Benchmark: 1 kilometre = 1,000 × 100 = 1,00,000 cm (one lakh centimeters).
Therefore, on a map with RF 1 : 60,00,000:

1 cm on the map = 60 km of actual geographical ground distance!

Geographical Straight-Line Distance vs. Actual Road Distance

The textbook presents an imperative real-world caution: The distance calculated using a map scale is the direct geographical distance (as the crow flies), not the road distance!

Measured with a straight ruler on the map and multiplied by the scale factor (e.g., distance in cm × 60 km). Ignores all terrain obstacles, rivers, hills, valleys, and national borders.

2.3 Ratios with More Than 2 Terms (Multi-Term Ratios)

In elementary math, students examine comparisons between two items: boys to girls (3 : 4) or length to width (16 : 9). However, the real physical world compounds multiple ingredients simultaneously.

8 : 4 : 2 : 1 :: 4 : 2 : 1 : 0.5
General Law of Multi-Term Proportion

When two ratios containing multiple terms are proportional:

a : b : c : d :: p : q : r : s

Then the corresponding terms share an invariant quotient:

a / p = b / q = c / r = d / s = k

  • For high-strength structural members such as earthquake-resistant columns, ceiling slabs, and bridge beams (Standard Grade M20), the universal volumetric ratio is:
    Cement : Sand : Gravel = 1 : 1.5 : 3
  • Textbook Example 2: A mason has 3 bags of cement. How much sand, gravel, and total concrete will be produced?

    • Sand required = 1.5 × 3 = 4.5 bags.
    • Gravel required = 3 × 3 = 9 bags.
    • Total concrete mixture = 3 + 4.5 + 9 = 16.5 bags!

2.4 Dividing a Whole in a Given Multi-Term Ratio

A frequent challenge in engineering, commerce, estate inheritance, and geometry is partitioning a known total quantity (a "whole" W) into pieces governed by a multi-term ratio a : b : c : ....

Master Partition Theorem

When a whole quantity W is divided in the multi-term ratio a : b : c : ...:

  1. Compute the total number of parts by summing the ratio terms:
    Sum of Parts (S) = a + b + c + ...
  2. Determine the physical magnitude of one single part:
    Value of 1 Part = W / (a + b + c + ...)
  3. Multiply the value of one part by each individual term:
    Part A = W × [a / (a + b + c + ...)]
    Part B = W × [b / (a + b + c + ...)]
    Part C = W × [c / (a + b + c + ...)]

Deep-Dive Geometric Application: Triangle Angle Sum Property

In Example 5 (Page 60), students are tasked with constructing a triangle whose interior angles are in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5.

  • We know from Euclid's Geometry that the sum of interior angles in any Euclidean triangle is strictly 180°.
    ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°
  • Sum of the ratio terms = 1 + 3 + 5 = 9 parts.
  • Value of 1 part = 180° ÷ 9 = 20°.
  • Evaluating the three interior angles:
    • ∠A = 1 × 20° = 20°
    • ∠B = 3 × 20° = 60°
    • ∠C = 5 × 20° = 100°
  • Verification Check: 20° + 60° + 100° = 180°. Because one angle is greater than 90° (∠C = 100°), this triangle is classified as an obtuse-angled scalene triangle!

Geometric Fallacy: Sidelength Ratios vs. Congruence & Existence

Questions 4 and 5 of "Figure It Out" (Page 60) explore two of the most profound conceptual traps in middle school geometry:

Trap 1: Do Triangles with Sidelength Ratio 3 : 4 : 5 Have to Be Congruent?

The Question: Construct a triangle with sidelengths in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5. Will all triangles drawn with this ratio be congruent to each other?

The Teacher's Explanation: ABSOLUTELY NOT!
• Triangles with sides in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 have sides that can measure (3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm), or (6 cm, 8 cm, 10 cm), or (30 cm, 40 cm, 50 cm).
• All these triangles share identical interior angles (90°, 53.13°, 36.87°). Therefore, they are SIMILAR (सଦୃଶ).
• However, CONGRUENCE (ସର୍ବସମତା) strictly demands that corresponding sides have identical physical lengths (so that one triangle completely covers the other when superimposed). A triangle with sides 6, 8, 10 cm is four times larger in area than a triangle of 3, 4, 5 cm! Thus, they are similar, but NOT congruent.

Trap 2: Can You Construct a Triangle with Sidelengths in Ratio 1 : 3 : 5?

The Question: Can you construct a triangle with sidelengths in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5? Why or why not?

The Teacher's Explanation: NO! SUCH A TRIANGLE CAN NEVER EXIST!
• By the fundamental Triangle Inequality Theorem (ତ୍ରିଭୁଜର ଅସମତା ସୂତ୍ର): The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be strictly greater than the length of the third side.

Side 1 = 1x,  Side 2 = 3x,  Side 3 = 5x (where x > 0)
Sum of two shorter sides = 1x + 3x = 4x
Comparison: 4x < 5x   (The sum of the two shorter sides is LESS than the longest side!)

If you try to draw this with a compass and ruler: draw a base segment of 5 cm. From one end, draw an arc of radius 1 cm; from the other end, draw an arc of radius 3 cm. Even if stretched completely flat along the base line, 1 cm + 3 cm = 4 cm, leaving a 1 cm empty gap! The arcs will never intersect. Hence, no closed polygon can be formed.

2.5 A Slice of the Pie: Pie Charts (Circle Graphs)

A Pie Chart (also termed a Circle Graph or ବୃତ୍ତଲେଖ in Odia) is a circular statistical graphic divided into radial slices or sectors. The arc length, central angle, and surface area of each sector are directly proportional to the quantity or frequency it represents.

Why Does a Circle Form the Ideal Proportional Canvas?

  • A complete geometric revolution around a central point measures exactly 360°.
  • Therefore, the entire data set (the total frequency N or 100%) corresponds to the complete 360° circle.
  • Each data component occupies a central sector angle θ proportional to its share of the whole:
    Central Angle (θ) / 360° = Component Frequency (f) / Total Frequency (N)
    Central Angle (θ) = [Frequency (f) / Total Frequency (N)] × 360°

Notice the ratio of students: 12 : 10 : 8 : 6 : 4.
Instead of multiplying large numbers, divide all frequencies by their Highest Common Factor (HCF = 2):
The simplified ratio is 6 : 5 : 4 : 3 : 2.

Therefore, Each 1 part = 360° ÷ 20 = 18°!

2.6 Inverse Proportions: The Reciprocal Universe

In direct proportion, when one variable increases, the other variable increases at the identical rate. But consider real-life situations:

  • If you ride your bicycle faster to school, do you take more time or less time? (Less time!)
  • If more construction workers join a building site, will they take more days or fewer days to finish the wall? (Fewer days!)
  • If a hostel admits 20 additional students, will the stored grain last for more days or fewer days? (Fewer days!)

These scenarios represent the realm of Inverse Proportion (ବ୍ୟସ୍ତ ସମାନୁପାତ), where quantities move in opposite directions under a strict reciprocal law!

The Journey from Lucknow to Kanpur (90 km Distance)

Puneeth's father travels between Lucknow and Kanpur, a fixed highway distance of 90 km. He records his travel duration using various modes of transportation:

Mode of Travel Speed (x in km/h) Speed Factor vs Walking Time Taken (y in hours) Time Factor vs Walking Product (x · y = Distance)
Walking on Foot 5 km/h 1× (Base) 18 hours 1× (Base) 5 × 18 = 90 km
Bicycle 15 km/h 3× faster (15 ÷ 5) 6 hours 1/3 duration (18 ÷ 3) 15 × 6 = 90 km
Motorcycle 30 km/h 6× faster (30 ÷ 5) 3 hours 1/6 duration (18 ÷ 6) 30 × 3 = 90 km
Car on Highway 60 km/h 12× faster (60 ÷ 5) 1.5 hours 1/12 duration (18 ÷ 12) 60 × 1.5 = 90 km
Mathematical Definition: Inverse Proportion

Two quantities x and y are said to vary in inverse proportion if there exists a relation of the type:

x · y = k   (where k is a positive constant called the Constant of Inverse Variation)

Equivalently, if (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) are corresponding pairs of values of the two quantities:

x₁ · y₁ = x₂ · y₂ = k

Dividing both sides reveals the reciprocal ratio statement:

x₁ / x₂ = y₂ / y₁

The Golden Reciprocal Law: When quantity x is multiplied by any factor n, the corresponding quantity y is automatically multiplied by the reciprocal factor 1/n (divided by n)!

Work, Time, and Worker-Days (The Product Invariant)

In civil work, harvesting, and manufacturing, the total quantum of work W required to complete an objective is measured in Worker-Days (ଶ୍ରମିକ-ଦିବସ) or Man-Hours:

Total Work (W) = Number of Workers (W) × Number of Days (D) = Constant k
  • Textbook Example 3: 20 workers take 4 days to lay a road. How many days will 10 workers take?
    Total Work Volume = 20 workers × 4 days = 80 worker-days.
    Let d be the days for 10 workers:
    10 × d = 80 ⇒ d = 80 ÷ 10 = 8 days!
    Notice: Workers halved (20 → 10, factor of 1/2), so days doubled (4 → 8, factor of 2)!
  • Textbook Example 4: 2 identical pumps fill a water reservoir in 18 hours. If 2 more pumps of the same capacity are added, how long will it take?
    Initial Pumps = 2. Added Pumps = 2. New Total Pumps = 2 + 2 = 4 pumps!
    Inverse Equation: 2 pumps × 18 hours = 4 pumps × t hours
    36 = 4t ⇒ t = 36 ÷ 4 = 9 hours!
  • Textbook Example 5: A boarding school has food grains for 80 students lasting 15 days. If 20 more students join, how long will the food last?
    Initial Students = 80. Food days = 15. Total student-days of grain = 80 × 15 = 1200.
    New Student Population = 80 + 20 = 100 students.
    Let d be the new number of days:
    100 × d = 1200 ⇒ d = 1200 ÷ 100 = 12 days!

The Combined Work Rate Theorem (Ram & Shyam)

What happens when two people with different working speeds collaborate together on a shared task?

Theorem: Combined Work Rate via Unitary Method

If Person A completes 1 full unit of work in t₁ hours, and Person B completes the same work in t₂ hours:

  1. Rate of Person A in 1 hour = 1 / t₁ units of work.
  2. Rate of Person B in 1 hour = 1 / t₂ units of work.
  3. Combined Rate working together in 1 hour:
    Combined 1-Hour Work = 1 / t₁ + 1 / t₂ = (t₁ + t₂) / (t₁ · t₂)
  4. Total Time (T) needed to finish 1 complete unit of work together:
    T = 1 / [Combined Rate] = (t₁ · t₂) / (t₁ + t₂)

Let us verify Example 6 (Page 66-67) using this exact rigorous framework:

  • Ram's speed: Ram takes 1 hour to cut a basket of vegetables. In 1 hour, Ram finishes 1 / 1 = 1 unit of work.
  • Shyam's speed: Shyam takes 1.5 hours (which is 3/2 hours). In 1 hour, Shyam finishes:
    1 ÷ (3/2) = 2/3 unit of work.
  • Working together for 1 hour:
    Combined Work = 1 + 2/3 = 5/3 units of work!
  • Time needed to complete 1 unit of work:
    Time = 1 ÷ (5/3) = 3/5 of an hour!
    Converting into clock minutes:
    Time = (3/5) × 60 minutes = 3 × 12 = 36 minutes!

The Master Formula Deck

1. Direct Proportion (Trairāśika)

x₁ / y₁ = x₂ / y₂  ⇒  x₁ · y₂ = x₂ · y₁

Finding the fourth unknown: y₂ = (x₂ · y₁) / x₁.

2. Inverse Proportion (Constant Product)

x₁ · y₁ = x₂ · y₂  ⇒  y₂ = (x₁ · y₁) / x₂

Product of initial pair divided by the new primary variable.

3. Multi-Term Ratio Expansion

a : b : c : d :: p : q : r : s  ⇒  a/p = b/q = c/r = d/s = k

Every corresponding term pairs to the identical scale factor k.

4. Pie Chart Central Sector Angle

θ = (Frequency f / Total N) × 360° = (% / 100) × 360°

Converts discrete frequency counts or percentages into protractor degrees.

5. Cartographic Ground Distance

Actual Ground Dist = Map Distance (cm) × Scale Factor (N)

For RF 1:60,00,000: Ground km = (Map cm × 60,00,000) ÷ 1,00,000 = Map cm × 60.

6. Combined Collaborative Work

1 / T_total = 1 / t₁ + 1 / t₂  ⇒  T_total = (t₁ · t₂) / (t₁ + t₂)

Harmonic combination rule for two workers or two inflow pumps.

• Solar Generation (45%): 1,20,000 × (45 / 100) = 54,000 kWh.
• Biomass Generation (20%): 1,20,000 × (20 / 100) = 24,000 kWh.
Answer: Solar PV generated 54,000 kWh and Biomass generated 24,000 kWh.

Part (iii): If every 1 kWh of solar electricity offsets 0.85 kg of carbon dioxide (CO₂) emissions compared to a thermal coal plant, calculate the total metric tonnes of CO₂ emissions prevented by the solar installation that year (1 tonne = 1,000 kg). [2 Marks]

• Solar electricity generated = 54,000 kWh.
• CO₂ emissions offset in kg = 54,000 kWh × 0.85 kg/kWh = 45,900 kg of CO₂.
• Convert kg to metric tonnes: 45,900 ÷ 1,000 = 45.9 metric tonnes.
Answer: The solar installation prevented 45.9 metric tonnes of CO₂ from entering the atmosphere that year.

PYQ 1 (CBSE 2023) Train Transit Speed Deceleration (75 km/h in 4h 48m to 60 km/h)

Question: An express train runs at an average speed of 75 km/h and covers a journey in 4 hours 48 minutes. How long will the same train take to complete the journey if its speed is reduced to 60 km/h?

Step 1: Time Unit Conversion & Distance Invariant

• Convert 4 hours 48 minutes into fractional hours:
48 minutes = 48/60 = 4/5 hours = 0.8 hours.
• Total initial time t₁ = 4.8 hours.
• Total track distance d = Speed × Time = 75 km/h × 4.8 hours = 360 km.

Step 2: Inverse Proportion Setup

Speed and time vary inversely: s₁ · t₁ = s₂ · t₂
60 × t₂ = 360 ⇒ t₂ = 360 ÷ 60 = 6 hours.

Step 3: Final Answer & Real-World Check

Final Answer: The train will take exactly 6 hours (an increase of 1 hour 12 minutes due to the 15 km/h speed reduction).

PYQ 2 (CBSE 2022) Repackaging Wheat: 120 Sacks of 25 kg vs. 30 kg Sacks

Question: A granary heap of wheat is packed into 120 sacks, each containing 25 kg of wheat. If the same heap is packed into larger sacks of 30 kg each, how many sacks will be required?

Step 1: Identifying Nature of Variation & Total Wheat Mass

Total grain weight is constant: Total Wheat = Sacks × Weight per Sack.
Larger sack capacity requires fewer total sacks: Inverse Proportion: n₁ · w₁ = n₂ · w₂.

Step 2: Calculation

• Total wheat mass = 120 sacks × 25 kg/sack = 3,000 kg.
• 30 × n₂ = 3,000 ⇒ n₂ = 3,000 ÷ 30 = 100 sacks.

Step 3: Final Answer

Final Answer: Exactly 100 sacks will be required.

PYQ 3 (CBSE 2024 Model) Stadium Blueprint at Scale 1 : 500 (Perimeter & Area)

Question: An architectural blueprint of a sports stadium has dimensions 18 cm by 12 cm. If the scale of the blueprint is 1 : 500, calculate the actual physical perimeter and ground area of the stadium in metres and square metres.

Step 1: Real Ground Linear Dimensions

Scale 1 : 500 means 1 cm on paper = 500 cm on ground = 5 metres.
• Actual Length (L) = 18 cm × 5 m/cm = 90 metres.
• Actual Width (W) = 12 cm × 5 m/cm = 60 metres.

Step 2: Calculating Actual Perimeter

Perimeter = 2 × (L + W) = 2 × (90 + 60) = 2 × 150 = 300 metres.

Step 3: Calculating Actual Ground Area

Ground Area = L × W = 90 m × 60 m = 5,400 square metres (m²).
Final Answer: The stadium's perimeter is 300 metres and its ground area is 5,400 m².

PYQ 4 (CBSE 2023) Collaborative Work: Priya & Rahul Painting a Hall

Question: Two painters, Priya and Rahul, working together can paint a community hall in 6 days. If Priya alone takes 10 days to paint the hall, how many days would Rahul take working completely alone?

Step 1: Work Rate Formulation

• In 1 day, Priya and Rahul together complete 1/6 of the hall.
• In 1 day, Priya alone completes 1/10 of the hall.
• Let Rahul's solo time be r days. In 1 day, Rahul completes 1/r of the hall.

Step 2: Subtracting Priya's Rate from Combined Rate

1/r = 1/6 - 1/10
LCM of 6 and 10 is 30:
1/r = 5/30 - 3/30 = 2/30 = 1/15.

Step 3: Inverting Rate & Final Answer

r = 15 days.
Final Answer: Rahul would take 15 days working alone to paint the hall.

PYQ 5 (NCERT Exemplar) Triangle Perimeter 72 cm with Sides in Ratio 2 : 3 : 4

Question: The perimeter of a triangle is 72 cm. Its sides are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. Find the length of each side. Also verify whether this triangle satisfies the Triangle Inequality Theorem.

Step 1: Partitioning Perimeter Whole in Ratio 2 : 3 : 4

• Perimeter = a + b + c = 72 cm.
• Sum of ratio terms = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 parts.
• Value of 1 part = 72 ÷ 9 = 8 cm.

Step 2: Calculating Sidelengths

• Side a = 2 × 8 = 16 cm.
• Side b = 3 × 8 = 24 cm.
• Side c = 4 × 8 = 32 cm.
Perimeter Check: 16 + 24 + 32 = 72 cm.

Step 3: Verification of Triangle Inequality Theorem

We test the sum of the two shorter sides against the longest side:
• a + b = 16 + 24 = 40 cm.
• Longest side c = 32 cm.
• Comparison: 40 cm > 32 cm (Sum strictly exceeds the third side!).
Also: 16 + 32 = 48 > 24, and 24 + 32 = 56 > 16.
Final Answer: The sidelengths are 16 cm, 24 cm, and 32 cm. It satisfies the Triangle Inequality Theorem (40 > 32) and forms a valid scalene triangle.

Section 6: Exam Hall Trap Alerts & 15 Revision Points

The ultimate exam guardrail. Review the top 5 conceptual traps that cost students marks and commit the 15 golden high-yield revision principles to memory.

The 5 Most Dangerous Student Blunders & How to Avoid Them

Blunder 1: The "Averaging Time" Trap in Combined Work Problems

The Typical Student Error:
"If Ram takes 4 hours to paint a room and Shyam takes 6 hours, working together they will take (4 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5 hours!"

Why This Fails Mathematically & Logically:
Think about this: If Ram can finish the entire room alone in 4 hours, having a friend help him CANNOT make it take longer than 4 hours! Combining workers always reduces completion time to less than the fastest individual worker's time.

The Correct Mathematical Method:
You cannot average times; you must sum their reciprocal rates of work!

Ram's 1-hour rate = 1/4  |  Shyam's 1-hour rate = 1/6
Combined Rate = 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 of the room per hour.
True Combined Time = 1 ÷ (5/12) = 12/5 = 2.4 hours (2 hours 24 minutes)!
Blunder 2: The Unit Conversion Blindspot in Cartographic Scales (RF)

The Typical Student Error:
"On a map with RF 1 : 50,000, 3 cm represents 3 × 50,000 = 1,50,000 km on the ground."

Why This Fails Mathematically:
A Representative Fraction is a pure, unitless ratio. 1 cm on paper represents 50,000 centimetres on the ground, not kilometres! 1,50,000 km would circle the entire globe nearly 4 times!

The Correct Mathematical Method:
Always execute the two-tier metric conversion chain:

1. cm to metres: Divide by 100  ⇒  1,50,000 ÷ 100 = 1,500 m
2. metres to km: Divide by 1,000  ⇒  1,500 ÷ 1,000 = 1.5 km!
Master Rule: Ground km = (Map cm × Scale Denominator) ÷ 1,00,000.
Blunder 3: The Cross-Multiplication Misapplication in Inverse Variation

The Typical Student Error:
"If 20 workers take 4 days, then 10 workers take x days: 20/4 = 10/x ⇒ 20x = 40 ⇒ x = 2 days!"

Why This Fails Mathematically:
The student blindly used direct variation cross-multiplication. But reducing workers from 20 to 10 cannot cause a road to be built in fewer days!

The Correct Mathematical Method:
In inverse proportion, the product is invariant, not the quotient:

Workers × Days = Constant Work Volume (w₁ · d₁ = w₂ · d₂)
20 × 4 = 10 × x  ⇒  80 = 10x  ⇒  x = 8 days!
Blunder 4: The Percentage-to-Degrees Confusion in Pie Charts

The Typical Student Error:
"If 30% of students scored Grade A, the central angle on the pie chart is 30°."

Why This Fails Mathematically:
A protractor circle measures 360°, whereas percentages are measured out of 100%. Treating 1% as 1° leaves 360 - 100 = 260° of empty unused circle!

The Correct Mathematical Method:
Always multiply the percentage by 3.6° (since 360° ÷ 100% = 3.6° per percent):

Central Angle θ = 30 × 3.6° = 108°!
(Verification: 108° / 360° = 0.30 = 30%).
Blunder 5: The "Ratios Guarantee Congruence" Fallacy in Geometry

The Typical Student Error:
"Since both triangles have sides in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5, they are congruent by SSS!"

Why This Fails Mathematically:
SSS congruence demands that corresponding sides have identically equal physical lengths, not merely equal ratios! A triangle with sides (3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm) has area 6 cm², while one with sides (6 cm, 8 cm, 10 cm) has area 24 cm².

The Correct Mathematical Method:
Equal sidelength ratios prove SIMILARITY (ସାଦୃଶ୍ୟ), meaning they have the identical shape and angles. They are CONGRUENT (ସର୍ବସମ) if and only if their side lengths are identically equal (scale factor k = 1).

15 High-Yield Revision Points (Morning-of-Exam Master Checklist)

  1. Direct Proportion Core Law: Two quantities x and y vary directly if their quotient is constant (x / y = k). Multiplying x by factor m multiplies y by the identical factor m.
  2. Inverse Proportion Core Law: Two quantities x and y vary inversely if their product is constant (x · y = k). Multiplying x by factor n divides y by n (multiplies by reciprocal 1/n).
  3. Cross-Multiplication Test: Two ratios a : b and c : d form a direct proportion if and only if a · d = b · c (Product of Extremes = Product of Means).
  4. Cartographic Representative Fraction: RF = Distance on Map / Ground Distance is a dimensionless ratio. Both map and ground distances must be in the identical metric unit (usually cm) before simplifying.
  5. The Metric Lakh Bridge: Exactly 1,00,000 cm = 1 km. On a map with RF 1 : 60,00,000, 1 cm represents 60,00,000 ÷ 1,00,000 = 60 km of real ground distance.
  6. Crow-Flight vs. Road Distance: Map scale measurements calculate direct geographical straight-line distance. Actual highway travel distance is typically 15% to 35% longer due to topographical curvature.
  7. Classroom Scale 1 : 50: Under a 1:50 drafting scale, 1 cm on paper = 50 cm in the room = 0.5 m. Conversely, 2 cm on paper = 1 metre in the room.
  8. Multi-Term Ratio Compounding: When a : b : c : d :: p : q : r : s, all corresponding pairs share the identical scale factor: a/p = b/q = c/r = d/s = k.
  9. Master Partitioning Formula: When dividing a whole quantity W in the multi-term ratio a : b : c : ..., each part equals W × [term / (a + b + c + ...)].
  10. Triangle Sidelength Feasibility: By the Triangle Inequality Theorem, no triangle can have sidelengths in ratio 1 : 3 : 5 because 1x + 3x = 4x < 5x (the two shorter sides cannot meet).
  11. Pie Chart Complete Angle: The complete revolution of a circle subtends exactly 360°. The central sector angle for any item is θ = (Frequency / Total N) × 360°.
  12. Percentage to Degrees Conversion: To convert a percentage P% into pie chart degrees, multiply by 3.6°: θ = P × 3.6° (e.g., 50% = 180°, 25% = 90°, 10% = 36°).
  13. Pie Chart HCF Mental Shortcut: Simplify data frequencies by their Highest Common Factor (HCF) before calculating angles. If sum of simplified terms is S, then 1 part = 360° ÷ S.
  14. Speed-Time Invariant: When transit distance is fixed, Speed₁ × Time₁ = Speed₂ × Time₂. Doubling cruising speed cuts travel time in half.
  15. Combined Work Reciprocal Theorem: Working together, completion times cannot be averaged. Work rates add directly: 1 / T_total = 1 / t₁ + 1 / t₂, yielding T_total = (t₁ · t₂) / (t₁ + t₂).
