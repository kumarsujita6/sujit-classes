1. The Paper Folding Venture: What is Exponential Growth?

Take a sheet of paper. When you fold it once in half, it has 2 layers. Fold it again, it has 4 layers. Fold it a 3rd time, it has 8 layers.

What is an Exponent? In the expression aⁿ:
• a is called the base (the number being repeatedly multiplied).
• n is called the exponent, index, or power (the count of times the base is multiplied by itself).

2. The 7 Fundamental Laws of Exponents

When working with powers that share the same base, we do not need to expand long multiplications. Instead, we use mathematical shortcuts called the Laws of Exponents:

Law 1: Multiplying Powers with the Same Base
aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ Teacher's intuition: 2³ × 2⁴ = (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = 2⁷ (3 + 4 = 7). You simply ADD the powers!
Law 2: Dividing Powers with the Same Base
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (where a ≠ 0 and m > n) Teacher's intuition: 5⁵ ÷ 5² = (5 × 5 × 5 × 5 × 5) / (5 × 5) = 5³ (5 - 2 = 3). You simply SUBTRACT the powers!
Law 3: Power of a Power
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ Teacher's intuition: (3²)⁴ = 3² × 3² × 3² × 3² = 3²⁺²⁺²⁺² = 3⁸ (2 × 4 = 8). You MULTIPLY the powers!
Law 4 & 5: Powers of Products and Quotients
(a × b)ⁿ = aⁿ × bⁿ      and      (a / b)ⁿ = aⁿ / bⁿ Teacher's intuition: (2 × 3)⁴ = 6⁴ = 1296. Also 2⁴ × 3⁴ = 16 × 81 = 1296!
Law 6: The Mystery of Power Zero (a⁰ = 1)
Why is any non-zero number raised to the power 0 equal to 1?
Consider aᵐ ÷ aᵐ.
• By normal arithmetic: Any number divided by itself equals 1 (e.g. 7³ ÷ 7³ = 343 / 343 = 1).
• By Law 2 of exponents: aᵐ ÷ aᵐ = aᵐ⁻ᵐ = a⁰.
Comparing both results: a⁰ = 1 (provided a ≠ 0).
Law 7: Negative Exponents (The Reciprocal Power)
a⁻ⁿ = 1 / aⁿ      and      1 / a⁻ⁿ = aⁿ A negative exponent simply means: "Turn me upside down (take my reciprocal) and make my power positive!"
Example: 2⁻³ = 1 / 2³ = 1 / 8.
Example: (3/5)⁻² = (5/3)² = 25 / 9.

3. Standard Scientific Notation

Astronomers and scientists deal with enormous numbers (mass of the sun: 2,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 kg) and sub-microscopic numbers (radius of a hydrogen atom: 0.000000000053 m). Writing all those zeros causes errors. To solve this, we use Scientific Notation.

Scientific Form = m × 10ᵏ
where 1 ≤ m < 10 (m has exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point)
and k is an integer (positive for large numbers, negative for tiny decimals).
Real-World Quantity Normal Number Scientific Notation

In-Text Activities & Classroom Explorations (Fully Solved)

Activity 1 (Page 5): Express each of the following as a product of powers of their prime factors: (i) 72, (ii) 432, (iii) 1000

(i) 72:

72 ÷ 2 = 36; 36 ÷ 2 = 18; 18 ÷ 2 = 9; 9 ÷ 3 = 3; 3 ÷ 3 = 1.
Factors: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 2³ × 3².

(ii) 432:

432 = 2 × 216 = 2 × 2 × 108 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 54 = 2⁴ × 27 = 2⁴ × 3³ = 2⁴ × 3³.

(iii) 1000:

1000 = 10³ = (2 × 5)³ = 2³ × 5³.

Activity 2 (Page 10): Simplify and write in exponential form: (i) 2⁵ × 2³, (ii) p³ × p², (iii) (-4)¹⁰ ÷ (-4)⁷

(i) 2⁵ × 2³:
Using Law 1 (aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ): 2⁵⁺³ = 2⁸.

(ii) p³ × p²:
Using Law 1: p³⁺² = p⁵.

(iii) (-4)¹⁰ ÷ (-4)⁷:
Using Law 2 (aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ): (-4)¹⁰⁻⁷ = (-4)³.

Activity 3 (Page 12): Evaluate (i) 2⁻⁴, (ii) (1/3)⁻², (iii) (-2)⁻⁵

(i) 2⁻⁴:
2⁻⁴ = 1 / 2⁴ = 1 / (2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = 1 / 16.

(ii) (1/3)⁻²:
(1/3)⁻² = (3/1)² = 3² = 9.

(iii) (-2)⁻⁵:
(-2)⁻⁵ = 1 / (-2)⁵ = 1 / (-32) = -1 / 32.

Complete End-of-Chapter 'Figure it Out' Exercises (Solved Step-by-Step)

Exercise 1: Simplify and express the result in power notation with positive exponent: (i) (-4)⁵ ÷ (-4)⁸, (ii) (1/2³)²
Part (i): (-4)⁵ ÷ (-4)⁸
Apply Law: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ ⇒ (-4)⁵⁻⁸ = (-4)⁻³
Positive Exp: a⁻ⁿ = 1 / aⁿ ⇒ 1 / (-4)³
Result: 1 / (-4)³
Part (ii): (1 / 2³)²
Apply Law: (a / b)ⁿ = aⁿ / bⁿ and (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ ⇒ 1² / 2³ˣ² = 1 / 2⁶
Result: 1 / 2⁶
Exercise 2: Find the value of: (3⁰ + 4⁻¹) × 2²
Step 1: 3⁰ = 1 (by zero exponent law).
Step 2: 4⁻¹ = 1 / 4.
Step 3: Bracket sum = 1 + 1/4 = 5/4.
Step 4: 2² = 4.
Step 5: Multiply: (5/4) × 4 = 5.
Result: 5
Exercise 3: Express the following in scientific notation: (i) 0.0000000000085, (ii) 602,000,000,000,000
Part (i): Move decimal 12 places to the right to place it after 8: 8.5 × 10⁻¹².
Result: 8.5 × 10⁻¹²
Part (ii): Move decimal 14 places to the left to place it after 6: 6.02 × 10¹⁴.
Result: 6.02 × 10¹⁴
Exercise 4: Compare the size of a red blood cell (0.000007 m) to a plant cell (0.00001275 m).
RBC Size: 7 × 10⁻⁶ m = 0.7 × 10⁻⁵ m
Plant Cell: 1.275 × 10⁻⁵ m ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ m
Comparison: Ratio = (0.7 × 10⁻⁵) / (1.3 × 10⁻⁵) ≈ 7 / 13 ≈ 1 / 2.
Result: A red blood cell is approximately half the diameter of a typical plant cell!

Interactive Practice Quiz Engine

1. What is the value of (5⁰ + 6⁰ + 7⁰)⁰?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) 1.
Inside the parentheses, 5⁰ = 1, 6⁰ = 1, 7⁰ = 1, so the sum is 1 + 1 + 1 = 3. Now 3⁰ = 1! Any non-zero base raised to the power 0 is always 1.

2. What is (2⁻¹)⁻³ equal to?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (d) 8.
Using (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ: (-1) × (-3) = +3. So 2³ = 8.

3. Which of the following is correctly written in scientific notation?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) 4.25 × 10⁷.
In scientific notation m × 10ᵏ, the leading number m must satisfy 1 ≤ m < 10. Only 4.25 meets this rule.

4. The value of (3/4)⁻² is:

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a) 16 / 9.
A negative exponent inverts the fraction: (3/4)⁻² = (4/3)² = 4² / 3² = 16 / 9.

5. If 2ˣ ÷ 2⁻⁴ = 2¹⁰, what is the value of x?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) 6.
Using division law: 2ˣ ÷ 2⁻⁴ = 2^(x - (-4)) = 2^(x + 4). Since 2^(x + 4) = 2¹⁰, we equate powers: x + 4 = 10 ⇒ x = 6.

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Pitfall 1: Confusing (-3)² with -3²!
• (-3)² means (-3) × (-3) = +9.
• -3² means -(3 × 3) = -9. The minus sign is outside the exponent!
Pitfall 2: Multiplying bases when bases are different!
2³ × 3² CANNOT be written as 6⁵! The laws only apply when the bases are identical (or when the exponents are identical: 2³ × 3³ = 6³).
