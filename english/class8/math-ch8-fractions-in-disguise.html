Section 02

Deep-Dive Core Concepts & Teacher Explanations

1. The Disguise Unveiled: What is a Percentage?

The Core Definition: Any fraction with a denominator of 100 is called a Percentage:
x% = x / 100 • To turn a fraction into a percentage: Multiply by 100% (e.g. 3/4 × 100% = 75%).
• To turn a percentage into a fraction: Divide by 100 and simplify (e.g. 20% = 20/100 = 1/5).

3. Commercial Math: Discount, Profit, Loss & Taxes

A. Discounts on Marked Price (MP):
• Discount = Marked Price (MP) - Selling Price (SP)
• Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100
• Selling Price = MP × (1 - Discount%/100)
B. Profit and Loss on Cost Price (CP):
• Profit = SP - CP (when SP > CP) ⇒ Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100
• Loss = CP - SP (when CP > SP) ⇒ Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100
C. GST (Goods and Services Tax):
GST is calculated on the selling price and ADDED to the final bill:
• Bill Amount = Selling Price + GST Amount

4. Simple vs Compound Interest

When money is lent or invested:

  • Simple Interest (SI): Calculated ONLY on the original Principal for the entire period:
    SI = (P × R × T) / 100      Amount = P + SI
  • Compound Interest (CI): Interest earned at the end of each year is ADDED to the Principal to form a new larger Principal! It is "interest earning interest"!
Section 03

In-Text Activities & Classroom Explorations (Fully Solved)

Activity 1 (Page 4): Out of 25 students in a class, 18 come to school by walking. What percentage of students walk?

Fraction of students walking: 18 / 25.

Convert to Percentage: (18 / 25) × 100% = 18 × 4% = 72%.

So, 72% of the students come to school by walking.

Activity 2 (Page 8): Calculate mentally: 15% of ₹640.

Mental Shortcut: 15% = 10% + 5%.

  • 10% of 640 = ₹64 (move decimal one spot).
  • 5% of 640 = Half of ₹64 = ₹32.

Total: 64 + 32 = ₹96.

Activity 3 (Page 20): Samson bought a car for ₹4,40,000 after getting a 15% discount. What was the marked price?

Step 1: Since discount is 15%, the selling price is (100 - 15)% = 85% of MP.

Step 2: 0.85 × MP = 4,40,000.

Step 3: MP = 4,40,000 ÷ 0.85 = 44,000,000 ÷ 85 ≈ ₹5,17,647.

Section 04

Complete End-of-Chapter 'Figure it Out' Exercises (Solved Step-by-Step)

Exercise 1: An article marked at ₹800 is sold for ₹680. Find the discount and the discount percentage.
Discount: MP - SP = 800 - 680 = ₹120.
Formula: Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100.
Calculation: (120 / 800) × 100 = 120 / 8 = 15%.
Result: Discount = ₹120, Discount % = 15%.
Exercise 2: A shopkeeper purchased a television for ₹15,000 and spent ₹1,000 on transportation. If he sells it for ₹18,000, find his profit percentage.
Total CP: 15,000 + 1,000 (overhead) = ₹16,000.
Profit: SP - Total CP = 18,000 - 16,000 = ₹2,000.
Profit %: (2,000 / 16,000) × 100 = (1 / 8) × 100 = 12.5%.
Result: Profit percentage is 12.5%.
Exercise 3: Find the simple interest and total amount on ₹5,000 for 2 years at 8% per annum.
Given: P = ₹5,000, R = 8%, T = 2 years.
Formula: SI = (P × R × T) / 100 = (5000 × 8 × 2) / 100 = 50 × 16 = ₹800.
Amount: P + SI = 5,000 + 800 = ₹5,800.
Result: Simple Interest = ₹800, Total Amount = ₹5,800.

Interactive Practice Quiz Engine

Score: 0 / 5 Answer the questions below to test your mastery!

1. What is 3/5 expressed as a percentage?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) 60%.
(3 / 5) × 100% = 3 × 20% = 60%.

2. A shirt marked ₹500 is offered with a 20% discount. What is the selling price?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) ₹400.
20% of 500 = ₹100. Selling price = 500 - 100 = ₹400.

3. On which price is profit percentage or loss percentage always calculated?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a) Cost Price (CP).
Profit or loss measures return on the money initially invested, which is the Cost Price.

4. A toy costs ₹200 plus 12% GST. What is the total bill amount?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (d) ₹224.
12% of 200 = ₹24. Bill amount = 200 + 24 = ₹224.

5. In Compound Interest, why does the interest grow larger each year?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b).
Compound interest is "interest on interest", where past earnings join the principal to generate even higher returns.

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Pitfall: Calculating discount on Selling Price instead of Marked Price!
Discount is always given on the Marked Price (MP)!
Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100.
