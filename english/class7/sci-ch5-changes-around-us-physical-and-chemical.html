Exhaustive Concept Explanations

Line-by-line detailed explanations crafted by caring science teachers.

1. Physical Changes: Changes in Size, Shape, or State

Key Definition • Physical Change:
A change in which a substance undergoes a change in its physical properties—such as shape, size, color, or state of matter—WITHOUT any new chemical substance being formed is called a physical change.
Key Test: The molecules of the substance remain chemically identical before and after the change!

Classic Everyday Examples of Physical Changes

  • Melting of Ice to Water (and Freezing): Solid ice turns to liquid water when warmed, and steam when boiled. In all three states (ice, liquid water, steam), the chemical formula is strictly H₂O. No new substance is created!
  • Tearing or Folding a Sheet of Paper: When you tear a sheet of paper into four pieces or fold it into a toy boat, the size and shape change, but every piece is still made of cellulose paper fibres.
  • Dissolving Sugar or Salt in Water: When sugar dissolves, individual sucrose molecules disperse between water molecules. The sugar can be completely recovered by evaporating the water!
  • Stretching a Rubber Band or Spring: The length increases under tension, and returns to its original length when released.

2. Chemical Changes: Changes in Chemical Composition

Key Definition • Chemical Change (Chemical Reaction):
A change in which one or more brand-new substances with entirely different chemical properties are formed is called a chemical change. A chemical change cannot be reversed by simple physical methods.
Signs of a Chemical Change: Evolution of a gas (fizzing/bubbles), release or absorption of heat and light, change in permanent colour, change in smell, or formation of a precipitate.

3. Classic Laboratory Chemical Reactions Explored

Activity 5.3 • Blowing Breath into Lime Water

When you blow gently into freshly prepared clear lime water [calcium hydroxide, $ ext{Ca(OH)}_2$] through a drinking straw, the clear liquid gradually turns milky white!

Ca(OH)₂ (Lime Water) + CO₂ (Carbon Dioxide) → CaCO₃ ↓ (White Precipitate) + H₂O

Why? The carbon dioxide present in our exhaled breath reacts with dissolved calcium hydroxide to form insoluble calcium carbonate ($ ext{CaCO}_3$) particles that float suspended in the water, giving it a milky appearance. This is the universal chemical confirmatory test for carbon dioxide gas!

Activity 5.4 • Vinegar + Baking Soda Reaction

When a teaspoon of white baking soda is dropped into a cup of vinegar, immediate vigorous fizzing and bubbling (effervescence) occurs:

Acetic Acid (Vinegar) + Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate (Baking Soda) → Sodium Acetate + Water + CO₂ ↑ (Gas)

If this released gas is passed into lime water, the lime water turns milky, confirming that the bubbles are pure $ ext{CO}_2$ gas!

4. Combustion and the Fire Triangle

Key Definition • Combustion:
Combustion is a chemical process in which a fuel reacts rapidly with oxygen gas with the simultaneous release of energy in the form of heat and light.
The Fire Triangle: A fire cannot ignite or sustain itself unless three essential conditions are present simultaneously:
1. Fuel (combustible substance like candle wax, wood, paper, gas).
2. Oxygen (air supply).
3. Heat (reaching the ignition temperature).
Firefighting Rule: Extinguish a fire by removing any ONE of these three sides!

Figure 5.3: The Fire Triangle • Cut off oxygen (smother with blanket) or cool below ignition (water) to stop fire.

5. Slow Natural Changes: Weathering, Erosion & Life Adaptations

Weathering of Rocks and Soil Formation

Soil is not manufactured in factories; it is born through weathering—a slow natural process spanning thousands of years:

  • Physical Weathering: Water enters micro-cracks in massive boulders. During cold nights, the water freezes into ice, expanding by 9% and shattering rocks into smaller stones and pebbles (a physical change).
  • Chemical Weathering: Rainwater absorbs atmospheric carbon dioxide and sulfur compounds, forming weak natural acids. These mildly acidic waters dissolve rock minerals (like feldspar and limestone), converting hard rock into mineral-rich clay and silt (a chemical change).
  • Erosion & Sedimentation: Rivers, glaciers, and blowing winds transport fine rock sediments across valleys, depositing fertile alluvium in delta plains.
  • Chameleon Colour Change (Biological Reversibility): Chameleons can shift skin colours from green to bright yellow or red! They do this by actively shifting the lattice spacing of microscopic guanine nanocrystals inside specialised skin cells (iridophores), altering the wavelength of reflected light. When relaxed, the crystals return to their resting position, fully reversing the colour!

End-of-Chapter Exercises (Questions 1 to 10)

Question 1: Which of the following statements are characteristics of a physical change? Exercise Q1

Statements Given:

(i) The state of the substance may or may not change.
(ii) A substance with different properties is formed.
(iii) No new substance is formed.
(iv) The substance undergoes a chemical reaction.

Correct Answer: (c) (i) and (iii)

Reason: In a physical change, physical state (solid/liquid/gas) may alter (e.g. melting ice), but no new chemical substance is formed. Statements (ii) and (iv) describe chemical changes.

Question 3: State whether True or False. Correct the False statements. Exercise Q3
  • (i) Melting of wax is necessary for burning a candle. [ TRUE ]
    Reason: Solid wax does not burn directly; capillary action pulls molten liquid wax up the wick where it vaporises into flammable gas that combusts in air.
  • (ii) Collecting water vapour by condensing involves a chemical change. [ FALSE ]
    Correct Statement: Condensing water vapour involves a physical change because gaseous H₂O simply transitions into liquid H₂O with no change in chemical composition.
  • (iii) The process of converting leaves into compost is a chemical change. [ TRUE ]
    Reason: Microorganisms decompose organic cellulose and proteins, creating a brand-new dark organic substance called humus.
  • (iv) Mixing baking soda with lemon juice is a chemical change. [ TRUE ]
    Reason: Citric acid in lemon juice reacts with sodium hydrogen carbonate to produce sodium citrate, water, and brisk effervescence of carbon dioxide gas.
Question 4: Fill in the blanks. Exercise Q4
  1. (i) Nalini observed brown deposits on her cycle handle. The brown deposits are due to rusting, and this is a chemical change.
  2. (ii) Folding a handkerchief is a physical change and can be reversed.
  3. (iii) A chemical process in which a substance reacts with oxygen with evolution of heat is called combustion, and this is a chemical change.
  4. (iv) Magnesium, when burnt in air, produces a substance called magnesium oxide. The substance formed is basic in nature. Burning of magnesium is a chemical change.
Question 5: Are the changes of water to ice and water to steam, physical or chemical? Explain. Exercise Q5

Answer: Both are PHYSICAL changes.

Scientific Explanation:

  • In both processes (freezing of water to ice, and evaporation/boiling of water to steam), only the physical state (solid, liquid, gas) and arrangement of molecules change.
  • No new chemical substance is formed. Every molecule remains H₂O (two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom).
  • Both processes are easily reversible: warming ice produces liquid water, and cooling steam condenses it back into water.
Question 6: Is curdling of milk a physical or chemical change? Justify your statement. Exercise Q6

Answer: Curdling of milk is a CHEMICAL change.

Justification:

  1. Formation of a New Substance: When warm milk is inoculated with a little starter curd, Lactobacillus bacteria ferment the sweet lactose sugar of milk into lactic acid.
  2. Irreversible Texture & Taste Change: The lactic acid coagulates milk proteins (casein), changing sweet liquid milk into a thick, sour curd.
  3. Permanence: Curd cannot be transformed back into fresh raw milk by any physical or chemical means.
Question 7: Natural factors like wind and rain form soil from rocks. Is this change physical or chemical and why? Exercise Q7

Answer: Formation of soil from rocks involves BOTH Physical and Chemical changes!

Scientific Explanation:

  • Physical Aspect: Temperature fluctuations, freeze-thaw cycles, fast-flowing river currents, and howling winds physically pulverise big boulders into smaller rocks, sand, and silt without altering chemical composition.
  • Chemical Aspect: Atmospheric rainwater containing dissolved carbon dioxide and acids reacts chemically with rock minerals (like feldspar and silicates) through hydrolysis and oxidation, converting them into brand-new soft clay minerals and releasing essential plant nutrients.
Question 8: Story analysis: 'Eco-friendly Prithvi'. Choose physical/chemical changes and provide a suitable title. Exercise Q8

Suggested Title: "Prithvi's Green Kitchen: The Cycle of Matter"

Selected Options:

  • Chopping vegetables, peeling potatoes, and cutting fruits → Physical changes (altering size and shape without changing chemical identity).
  • Collecting seeds, fruit and vegetable peels into a clay pot → Physical change (simple mechanical gathering).
  • Materials decomposing due to bacteria and fungi into compost → Chemical change (biological breakdown producing humus and new mineral compounds).
  • Seeds germinating and growing into blooming plants → Chemical change (complex biochemical synthesis of cellulose, sugars, chlorophyll, and floral pigments).

Question 10: Fig. 5.11 experiments (Vinegar + Baking soda; Vinegar + Salt; Lemon + Vinegar; Lemon + Baking soda). In which case(s) did lime water turn milky and why? Exercise Q10

Answer: Lime water will turn milky in Case (a) and Case (d)!

Scientific Explanation:

  • Case (a) [Vinegar + Baking Soda]: Acetic acid reacts with sodium hydrogen carbonate to produce brisk effervescence of carbon dioxide gas (CO₂). When this gas passes into lime water, insoluble calcium carbonate forms, turning it milky white!
  • Case (d) [Lemon Juice + Baking Soda]: Citric acid in lemon juice also reacts with sodium hydrogen carbonate to release carbon dioxide gas (CO₂), which turns lime water milky white!
  • Why Cases (b) and (c) do NOT turn milky:
    • Case (b) [Vinegar + Common Salt]: Acetic acid does not react with sodium chloride; no gas is evolved.
    • Case (c) [Lemon Juice + Vinegar]: Both are acids; no neutralisation occurs, and zero CO₂ gas is produced.

Q1. Which of the following is an example of an irreversible chemical change?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b)
Cooking an egg permanently denatures and cross-links its albumin proteins, forming a new solid structure that can never be converted back into a liquid raw egg.

Q2. When a candle burns, what changes occur?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (d)
Melting of solid wax near the wick is a physical change (can re-solidify). Vaporisation and combustion of wax vapour with oxygen to produce CO₂, water vapour, heat, and light is a chemical change.

Q3. A person throws a heavy woollen blanket over a person whose clothes have caught fire. Which element of the fire triangle is removed?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a)
Wrapping in a thick blanket smothers the flames, cutting off fresh atmospheric oxygen. Without oxygen, the combustion reaction stops immediately.

Q4. Clear lime water turns milky when carbon dioxide gas is passed through it due to the precipitation of:

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c)
Carbon dioxide reacts with calcium hydroxide solution to form solid calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) particles that turn the liquid milky white.

Q5. How does a chameleon change its skin colour, and is this change reversible?

Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a)
Chameleons tune the structural lattice spacing of guanine nanocrystals within iridophores. When calm, the lattice tightens back, reversing the colour completely!

Assertion & Reason Drills

Drill 1: Assertion (A): Rusting of iron is classified as a chemical change.
Reason (R): Iron reacts chemically with atmospheric oxygen and water to form a brand-new reddish-brown compound (hydrated iron oxide). Assertion-Reason
Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Rust has different chemical properties from elemental iron. Once iron turns to rust, it cannot be physically converted back into metallic iron.
Drill 2: Assertion (A): When water boils into steam, it undergoes an irreversible chemical change.
Reason (R): Steam contains hydrogen and oxygen gases that have permanently split apart. Assertion-Reason
Answer: (d) Both Assertion and Reason are completely FALSE!
Boiling is a physical change of state. Steam is gaseous H₂O; cooling condenses it back into liquid water.

Exam Hall Traps & 15 High-Yield Revision Points

Bulletproof your preparation with common exam pitfalls and a rapid 2-minute memory checklist.

Exam Hall Trap #1 • Classifying Burning of a Candle as "Only Physical":
Trap: Writing that candle burning is purely physical because wax melts.
Correction: Burning a candle involves BOTH! The melting of wax is a physical change, but the combustion of wax vapour producing heat, light, and CO₂ is a chemical change!
Exam Hall Trap #2 • Assuming All Chemical Changes are "Instant":
Trap: Believing that chemical reactions always happen in seconds.
Correction: While vinegar + baking soda is instantaneous, rusting of iron and chemical weathering of rocks take days, years, or millennia!

15 Lightning Revision Points for Quick Exam Recap

  1. Physical Change: A change in shape, size, or state where NO new chemical substance is formed.
  2. Chemical Change: A process where one or more brand-new substances with distinct properties are created.
  3. Reversibility: Physical changes are generally reversible; chemical changes are generally irreversible.
  4. Combustion: Rapid chemical reaction of a fuel with oxygen producing heat and light.
  5. Fire Triangle: Three requirements: Fuel + Oxygen + Heat (ignition temperature).
  6. Rusting Equation: 4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃ • xH₂O (hydrated iron oxide).
  7. Rusting Dual Condition: Requires BOTH oxygen (air) and water (moisture) simultaneously.
  8. Lime Water Test: CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃ ↓ (milky precipitate) + H₂O.
  9. Fizzing Reaction: Vinegar + Baking soda produces immediate bubbles of CO₂ gas.
  10. Burning Candle: Exemplifies both physical change (wax melting) and chemical change (wax burning).
  11. Curdling of Milk: Irreversible chemical change driven by lactic acid produced by Lactobacillus.
  12. Dissolving Sugar: Physical change; sugar molecules can be recovered by evaporating water.
  13. Weathering of Rocks: Physical breakdown (frost wedging) and chemical alteration (acid rain) forming soil.
  14. Chameleon Adaptation: Reversible biological change altering nanocrystal spacing in skin iridophores.
  15. Yeast Fermentation: Yeast decomposes sugar anaerobically to produce ethanol and CO₂ gas bubbles.
