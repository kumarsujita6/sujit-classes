7.1 The Triangle Inequality Property

If you take three sticks of lengths 2 cm, 3 cm, and 6 cm, can you build a triangle? Try it with compasses: draw a base of 6 cm. From one end, draw an arc of radius 2 cm. From the other end, draw an arc of radius 3 cm. Even when stretched completely flat towards each other, 2 cm + 3 cm = 5 cm, which cannot even span the 6 cm gap! The arcs never touch!

The Golden Law of Triangle Existence: A triangle can exist if and only if the sum of the lengths of any two sides is strictly greater than the length of the third side:
a + b > c   b + c > a   c + a > b.
Quick Test: Check only the two shortest sides! If (Shortest + Medium) > Longest, the triangle exists!

Figure 7.2: Compass arcs show why 2 cm, 3 cm, 6 cm cannot form a triangle, while 4 cm, 5 cm, 6 cm intersect perfectly.

7.2 Angle Sum Property & Exterior Angle Theorem

Inside any triangle, the sum of all three interior angles is always 180°.
Exterior Angle Theorem: If one side of a triangle is extended, the exterior angle formed is equal to the sum of the two interior opposite angles:

∠ACD (Exterior Angle) = ∠A + ∠B (Interior Opposite Angles)

7.3 Medians and Altitudes

FeatureMedianAltitude
Definition Line connecting a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. Perpendicular line dropped from a vertex to the opposite side.

Activity 1: Existence of Triangles (Page 9)

(a) 10 km, 10 km, and 25 km:
Check two shorter sides: 10 + 10 = 20 km.
Since 20 km < 25 km, no triangle can exist!
(b) 5 mm, 10 mm, and 20 mm:
Check two shorter sides: 5 + 10 = 15 mm.
Since 15 mm < 20 mm, no triangle can exist!
(c) 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm:
Check two shorter sides: 3 + 4 = 7 cm.
Since 7 cm > 5 cm, a triangle exists! (In fact, it is a right-angled triangle since 3² + 4² = 5²).

Activity 2: Missing Angle Calculation (Page 20)

Problem: In ΔABC, ∠B = 50° and ∠C = 70°. Find the measure of angle ∠A.

Rule: ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180° (Angle Sum Property).
Calculation: ∠A + 50° + 70° = 180° ⇒ ∠A + 120° = 180° ⇒ ∠A = 180° − 120° = 60°.
✓ Answer: ∠A = 60°.

Q1. Exterior Angle and Remote Interior Angles

Problem: An exterior angle of a triangle is 115°, and one of the interior opposite angles is 45°. Find the other interior opposite angle and the third interior angle.

Step 1: Exterior Angle = Interior Opposite 1 + Interior Opposite 2
115° = 45° + x ⇒ x = 115° − 45° = 70°.
Step 2: Third interior angle (forming a linear pair with 115°):
180° − 115° = 65°.
Check: 45° + 70° + 65° = 180°. ✓ (Verified!).
✓ Answer: Other interior opposite angle = 70°; third angle = 65°.
Q2. Right-Angled Triangle and Pythagoras Property

Problem: A 15 m long ladder reaches a window 12 m high on a wall. Find the distance of the foot of the ladder from the wall.

Given: Ladder = Hypotenuse (c) = 15 m; Height of wall = Perpendicular (a) = 12 m; Distance from wall = Base (b).
Pythagoras Property: a² + b² = c² ⇒ 12² + b² = 15²
Calculation: 144 + b² = 225 ⇒ b² = 225 − 144 = 81 ⇒ b = √81 = 9 m.
✓ Answer: The foot of the ladder is 9 meters away from the wall.

Q3: The point where the three medians of a triangle intersect is called the:
Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) Centroid. The medians meet at the centroid, which is the physical center of gravity of the triangle.
Q4: In an obtuse-angled triangle, where does the orthocenter (intersection of altitudes) lie?
Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) Outside the triangle. Two of the altitudes fall outside the triangle, so their intersection lies in the exterior.
Q5: In a right triangle with legs 6 cm and 8 cm, what is the length of the hypotenuse?
Teacher Explanation
Correct Answer: (a) 10 cm. By Pythagoras theorem: c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 ⇒ c = √100 = 10 cm.
Section 6

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Essential exam tips and triangle formulas.

Don't Make This Mistake in Geometry Exams:
• Strict Inequality: a + b > c must be strictly GREATER, not ≥! If a + b = c, the three points form a straight line, NOT a triangle!
• Exterior Angle Trap: An exterior angle is equal to the sum of its TWO OPPOSITE interior angles, NOT the adjacent one!
• Pythagoras only in Right Triangles: Never apply a² + b² = c² unless the triangle has a 90° right angle.
Teacher's Triangle Cheat-Sheet:
1. Existence Rule: Shortest + Middle > Longest side.
2. Angle Sum: ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°.
3. Exterior Angle: ∠Ext = ∠Opp1 + ∠Opp2.
4. Median: Connects vertex to opposite midpoint (Centroid).
5. Altitude: Perpendicular from vertex to opposite side (Orthocenter).
