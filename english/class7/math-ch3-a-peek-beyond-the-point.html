3.1 The Need for Smaller Units

Sonu's mother is tightening a screw in a toy. She notices that the length of the screw is more than 2 cm, but less than 3 cm. On looking closely at the millimeter markings, she sees it is 2 centimeters and 4 millimeters. How can we write this in a single number? 2.4 cm!

3.3 Metric Unit Conversions

All metric conversions are naturally based on powers of 10:

• 1 cm = 10 mm ⇒ 1 mm = 1/10 cm = 0.1 cm
• 1 m = 100 cm ⇒ 1 cm = 1/100 m = 0.01 m
• 1 km = 1,000 m ⇒ 1 m = 1/1000 km = 0.001 km
• 1 kg = 1,000 g ⇒ 1 g = 1/1000 kg = 0.001 kg
• ₹1 = 100 paise ⇒ 1 paisa = ₹1/100 = ₹0.01

3.4 Comparing Decimals: The Secret Rule

Common Student Misconception: "0.46 is bigger than 0.5 because 46 is bigger than 5."
Teacher Correction: Never compare decimals by looking at the digits as whole numbers! Write them with the same number of decimal places by adding trailing zeroes:
0.5 = 0.50 (50 hundredths).
0.46 = 0.46 (46 hundredths).
Since 50 hundredths > 46 hundredths, 0.5 > 0.46!

Section 6

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Essential memory checkpoints and test tips.

Don't Make This Mistake in Exams:
• Misaligned Decimal Points: In 12.3 + 4.56, never add 123 and 456! Align the decimal points: 12.30 + 4.56 = 16.86.
• Confusing Tenths and Hundredths: 3 tenths = 0.3, while 3 hundredths = 0.03.
• Trailing Zeroes in Decimals: Adding zeroes to the far right of a decimal does not change its value: 0.4 = 0.40 = 0.400.
Super Summary of Decimals:
1. Tenths: First place after point (1/10 = 0.1).
2. Hundredths: Second place after point (1/100 = 0.01).
3. Thousandths: Third place after point (1/1000 = 0.001).
4. To Compare: Fill trailing zeroes to equalise decimal places, then compare.
5. To Add / Subtract: Line up decimal points in a straight vertical column!
