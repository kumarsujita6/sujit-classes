5.1 Intersecting Lines & Vertically Opposite Angles

When two straight lines cross, they form four angles. Opposite pairs of angles are called vertically opposite angles.

The Proof that Vertically Opposite Angles are Equal:
Let line 1 and line 2 intersect at point O forming angles a, b, c, d in cyclic order.
• Since angles on a straight line add to 180° (Linear Pair): a + b = 180°.
• Similarly: b + c = 180°.
• Subtracting equation 2 from equation 1: (a + b) − (b + c) = 180° − 180° ⇒ a − c = 0 ⇒ a = c!
Hence, vertically opposite angles are always equal.

Activity 2: Testing Parallelism of Lines a, b, c (Figure 5.13, Page 9)

Problem: Line a has angle 75° with a transversal. Line b has corresponding angle 75°. Line c has corresponding angle 72°. Which line is parallel to line a?

Condition: Two lines are parallel if and only if their corresponding angles are equal.
Line a vs. Line b: Corresponding angles are both 75° (equal). Hence, line b || line a.
Line a vs. Line c: Corresponding angles are 75° and 72° (not equal: 75° ≠ 72°). Hence, line c is NOT parallel to line a.
✓ Answer: Line b is parallel to line a because corresponding angles are equal.

Q1. Parallel Lines and Perpendiculars (Fig. 5.34, Page 20)

Problem: In Fig. 5.34, AB is parallel to CD, and CD is parallel to EF. Also, EA is perpendicular to AB. If ∠BEF = 55°, find the values of x and y.

Step 1: Given CD || EF, and BE is a transversal cutting them.
Angles x and ∠BEF are co-interior angles (interior angles on the same side of the transversal).
Therefore, x + 55° = 180° ⇒ x = 180° − 55° = 125°.
Step 2: Given AB || CD, and the same transversal intersects them.
Angles x and y are corresponding angles (F-shape).
Therefore, y = x = 125°.
✓ Answer: x = 125° and y = 125°.
Q2. Complex Angular Pathway: Finding ∠NOP (Fig. 5.35, Page 20)

Problem: In Fig. 5.35, line LM is parallel to PQ. Angle ∠LNO = 40°, ∠N = 96°, and ∠OPQ = 52°. Find the measure of ∠NOP.

Construction: Through point N, draw a line parallel to LM. Through point O, draw another line parallel to LM and PQ.
Angle at N: The alternate interior angle to 40° is 40°. Since total angle at N is 96°, the remaining bottom angle is 96° − 40° = 56°.
Angle at O:
• The top portion of ∠NOP is alternate interior to 56° ⇒ 56°.
• The bottom portion of ∠NOP is alternate interior to ∠OPQ (52°) ⇒ 52°.
Total Angle: ∠NOP = 56° + 52° = 108°.
✓ Answer: ∠NOP = 108°.

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Exam master tips and golden geometric rules.

Don't Make This Mistake in Geometry Exams:
• Equating Co-Interior Angles: Co-interior angles are NOT equal! They are supplementary (add up to 180°). Only corresponding and alternate angles are equal!
• Assuming Lines are Parallel from Appearance: Never assume lines are parallel just because they look parallel. Always look for angle evidence (equal alternate/corresponding angles).
• Straight Line Angle Sum: Angles in a linear pair add to 180°, NOT 360°!
Teacher's Golden Geometry Rules:
1. Vertically Opposite Angles: Formed by an 'X', always equal.
2. F-shape: Corresponding angles are equal.
3. Z-shape: Alternate interior angles are equal.
4. C-shape: Co-interior angles add up to 180°.
5. If any of rules 2, 3, or 4 holds true, the lines MUST be parallel!
