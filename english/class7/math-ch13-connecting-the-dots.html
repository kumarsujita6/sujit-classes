Section 1: Concept Awakening & Visual Intuition

1. The Meaning of "Average": Equal Levelling

Suppose over 5 days, a garden plant produces 7, 3, 5, 8, and 2 Hibiscus flowers. What does the "average" flowers per day really represent?

Imagine stacks of flower tokens of heights 7, 3, 5, 8, 2. If you take tokens from the tall stacks and place them onto the short stacks until every single stack is at the exact same level:

Total Flowers = 7 + 3 + 5 + 8 + 2 = 25 flowers
Divided equally across 5 days = 25 ÷ 5 = 5 flowers per day!

2. Ancient Indian Heritage: The Concept of Samamiti

Centuries before modern statistics, ancient Indian mathematicians developed deep geometric and arithmetic insights into the mean as an equalising balance:

Classical Sanskrit Terms for the Arithmetic Mean
  • Samarajju (equalising measuring rope) • Brahmagupta (628 CE)
  • Samīkaraṇa (making even / levelling) • Mahāvīrācārya (850 CE)
  • Sāmya (fairness, impartial balance) • Śrīpati (1039 CE)
  • Samamiti (equal measure; sama = equal, miti = measure) • Bhāskarācārya (1150 CE) and Gaṇeṣa (1545 CE)

3. "A Mean Foot" • Europe (1500s)

In 16th-century Europe, the standard survey rod was defined as 16 feet. To remove bias caused by individual foot sizes, surveyor Jacob Köbel had 16 random adult men coming out of church stand heel-to-toe in a straight line. Dividing this 16-man line into 16 equal segments created the standard 1-foot ruler—an early practical implementation of the Arithmetic Mean!

Section 2: Core Theory & Statistical Measures

1. Measures of Central Tendency

Measure Formula / Definition Key Strengths Vulnerabilities
Arithmetic Mean (μ) Sum of all observations ÷ Total count (N) Uses every data point; great for total budget and resource planning. Highly sensitive to extreme outliers.
Median Middle value of arranged data (or average of two middle values if N is even). Completely unaffected by extreme outliers; reflects the typical experience. Ignores the actual magnitude of values far from the center.
Range Maximum value − Minimum value Shows total spread and variability at a glance. Depends solely on two extreme points.
"A Mean Decision!": The Door Height Trap
A carpenter designs a house door based on the average height of a family (155 cm). The father is 185 cm tall and the mother is 160 cm tall. Every time the father walks in, he bumps his head!
Lesson: The Mean is NOT always the right tool. For doors, bridges, and ceilings, we must use the Maximum, not the Mean!

2. Visualising Data: Dot Plots

A Dot Plot plots a dot for every occurrence of a value above a number line. It allows you to instantly see:

  • Peaks (Clusters): Where data piles up most densely.
  • Spread (Variability): How tightly clustered or spread out the dots are.
  • Outliers: Lone dots far away from the main cluster.

3. Clustered & Double Bar Graphs

When you want to compare two related categories simultaneously (e.g. Boys vs Girls, Watching vs Participating, Blue Team vs Red Team runs), draw a Double Bar Graph where each category has two side-by-side bars with distinct colors and a clear legend.

Figure it Out (Page 5) • First Steps in Arithmetic Mean

1. Shreyas Ball Bouncing Attempts (6, 2, 9, 5, 4, 6, 3, 5)
1. Given: Data: 6, 2, 9, 5, 4, 6, 3, 5. Total attempts (N) = 8.
2. Formula: Mean = (Sum of bounces) ÷ (Number of attempts).
3. Calculation:
Sum = 6 + 2 + 9 + 5 + 4 + 6 + 3 + 5 = 40
Mean = 40 ÷ 8 = 5 bounces
4. Answer: Shreyas was able to make an average of 5 bounces per attempt.
4. Nikhil vs Sunil 100m Race Training (Who Ran Quicker?)
Nikhil's times (sec): 17, 18, 17, 16, 19, 17, 18 (7 trials)
Sum = 17 + 18 + 17 + 16 + 19 + 17 + 18 = 122
Nikhil's Mean = 122 ÷ 7 ≈ 17.43 seconds
Sunil's times (sec): 20, 18, 18, 17, 16, 16, 17 (7 trials)
Sum = 20 + 18 + 18 + 17 + 16 + 16 + 17 = 122
Sunil's Mean = 122 ÷ 7 ≈ 17.43 seconds
Conclusion: Both friends had the exact same average time of 17.43 seconds! However, Sunil recorded the fastest single trial (16 seconds twice), but also the slowest trial (20 seconds).
5. School Enrolment Over 6 Consecutive Years
Given: 1555, 1670, 1750, 2013, 2040, 2126. Count = 6.
Sum = 1555 + 1670 + 1750 + 2013 + 2040 + 2126 = 11,154
Mean Enrolment = 11,154 ÷ 6 = 1859 students

1 & 2. Onion Prices Median & Sanskruti's Domestic Pets Survey

1. Onion Prices Median:

2. Sanskruti Pets Data: 0, 1, 0, 4, 8, 0, 0, 2, 1, 1, 5, 3, 4, 0, 0, 10, 25, 2, 2, 4 (20 recorded students).

Analysis:
Sum = 72. Mean = 72 ÷ 20 = 3.6 pets.
Sorted: 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 4, 4, 4, 5, 8, 10, 25.
Median = (10th + 11th values) ÷ 2 = (2 + 2) ÷ 2 = 2 pets.

3 & 4. Habra Palm Tree Heights & Water Usage Bounds

3. Palm Tree Heights (29 trees):

Sorted values (feet): 43, 44, 45, 46, 49, 50, 51, 52, 52, 54, 54, 55, 55, 56, 56, 57, 58, 59, 60, 60, 60, 60, 61, 61, 62, 63, 65, 66, 67.
Total Sum = 1645 feet. Count = 29 trees.
Mean = 1645 ÷ 29 ≈ 56.72 feet.
Median (15th value) = 56 feet.
Trees shorter than average (< 56.72 ft): Exactly 15 trees.

4. Water Usage Bounds: Data: 5.6, 8, 3.09, 12.9, 6.5, 12.1, 11.3, 20.5, 7.4.

(a) Can mean or median lie between 25 and 30?
NO! The maximum usage is 20.5 L. The mean and median can NEVER exceed the maximum data value!
(b) Universal Property:

For any dataset: Minimum ≤ Median ≤ Maximum and Minimum ≤ Mean ≤ Maximum. Both representative measures always stay strictly bounded within the data range!

Q2: Player Points Scoring Across 4 Games

(a) Average points scored per game by Player A:

A's scores: 14, 16, 10, 10 ⇒ Mean = (14 + 16 + 10 + 10) ÷ 4 = 50 ÷ 4 = 12.5 points.

(b) Mean for Player C vs Player B:

• Player C: Did not play Game 3. We divide by 3 because Player C only participated in 3 games! Mean = (8 + 11 + 13) ÷ 3 = 32 ÷ 3 ≈ 10.67 points.
• Player B: Played all 4 games and scored 0 in Game 1. We must divide by 4, because 0 is a valid score! Mean = (0 + 8 + 6 + 4) ÷ 4 = 18 ÷ 4 = 4.5 points.

(c) Who is the best performer?

Player A is the best performer with the highest mean score of 12.5 points per game.

Clinic Waiting Room Wait Times
A doctor records the waiting times (in minutes) of 7 patients: 12, 15, 8, 45, 14, 10, 11.
(a) Calculate the Mean and the Median waiting time.
(b) Which measure better describes the typical patient experience, and why?
View Solution
(a) Sum = 12 + 15 + 8 + 45 + 14 + 10 + 11 = 115.
Mean = 115 ÷ 7 ≈ 16.43 minutes.
Sorted: 8, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 45.
Median = 4th value = 12 minutes.

(b) The Median (12 minutes) is a far superior representative because 45 is an outlier (an emergency delay) that unfairly skews the mean upwards.
Assertion & Reason (Measures of Central Tendency)

Assertion (A): The median of any dataset is always one of the numbers present in the dataset.

Reason (R): When there are an even number of observations, the median is calculated by finding the mean of the two middle numbers, which may not be an original observation.

View Solution
Correct Option: Assertion (A) is FALSE, and Reason (R) is TRUE. For example, the median of {2, 4} is (2+4)/2 = 3, which is not in the original dataset!
