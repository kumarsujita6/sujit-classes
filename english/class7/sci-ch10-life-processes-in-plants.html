1. Autotrophic Nutrition & The Photosynthetic Factory

Unlike animals that must search for and consume organic matter, green plants are autotrophs (self-feeders). They are the primary food producers for the entire biosphere.

Definition: Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis is the physiological biochemical process by which green plants manufacture glucose from inorganic raw materials — Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) from the air and Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) from the soil — in the presence of Sunlight trapped by the green pigment Chlorophyll, releasing Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) as a vital byproduct.
The Master Chemical Equation of Photosynthesis
$$6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \mathbf{C_6H_{12}O_6 \text{ (Glucose)}} + \mathbf{6O_2 \uparrow \text{ (Oxygen)}}$$

Word Equation: $\text{Carbon Dioxide} + \text{Water} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \text{Glucose} + \text{Oxygen}$

Raw Materials for Photosynthesis

  1. Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$): Enters the leaves directly from the atmosphere through microscopic surface pores called stomata.
  2. Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$): Absorbed from the soil by root hairs via osmosis, along with dissolved mineral salts (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, magnesium), and transported up the stem through xylem vessels.
  3. Sunlight: Radiant solar energy captured by chlorophyll molecules inside specialised cell organelles called chloroplasts.
  4. Chlorophyll: The green pigment responsible for absorbing blue and red light wavelengths, enabling solar photonic energy to convert into chemical bond energy.

Fate of Synthesized Glucose

  • A portion of the simple glucose is immediately utilized by plant cells during respiration to release metabolic energy for cell division and growth.
  • The surplus glucose is converted into insoluble Starch and stored in storage organs like leaves, stems, roots (e.g. carrots, sweet potatoes), tubers (e.g. potatoes), and seeds (e.g. wheat, rice, peas).

2. Classic Laboratory Verifications of Photosynthesis

Activity 10.2: Testing a Leaf for Starch (The Iodine Decolorization Test)
Step-by-Step Procedure:
  1. Boil in Water: Dip a green leaf in boiling water for 2-3 minutes. (This kills the protoplasm, breaks cell membranes, and stops all enzymatic reactions).
  2. Decolorize in Alcohol Bath: Place the softened leaf in a test tube containing 70% alcohol (ethanol), and immerse this test tube in a beaker of boiling water (a water bath). Caution: Never heat alcohol directly over a naked flame as it is highly inflammable! The alcohol dissolves out all green chlorophyll, turning the leaf pale white or yellowish.
  3. Wash in Warm Water: Rinse the brittle decolorized leaf in warm water to soften its tissues.
  4. Add Iodine Solution: Spread the leaf on a white porcelain tile and add a few drops of dilute iodine solution.
Result: The leaf turns deep blue-black, confirming the synthesis and presence of starch!
Activity 10.3: Chlorophyll is Essential for Photosynthesis (Variegated Leaf Test)
Take a potted plant with variegated leaves (like Croton, Coleus, or Money Plant), where leaves contain green patches alongside white or yellow non-green patches.
• Keep the plant in bright sunlight for 6 hours.
• Pick a leaf, map the green and non-green zones on tracing paper, and perform the iodine starch test.
• Observation: Only the originally green parts turn blue-black! The white/yellow parts turn yellowish-brown with iodine.
• Conclusion: Photosynthesis and starch synthesis occur strictly in the presence of chlorophyll.
Activity 10.4: Carbon Dioxide is Essential for Photosynthesis (The KOH Bell Jar Setup)
Two destarched potted plants, A and B, are placed under identical airtight glass bell jars sealed with vaseline.
• In Jar A: A watch glass containing Potassium Hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$) pellets is placed. (KOH chemically absorbs all $\text{CO}_2$ from the air inside the jar).
• In Jar B: A watch glass with plain water is placed (normal $\text{CO}_2$ present).
• Both jars are exposed to bright sunlight for 4 hours.
• When leaves are tested with iodine: Leaf from Jar B turns deep blue-black; leaf from Jar A fails to turn blue-black!
• Conclusion: Without atmospheric carbon dioxide, photosynthesis is impossible.
Activity 10.5: Release of Oxygen during Photosynthesis
An aquatic plant (such as Hydrilla or Elodea) is submerged in a beaker filled with pond water, covered with an inverted glass funnel. An inverted test tube filled with water is placed over the funnel stem.
• When placed in direct sunlight, steady streams of tiny gas bubbles evolve from the cut stems, rising and displacing water at the top of the test tube.
• When a glowing wooden splinter is introduced into the collected gas, the splinter bursts vigorously into flame!
• Conclusion: The gas evolved is pure Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$).

3. Stomata: The Microscopic Gatekeepers of the Leaf

If leaves are the kitchen of the plant, stomata are its ventilation windows!

Structure of Stomata
Stomata (singular: stoma) are tiny microscopic apertures located primarily on the lower epidermis of leaves.
  • Each stoma is bordered and regulated by two kidney- or bean-shaped living cells called Guard Cells.
  • When guard cells absorb water by osmosis, they swell and become turgid, bowing outward and causing the stomatal pore to OPEN.
  • When guard cells lose water, they become flaccid, straightening and causing the stomatal pore to CLOSE.
  • Dual Functions:
    1. Gaseous Exchange: Influx of $\text{CO}_2$ for photosynthesis and efflux of $\text{O}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$.
    2. Transpiration: Evaporation of surplus water in the form of water vapour.

4. Vascular Transport in Plants: Xylem vs Phloem

Plants do not have a muscular pumping heart or circulating blood, yet tall trees (like the California Redwoods reaching over 100 metres high) lift hundreds of litres of water against gravity every single day! How is this achieved?

Feature Xylem Tissue Phloem Tissue
Nature of Cells Composed largely of dead, thick, lignified hollow tubes (vessels and tracheids). Composed of living cells (sieve tube elements and companion cells).
Substance Transported Water and dissolved inorganic mineral salts (nitrates, phosphates). Soluble organic food (mainly sucrose, amino acids).
Direction of Transport Unidirectional: Strictly UPWARDS from roots → stem → leaves. Bidirectional / Multidirectional: From source (leaves) to sink (roots, buds, fruits).
Driving Mechanism Driven physically by Transpiration Pull (suction force) and root pressure. Driven biologically by active osmotic transport (Translocation).
Activity 10.7: Demonstrating Water Transport via Xylem (The Red Ink Experiment)
A tender herbaceous plant with translucent stems (like Balsam) or a white carnation flower is cut at the base under water and placed in a beaker containing water tinted with red eosin or red fountain pen ink.
• Within a few hours, fine red lines appear along the stem and travel directly into the delicate vein networks of the leaves and white petals!
• A sharp thin transverse cross-section (T.S.) of the stem examined under a microscope reveals that only the xylem vessels are stained red.
• Conclusion: Water is conducted exclusively through xylem vessels.
The Physics of Transpiration Pull
As water constantly evaporates through open stomata into the drier atmosphere (Transpiration), a negative pressure (suction) is created at the top of the continuous column of water inside xylem vessels. Because water molecules have strong mutual attraction (cohesion and adhesion), this continuous unbroken thread of water is hauled upward through the entire height of the plant, much like drinking cold juice through a straw!

5. Do Plants Respire? Yes, 24 Hours a Day!

A widespread student misconception is that "plants breathe in carbon dioxide and breathe out oxygen". In reality, plants carry out aerobic cellular respiration continuously, just like animals!

Cellular Respiration in Plants
$$\text{Glucose } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6) + 6\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \mathbf{Energy \ (ATP)}$$ Plant cells require metabolic energy (ATP) around the clock for nutrient uptake, protein synthesis, cell division, and cellular maintenance. Every living part — leaves, green stems, woody bark (via lenticels), and roots (absorbing soil air) — respires!
Activity 10.8: Demonstration of Respiration in Germinating Seeds
Moist germinating gram (chana) or pea seeds are placed inside an airtight conical flask fitted with a delivery tube leading into a test tube of clear lime water ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$).
• Germinating seeds are growing vigorously and metabolizing at a high rate.
• Within a few hours, the clear lime water turns milky white!
• Conclusion: Actively respiring plant tissues release substantial carbon dioxide gas.

Comparison: Daytime vs Nighttime Gas Exchange

Time of Day Photosynthesis Status Respiration Status Net Atmospheric Gas Exchange
Bright Daytime Occurring at peak rate (utilizing abundant solar energy). Occurring at normal baseline rate. Net $\text{O}_2$ Released, Net $\text{CO}_2$ Consumed. (Photosynthesis rate is 10 to 20 times faster than respiration; the plant consumes all its own respiratory $\text{CO}_2$ plus draws extra from the atmosphere, releasing vast excess $\text{O}_2$).
Nighttime (Darkness) Completely STOPPED (no light photons available). Occurring continuously at normal baseline rate. Net $\text{O}_2$ Consumed, Net $\text{CO}_2$ Released. (The plant behaves exactly like an animal, taking in atmospheric oxygen and discharging carbon dioxide).
Why Elders Advise Against Sleeping Under Large Trees at Night
During the night, in the absence of sunlight, trees perform only cellular respiration and emit substantial amounts of carbon dioxide while consuming oxygen. Sleeping closely underneath a dense tree canopy can lead to breathing stagnant air with elevated $\text{CO}_2$ levels, which can cause headaches, mild suffocation, or restless sleep.
Scientist in Focus: Dr. Kamala Sohonie (1911–1998)
Dr. Kamala Sohonie was the pioneering first Indian woman to earn a Ph.D. in Science from Cambridge University. Her groundbreaking doctoral research focused on plant tissue respiration and discovered that every plant cell contains the respiratory enzyme cytochrome c. Returning to India, she served as Director of the Royal Institute of Science in Bombay and developed the affordable, nutrient-packed beverage Neera (fresh palm sap rich in vitamin C and iron) for malnourished tribal communities.

Question 2: Imagine a situation where all the organisms that carry out photosynthesis on the earth have disappeared. What would be the impact of this on living organisms?

Teacher's Answer If all photosynthetic organisms (green plants, trees, phytoplankton, cyanobacteria) disappeared, it would cause a total catastrophic extinction of nearly all life on Earth:

  1. Collapse of Food Chains: Plants are the universal primary producers. Without photosynthesis, no organic food would be synthesized. Herbivorous animals would starve and die out within weeks; subsequent carnivores and omnivores (including human beings) would starve soon after.
  2. Depletion of Atmospheric Oxygen: Photosynthesis is the sole biological source of free oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) in our atmosphere. As existing organisms, decomposition, and fires consume available oxygen, oxygen levels would drop steadily to zero, suffocating all aerobic life.
  3. Runaway Greenhouse Effect: With no photosynthetic uptake of carbon dioxide, $\text{CO}_2$ released by remaining respiration and decay would accumulate uncontrollably, causing catastrophic global warming and climate chaos.
  4. Conclusion: In short, life as we know it would cease to exist on planet Earth!
Question 3: A potato slice shows the presence of starch with iodine solution. Where does the starch in potatoes come from? Where is the food synthesised in the plant, and how does it reach the potato?

Comprehensive Mechanism

  1. Site of Food Synthesis: Food is originally synthesized in the green leaves of the potato plant through the process of photosynthesis in the form of simple sugar (glucose).
  2. Transport Mechanism: Glucose is converted into soluble sugar (sucrose). It is then loaded into the vascular tissue called phloem. Through the process of translocation, phloem conducts the soluble food downwards from the leaves through the stem to underground modified stem branches called stolons.
  3. Storage as Starch: At the tips of these underground stolons, the excess sucrose is unloaded into specialized storage cells and converted into insoluble, compact starch molecules. The stolons swell up to form the familiar potato tubers!
Question 4: Does the broad and flat structure of leaves make plants more efficient for photosynthesis? Justify your answer.

Verdict & Justification Yes, the broad and flat structure of leaves dramatically enhances photosynthetic efficiency.

Scientific Justification:

  • Maximum Sunlight Interception: A broad, flattened leaf blade (lamina) provides an expansive surface area to capture the maximum amount of incident sunlight (photons).
  • Minimal Diffusion Distance: A flat, thin leaf ensures that solar rays can easily penetrate through to the middle chlorophyll-rich mesophyll cells.
  • Efficient Carbon Dioxide Absorption: The broad lower surface accommodates thousands of stomata per square millimetre, facilitating rapid diffusion of atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ directly into photosynthetic cells without requiring long internal transport pathways.
Question 5: $X$ is broken down using $Y$ to release carbon dioxide, $Z$, and energy. $X + Y \longrightarrow \text{Carbon dioxide} + Z + \text{Energy}$. What do $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ stand for?

Identification This represents the fundamental chemical equation for Aerobic Cellular Respiration:

  • $X$ stands for: Glucose (or food / carbohydrate, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$).
  • $Y$ stands for: Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$).
  • $Z$ stands for: Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

$$\text{Glucose } (X) + \text{Oxygen } (Y) \longrightarrow \text{Carbon dioxide} + \text{Water } (Z) + \text{Energy (ATP)}$$

Question 6: Krishna set up an experiment with two potted plants of same size: one in sunlight, the other in a dark room (Fig. 10.10). Answer: (i) What idea might she be testing? (ii) What are visible differences? (iii) Which leaves confirm the iodine test for starch?

Answers

(i) What idea might she be testing?
Krishna is testing the scientific hypothesis: "Sunlight is an indispensable condition required for photosynthesis and healthy plant growth."

(ii) What are the visible differences in plants in both conditions?
• Plant in Sunlight: Grows vigorously, possesses healthy lush green leaves, a thick sturdy stem, and upright growth.
• Plant in Complete Dark: Displays pale yellow or whitish leaves (chlorophyll degenerates), a thin, spindly, elongated weak stem (etiolation), and starts wilting or drooping due to starvation.

(iii) Leaves of which plant confirm the iodine test for the presence of starch?
Only the leaves of the plant kept in sunlight will confirm the iodine test by turning deep blue-black. The leaves of the plant kept in the dark will remain yellowish-brown because, in the absence of sunlight, photosynthesis could not take place and existing starch was fully consumed during respiration (destarched).

Question 7: Vani believes that 'carbon dioxide is essential for photosynthesis' and sets up four potted plants (Fig. 10.11): (a) Sunlight with CO2, (b) Sunlight without CO2, (c) Dark with CO2, (d) Dark without CO2. Answer (i) to (iv).

Condition Evaluation Photosynthesis requires BOTH sunlight AND carbon dioxide simultaneously:

  • (i) In which plant(s) will starch be formed?
    Plant (a) ONLY. Plant (a) has both necessary conditions: sunlight and carbon dioxide.
  • (ii) In which plant(s) will starch NOT be formed?
    Plants (b), (c), and (d).
    • Plant (b) lacks carbon dioxide.
    • Plant (c) lacks sunlight.
    • Plant (d) lacks both sunlight and carbon dioxide.
  • (iii) In which plant(s) will oxygen be generated?
    Plant (a) ONLY. Oxygen is released as a byproduct only when the light-driven water-splitting reactions of photosynthesis take place.
  • (iv) In which plant(s) will oxygen NOT be generated?
    Plants (b), (c), and (d). Since photosynthesis cannot proceed in these plants, no oxygen will be produced.
Question 8: Ananya filled 4 test tubes with water and added CO2 indicator (Fig. 10.12): Tube A (Snail), Tube B (Water plant), Tube C (Snail + Water plant), Tube D (Only water). What is she trying to find out? How will she know if she is correct?

Investigation Goal Ananya is investigating: "The ecological balance and mutual interdependence between animal respiration and plant photosynthesis with respect to Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) exchange."

Expected Results & Confirmation

  • Tube A (Water + Snail): The snail carries out only respiration, releasing $\text{CO}_2$ continuously. The $\text{CO}_2$ concentration increases significantly, turning the indicator acidic (yellowish).
  • Tube B (Water + Plant in light): The plant carries out both photosynthesis and respiration, but the rate of photosynthesis is far greater, consuming $\text{CO}_2$ rapidly. The $\text{CO}_2$ concentration drops, turning the indicator alkaline (deep purple/magenta).
  • Tube C (Water + Snail + Plant): The $\text{CO}_2$ produced by the respiring snail is immediately utilized by the plant for photosynthesis, while the $\text{O}_2$ released by the plant is used by the snail for respiration! The $\text{CO}_2$ level remains in balance (indicator stays neutral green).
  • Tube D (Water only): Acts as a control setup to prove that without living organisms, the indicator does not change color on its own.
Question 9: Design an experiment to observe if water transportation in plants is quicker in warm or cold conditions.

Experimental Design

  1. Apparatus: Two identical leafy twigs of a white-flowering balsam plant or white carnation, two 250 mL beakers, red fountain pen ink, water, a timer/stopwatch, and a thermometer.
  2. Preparation: Fill both beakers with 150 mL of water and add 5 mL of red ink to each. Cut the cut bases of both twigs under water at an identical 45-degree angle. Place Twig 1 in Beaker 1 and Twig 2 in Beaker 2.
  3. Controlled Variables:
    • Beaker 1 (Warm Condition): Placed in a warm, sunny room or near an incandescent lamp at approximately $32^\circ\text{C}$ to $35^\circ\text{C}$.
    • Beaker 2 (Cold Condition): Placed in a cool, air-conditioned room or near an ice basin at approximately $10^\circ\text{C}$ to $12^\circ\text{C}$.
  4. Observation: Measure the exact time taken for the red ink to visibly travel up the stem and tint the veins of the leaves and white flower petals.
  5. Expected Conclusion: The red color appears significantly faster in the warm condition (Twig 1). Warm temperature increases the rate of stomatal evaporation (transpiration), creating a much stronger transpiration pull that accelerates upward water transport through the xylem!
Question 10: Photosynthesis and respiration are essential to maintain balance in nature. Discuss.

Discussion Photosynthesis and cellular respiration form a closed, self-sustaining biochemical cycle that stabilizes the planet's biosphere:

  • Oxygen-Carbon Dioxide Atmospheric Equilibrium:
    • Animals and respiring plants continuously consume atmospheric oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) and release carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$).
    • Concurrently, photosynthetic plants absorb $\text{CO}_2$ and release $\text{O}_2$ back into the air.
    • This complementary reciprocity maintains the atmospheric concentration of oxygen at roughly $21\%$ and carbon dioxide at $0.04\%$, preventing runaway asphyxiation or global overheating.
  • Energy and Biomass Flow: Photosynthesis captures solar radiation and locks it into high-energy organic bonds (glucose and starch). Respiration unlocks this chemical bond energy, converting it into ATP to fuel all metabolic work in living organisms.
  • Without this perpetual dynamic balance between photosynthesis and respiration, geochemical recycling would collapse, rendering Earth barren.

Exam Hall Readiness: Traps & Lightning Revision

Common Exam Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: "Plants only respire at night."
Wrong! Respiration is a continuous, 24-hour vital process. If a plant stops respiring for even a few minutes, its cells will die from lack of ATP energy. In daytime, respiration happens concurrently with photosynthesis!
Trap 2: Heating alcohol directly over a burner
DANGER! In the starch test, alcohol must NEVER be heated directly over a flame because alcohol vapours are highly flammable and will catch fire instantly. Always use a water bath!
Trap 3: Confusing Xylem and Phloem directionality
Xylem transport is strictly unidirectional (upwards only). Phloem transport is bidirectional (multidirectional), moving nutrients up to growing buds or down to storage roots.
