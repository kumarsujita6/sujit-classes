1. Nutrition in Animals: The Five Universal Stages

Animals are heterotrophs — unlike green plants, they cannot synthesize organic nutrients through photosynthesis. Instead, they ingest complex organic food (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals) that must be biochemically simplified.

1. Ingestion

3. Absorption
→
4. Assimilation
→
5. Egestion
Stage Biological Meaning Primary Human Location
1. Ingestion The physical act of taking food into the body. Mouth (Buccal cavity) using lips, teeth, and tongue.
2. Digestion Biochemical breakdown of complex, insoluble macromolecules into simple, water-soluble micro-molecules. Mouth, Stomach, and Small Intestine (via digestive enzymes).
3. Absorption Diffusion of simple soluble nutrients across intestinal mucosal walls into the blood and lymph circulation. Small Intestine (specifically through millions of micro-villi).
4. Assimilation Uptake and incorporation of absorbed nutrients by body cells for cellular growth, repair, and ATP energy production. All living somatic cells across the entire body.
5. Egestion Discharge or expulsion of undigested, unabsorbed solid waste residues (faeces) from the body. Anus (controlled by the anal sphincter).

2. The Human Digestive System: Step-by-Step Anatomy

The continuous muscular tube running from the mouth to the anus is called the Alimentary Canal (or gastrointestinal tract). It spans roughly 9 metres in length and is assisted by associated digestive glands (salivary glands, liver, gallbladder, and pancreas).

(a) The Buccal Cavity (Mouth)

  • Teeth: Mechanically grind and masticate food into smaller particles.
    • Incisors (8): Chisel-shaped front teeth for biting and cutting.
    • Canines (4): Dagger-shaped sharp teeth for tearing tough food.
    • Premolars (8) & Molars (12): Broad, ridged surface for crushing and grinding food.
  • Tongue: A fleshy muscular organ attached to the floor of the buccal cavity. It manipulates food, mixes it thoroughly with saliva, assists in swallowing, and contains taste buds sensitive to sweet, salty, sour, and bitter tastants.
  • Salivary Glands & Saliva: Secrete watery saliva containing the enzyme Salivary Amylase (ptyalin), which initiates chemical digestion by converting insoluble complex starch into soluble maltose sugar.
Activity 9.1: Enzymatic Action of Saliva on Starch
Procedure: Take two test tubes, A and B.
• In Tube A: Add 1 teaspoon of boiled rice (or rice flour solution).
• In Tube B: Add 1 teaspoon of boiled rice that has been thoroughly chewed for 3 to 5 minutes.
• Add 3-4 mL of water to both and add 2-3 drops of iodine solution.
Observations:
• Tube A (unchewed): Turns intense blue-black, indicating the presence of abundant starch.
• Tube B (chewed): Remains yellowish-brown; NO blue-black color appears!
Scientific Conclusion: Salivary amylase present in saliva broke down the starch in the chewed rice into simple sugars (maltose), which do not give a blue-black color with iodine!

(b) The Oesophagus (Food Pipe)

A 25 cm long muscular tube connecting the throat to the stomach. Food is not pulled down simply by gravity; rather, the muscular walls of the oesophagus undergo rhythmic, alternating waves of contraction and relaxation called Peristalsis, pushing the semi-solid food bolus downward into the stomach. (This is why astronauts can swallow food even in zero-gravity space!).

(c) The Stomach

A thick-walled, elastic, J-shaped muscular sac. The inner mucosal lining of the stomach secretes Gastric Juice, which contains three vital components:

  1. Hydrochloric Acid (HCl): Creates an acidic environment ($ ext{pH } pprox 1.5 - 2.0$) essential for activating pepsinogen into active pepsin, and acts as a chemical barrier by killing harmful bacteria swallowed along with food.
  2. Pepsin: A powerful protein-digesting enzyme that cleaves complex dietary proteins into smaller soluble peptides and peptones.
  3. Mucus: Forms a thick, slimy protective lubricating barrier over the stomach wall, preventing the stomach's own hydrochloric acid and pepsin from corroding and digesting the muscular organ (preventing gastric ulcers).

(d) The Small Intestine & Associated Glands

The longest segment of the alimentary canal (~6 to 7.5 metres), arranged in compact coils. It is the principal site of complete digestion and nutrient absorption.

  • The Liver & Gallbladder: The liver is the largest gland in the human body (reddish-brown). It secretes Bile juice, which is temporarily stored and concentrated in the Gallbladder. Bile contains no enzymes, but performs two critical functions:
    1. Neutralizes acidic chyme arriving from the stomach, turning it alkaline so pancreatic enzymes can function.
    2. Emulsification of Fats: Breaks down huge, insoluble fat globules into minute microscopic droplets, vastly increasing the surface area for lipase enzymes to digest fats.
  • The Pancreas: A cream-coloured gland located beneath the stomach. It secretes Pancreatic Juice containing:
    • Pancreatic Amylase: Digestion of remaining starch → maltose.
    • Trypsin: Cleaves proteins and peptones → peptides and amino acids.
    • Pancreatic Lipase: Breaks down emulsified fat droplets → fatty acids and glycerol.
  • Intestinal Juice: Secreted by intestinal wall glands; completes digestion:
    • Carbohydrates → Glucose
    • Proteins → Amino Acids
    • Fats → Fatty Acids and Glycerol
Structural Marvel: Intestinal Villi
The inner surface of the small intestine is covered with millions of microscopic, finger-like vascular projections called Villi (singular: villus).
• Enormous Surface Area: Villi increase the absorptive surface area of the intestine by more than 30-fold (equivalent to the size of a tennis court!).
• Ultra-thin Epithelium: Single-cell thick surface allowing rapid diffusion of nutrients.
• Rich Blood & Lymph Capillary Network: Absorbed glucose and amino acids enter blood capillaries; fatty acids and glycerol are absorbed into specialized central lymph vessels called lacteals.

(e) The Large Intestine, Rectum, and Anus

Wider in diameter but shorter in length (~1.5 metres). It does not secrete any digestive enzymes. Its primary job is to absorb excess water and mineral salts from unabsorbed, indigestible food residues. The remaining solidified waste (faeces) is temporarily stored in the Rectum until it is periodically expelled through the Anus by the process of egestion (defecation).

3. Digestion in Grass-Eating Animals (Ruminants)

Cows, buffaloes, deer, and goats feed predominantly on grass, which is rich in tough, fibrous cellulose. Humans cannot digest cellulose because our digestive tract lacks the necessary enzymes and symbiotic microbes!

How Ruminants Digest Cellulose
  1. Rapid Ingestion: Ruminants quickly chew grass superficially and swallow it into the largest chamber of their four-chambered stomach, called the Rumen.
  2. Symbiotic Bacterial Fermentation: In the rumen, billions of anaerobic bacteria and ciliate protozoa produce cellulase enzymes that ferment and partially digest cellulose. The partially digested mass is called Cud.
  3. Rumination: When resting, the animal regurgitates cud back into the mouth in small portions and chews it methodically and thoroughly (called chewing the cud or rumination).
  4. Final Digestion: The re-chewed cud passes into the reticulum, omasum, and abomasum (the true stomach, where gastric juices and true enzymatic digestion occur). A large sac-like structure called the caecum between the small and large intestine hosts additional cellulose-fermenting microbes.
Avian Adaptation: Digestion in Birds
Birds lack teeth (to keep body weight minimal for flight). Instead, they have two specialized digestive compartments:
• Crop: An expandable storage pouch at the base of the neck that softens and moistens swallowed whole seeds and grains.
• Gizzard: A thick, heavy muscular organ lined with a tough keratinous plate. Birds swallow tiny stones and grit, which tumble inside the churning muscular gizzard to mechanically grind hard grains into fine meal!

4. Respiration in Animals: Breathing vs Cellular Respiration

Many students make the grave conceptual mistake of treating "breathing" and "respiration" as synonyms. They are fundamentally distinct!

Feature Breathing (External Respiration) Cellular Respiration (Internal Respiration)
Nature of Process Purely a physical, mechanical process of air intake and expulsion. A complex biochemical enzymatic process of food oxidation.
Site of Occurrence Extracellular (takes place in respiratory organs: lungs, gills, skin). Intracellular (takes place inside the cytoplasm and mitochondria of every cell).
Energy Transformation Consumes energy (muscular effort of diaphragm/ribs); generates NO ATP. Releases biological energy, trapping it as ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate).
Enzymes Involved No enzymes required. Involves a sequence of specialized respiratory enzymes.
Cellular Respiration Equation
$$ ext{Glucose } ( ext{C}_6 ext{H}_{12} ext{O}_6) + 6 ext{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6 ext{CO}_2 + 6 ext{H}_2 ext{O} + \mathbf{Energy \ (ATP)}$$ Glucose delivered by the digestive system is oxidized by oxygen delivered by the respiratory system, yielding cellular fuel (ATP).

5. The Human Respiratory System & Mechanics of Breathing

The human respiratory pathway consists of:
Nostrils → Nasal Cavity → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli.

The Protective Design of the Nasal Cavity

Breathing through the nose is far healthier than mouth-breathing because:
1. Fine nasal hair (cilia): Filter and trap coarse airborne dust particles, insect debris, and soot.
2. Sticky mucus lining: Traps microscopic dust, pollen grains, and bacterial spores.
3. Rich superficial blood vessels: Warm and humidify cold, dry incoming air to match internal body core temperature ($37^\circ ext{C}$).

The Mechanics of Inhalation and Exhalation

Component Inhalation (Breathing In) Exhalation (Breathing Out)
Ribcage External intercostal muscles contract → Ribs move UPWARDS and OUTWARDS. Intercostal muscles relax → Ribs move DOWNWARDS and INWARDS.
Diaphragm Diaphragm muscle contracts and moves DOWNWARDS (flattens out). Diaphragm relaxes and arches UPWARDS (returns to dome shape).
Chest Cavity Volume Volume increases significantly. Volume decreases.
Intra-pulmonary Pressure Pressure inside lungs drops below outdoor atmospheric pressure. Pressure inside lungs rises above atmospheric pressure.
Direction of Air Flow Fresh atmospheric air rushes INTO the lungs. $ ext{CO}_2$-rich stale air is forced OUT of the lungs.
Activity 9.2: Working Model of Lungs and Diaphragm
Using a clear plastic bottle with the bottom cut off, a Y-shaped glass/plastic tube with two small balloons attached to the prongs (representing the two lungs), and a stretched thin rubber sheet tied over the open bottom (representing the muscular diaphragm):
• Pulling the rubber sheet downward: Increases internal bottle volume → Air pressure drops → Atmospheric air rushes into the balloons, inflating them (simulating Inhalation).
• Pushing the rubber sheet upward: Decreases internal volume → Air pressure rises → Air is squeezed out of the balloons, deflating them (simulating Exhalation).
Activity 9.3: Lime Water Test for Exhaled Air
When exhaled air is blown through a drinking straw into a test tube containing freshly prepared clear Lime Water ($ ext{Calcium Hydroxide, Ca(OH)}_2$):
• The lime water turns rapidly milky white!
• Chemical Reaction: $$ ext{Ca(OH)}_2 ext{ (aq)} + ext{CO}_2 ext{ (g)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{CaCO}_3 \downarrow ext{ (white precipitate)} + ext{H}_2 ext{O}$$
• Significance: Inhaled air contains only $0.04\% ext{ CO}_2$, whereas exhaled air contains approximately $4.4\% ext{ CO}_2$ (over 100 times more!).

6. Diverse Respiratory Organs Across the Animal Kingdom

Organism Respiratory Organ Adaptation & Mechanism
Fish Gills Thin, feathery gill filaments covered with ultra-thin membranes and dense blood capillaries. Water taken in through the mouth washes over gills; dissolved $ ext{O}_2$ diffuses into blood, and $ ext{CO}_2$ diffuses out.
Earthworm Moist Skin (Cutaneous) Body surface is kept constantly slimy and moist with mucus. Atmospheric oxygen dissolves in the moisture layer and diffuses directly across thin capillary walls into hemoglobin.
Insects (Cockroach) Spiracles & Tracheal Tubes A network of branching internal air pipes (tracheae) opening along the body sides through tiny valves called spiracles. Oxygen is delivered directly to body tissues without using blood!
Frog Skin & Lungs (Dual) Under water and during hibernation, breathes through moist, permeable skin. On land, breathes using simple sac-like lungs and buccopharyngeal cavity.
Birds Lungs with Air Sacs Possess interconnected auxiliary air sacs providing a continuous, unidirectional flow of oxygenated air through lungs during both inhalation and exhalation, supporting high-altitude flight.

The Box-Breathing Technique (Sama Vritti)
Used by deep-sea divers, elite athletes, and yoga practitioners for mental focus and lung capacity:
• Step 1: Inhale slowly through nose for 4 seconds.
• Step 2: Hold breath with full lungs for 4 seconds.
• Step 3: Exhale smoothly through mouth for 4 seconds.
• Step 4: Hold empty lungs for 4 seconds.
Activates the parasympathetic nervous system, lowers heart rate, and enhances alveolar gas diffusion.

Question 3: What is the role of the diaphragm in breathing? (i) To filter the air, (ii) To produce sound, (iii) To help in inhalation and exhalation, (iv) To absorb oxygen.

Correct Option (iii) To help in inhalation and exhalation

Scientific Explanation: The diaphragm is a dome-shaped muscular sheet separating the thoracic and abdominal cavities. During inhalation, it contracts and moves downward (flattens), expanding the chest volume and drawing air into the lungs. During exhalation, it relaxes and curves upward (dome shape), decreasing chest volume and expelling stale air from the lungs.

Question 5: Anil claims to his friend Sanvi that respiration and breathing are the same process. What question(s) can Sanvi ask him to make him understand that he is not correct?

Teacher's Inquiry Approach Sanvi can challenge Anil with the following probing scientific questions:

  1. "Anil, does breathing happen in the lungs or inside every microscopic cell of our kidneys, brain, and muscles?" (Breathing occurs only in respiratory organs, whereas respiration takes place inside every single living cell!).
  2. "Can simply pulling air in and pushing it out generate chemical energy (ATP) to keep our heart beating, or does that require burning glucose with oxygen?" (Breathing does not produce energy; cellular respiration oxidizes glucose to release ATP).
  3. "Do you need complex biochemical enzymes to breathe, or just muscle movements of the ribs and diaphragm?" (Breathing is purely a physical mechanical process; cellular respiration is an enzyme-driven biochemical process).
Question 6: Which of the following statements is correct and why? Anu: We inhale air. Shanu: We inhale oxygen. Tanu: We inhale air rich in oxygen.

Correct Statement Tanu is the most scientifically accurate: "We inhale air rich in oxygen." (Anu's statement is also acceptable in general terms, but Tanu's is precise).

Detailed Scientific Reason:

  • We do not have a selective filter in our nostrils to separate and inhale pure $100\%$ oxygen alone (which disproves Shanu).
  • When we breathe in, we inhale atmospheric air, which is a mixture of gases containing roughly $21\%$ Oxygen, $78\%$ Nitrogen, and $0.04\%$ Carbon dioxide.
  • Since atmospheric air has a higher concentration of oxygen compared to carbon dioxide, it is correctly characterized as "air rich in oxygen".
Question 7: We often sneeze when we inhale a lot of dust-laden air. What can be possible explanations for this?

Scientific Explanation Sneezing is a protective, involuntary reflex action of the respiratory system:

  1. When we inhale air loaded with dust, pollen, smoke, or allergen particles, these foreign irritants bypass the fine nasal hairs and irritate the sensitive mucosal lining of the nasal cavity.
  2. Sensory nerve endings in the nasal lining detect this irritation and transmit an urgent signal to the reflex control center in the brainstem (medulla oblongata).
  3. The brain triggers a sudden, violent, spasmodic contraction of the chest muscles and diaphragm, expelling a high-velocity blast of air through the nose and mouth.
  4. This powerful blast sweeps out the trapped dust particles, foreign irritants, and excess mucus, keeping the delicate lower respiratory tract (windpipe and lungs) clean and unobstructed.
Question 8: Paridhi and Anusha of Grade 7 started running for their morning workout. After they completed their running, they counted their breaths per minute. Anusha was breathing faster than Paridhi. Provide at least two possible explanations for why Anusha was breathing faster than Paridhi.

Two Plausible Explanations

  1. Difference in Running Intensity / Physical Exertion: Anusha might have run at a higher speed, covered a greater distance, or sprinted aggressively uphill compared to Paridhi. Higher muscular exertion requires muscle cells to break down glucose faster, causing high oxygen consumption and rapid $ ext{CO}_2$ buildup, which triggers the brain to accelerate breathing rate to flush out $ ext{CO}_2$ and repay the "oxygen debt".
  2. Difference in Aerobic Fitness & Vital Lung Capacity: Paridhi might engage in regular sports, jogging, or yoga, giving her a higher vital lung capacity and more efficient cardiovascular conditioning. A well-trained athlete takes deeper, more oxygen-rich breaths, enabling her body to satisfy its oxygen demand with fewer breaths per minute, whereas a less conditioned runner must breathe much faster to deliver the same volume of oxygen.
Question 9: Yadu took two test tubes, A and B, added rice flour in water to both, and added saliva to Tube B only. After 35-45 min, he added iodine solution to both. Tube A turned dark blue-black; Tube B stayed brown (Fig. 9.15). What do you think he wants to test?

Scientific Objective Yadu wants to test and investigate: "The digestive action of human saliva (specifically the enzyme salivary amylase) on complex starch."

Deduction from Results:

  • In Test Tube A (rice flour without saliva), the starch remains completely intact and unhydrolyzed, giving an intense blue-black coloration upon the addition of iodine.
  • In Test Tube B (rice flour incubated with saliva for 40 minutes), the enzyme salivary amylase broke down the starch into simple, soluble sugars (maltose). Since maltose does not react with iodine, Tube B remains yellowish-brown.
Question 10: Rakshita took two clean test tubes, A and B, with lime water (Fig. 9.16). In Tube A, surrounding atmospheric air was sucked/bubbled through, while in Tube B, exhaled air was blown through a pipe. What is she trying to investigate? How can she confirm her findings?

Investigation Goal Rakshita is investigating: "Whether exhaled air contains a substantially higher concentration of Carbon Dioxide ($ ext{CO}_2$) compared to normal atmospheric (inhaled) air."

Confirmation of Findings

  • In Tube B (Exhaled air blown): The clear lime water ($ ext{Ca(OH)}_2$) turns distinctly milky and cloudy almost immediately (within 10-20 seconds). This confirms that exhaled air contains an abundant amount of carbon dioxide (~$4.4\%$), which reacts with lime water to precipitate insoluble white calcium carbonate ($ ext{CaCO}_3$).
  • In Tube A (Atmospheric air passed): The lime water remains largely clear and transparent, or turns very faintly cloudy only after prolonged bubbling, confirming that ambient inhaled air contains only a trace amount of carbon dioxide (~$0.04\%$).

Section 6

Exam Hall Readiness: Traps & Lightning Revision

Common Exam Traps & Misconceptions

Trap 1: "Digestion of all food takes place in the stomach."
Wrong! In the mouth, only starch is partially broken down. In the stomach, only proteins undergo initial digestion. The actual complete digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats happens in the Small Intestine!
Trap 2: "Bile digests fats using enzymes."
Wrong! Bile contains NO enzymes. It acts purely through emulsification (physically breaking big fat blobs into tiny droplets) and creating an alkaline pH so that pancreatic lipase can chemically digest them.
Trap 3: "During exhalation, the diaphragm moves down."
Wrong! The diaphragm relaxes and curves UPWARDS into a dome shape during exhalation, compressing the thoracic cavity and pushing air out.

15 Lightning Revision Key Takeaways

  1. 5 stages of animal nutrition: Ingestion, Digestion, Absorption, Assimilation, Egestion.
  2. Salivary amylase breaks starch into maltose; unchewed food turns blue-black with iodine.
  3. Peristalsis is the wave-like muscular contraction moving food along the alimentary canal.
  4. Stomach secretes HCl (kills bacteria, acidic pH), pepsin (digests protein), and mucus (protects lining).
  5. Bile from liver emulsifies fats; Pancreatic juice digests starch, proteins, and fats.
  6. Complete digestion occurs in the small intestine, producing glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids.
  7. Villi and microvilli in the small intestine massively expand surface area for absorption.
  8. Large intestine absorbs water and mineral salts, forming solid faeces.
  9. Ruminants possess a rumen with symbiotic bacteria to ferment plant cellulose (cud).
  10. Birds lack teeth; their muscular gizzard grinds hard seeds with swallowed grit.
  11. Breathing is mechanical gas exchange; cellular respiration is enzymatic ATP energy release.
  12. Inhalation: Diaphragm flattens (moves down), ribs move up and out → lungs expand.
  13. Exhalation: Diaphragm domes up, ribs move down and in → air pushed out.
  14. Exhaled air contains ~4.4% CO2, turning clear lime water milky white (CaCO3 precipitate).
  15. Fish breathe through gills; earthworms through moist skin; insects through tracheal spiracles.
