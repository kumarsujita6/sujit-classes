Deep-Dive Core Concepts & Teacher Explanations

The 4 valid congruence criteria vs. invalid conditions explained with visual diagrams.

9.1 Correspondence and Notation

If triangle ABC is congruent to triangle PQR:
Symbol: ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR.
This simple statement contains six separate mathematical equalities:

  • Corresponding Vertices: A ↔ P, B ↔ Q, C ↔ R.
  • Corresponding Sides: AB = PQ, BC = QR, CA = RP.
  • Corresponding Angles: ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R.
Order Matters! If A corresponds to P, writing ΔABC ≅ ΔQPR is completely false! The letters must match in the exact same sequence.

Figure 9.2: The four pillars of triangle congruence.

9.2 Why AAA and SSA are Not Valid Criteria

  • AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle): An equilateral triangle with side 2 cm has angles 60°, 60°, 60°. Another equilateral triangle with side 10 cm also has angles 60°, 60°, 60°. They have the same shape, but vastly different sizes! Thus, AAA guarantees similarity, NOT congruence.
  • SSA (Side-Side-Angle): When the angle is not enclosed between the two sides, swinging the second arm can intersect the third line in two different locations, forming two non-congruent triangles!

Activity 1: Diagonals of a Rectangle (Page 8)

Problem: In rectangle ABCD, diagonal BD divides the rectangle into two triangles: ΔABD and ΔCDB. Prove they are congruent and write the correct correspondence.

Given: Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal: AB = CD, and AD = CB.
Third Side: Diagonal BD is common to both: BD = DB.
Congruence: By SSS Congruence Criterion, ΔABD ≅ ΔCDB.
Correspondence: Vertex A ↔ C, Vertex B ↔ D, Vertex D ↔ B.
✓ Conclusion: ΔABD ≅ ΔCDB by SSS.

Activity 2: Midpoint Intersection Proof (Page 13)

Problem: Line segments AD and BC bisect each other at point O. Prove that ΔAOB ≅ ΔDOC.

Side 1: AO = OD (given, O is midpoint of AD).
Angle: ∠AOB = ∠DOC (vertically opposite angles are equal).
Side 2: BO = OC (given, O is midpoint of BC).
Criterion: Two sides and the included angle match ⇒ SAS Criterion!
✓ Answer: ΔAOB ≅ ΔDOC by SAS. (Consequently, AB = CD and AB || CD).

Q2. Isosceles Triangle Base Angle Theorem

Theorem: Angles opposite to equal sides of an isosceles triangle are equal.

Given: ΔABC with AB = AC.
Construction: Draw angle bisector AD of ∠A, meeting BC at D.
Proof: In ΔABD and ΔACD:
• AB = AC (Given)
• ∠BAD = ∠CAD (AD bisects ∠A)
• AD = AD (Common side)
Therefore, ΔABD ≅ ΔACD by SAS Criterion!
CPCTC: Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent ⇒ ∠B = ∠C.
✓ Proved: ∠B = ∠C.

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Exam master tips and congruence criteria review.

Don't Make This Mistake in Exams:
• Non-Included Angle in SAS: The angle MUST be trapped strictly between the two given sides. If it is elsewhere, it is SSA (invalid!).
• Scrambled Vertex Order: Writing ΔABC ≅ ΔRPQ when A corresponds to P is marked wrong.
• Equal Area ≠ Congruence: Two triangles can have the exact same area (e.g. Area = 12 cm²) but completely different shapes!
Teacher's Congruence Memory Sheet:
1. SSS: 3 sides match.
2. SAS: 2 sides and included angle match.
3. ASA: 2 angles and included side match.
4. RHS: Right angle, Hypotenuse, and 1 leg match.
5. CPCTC: Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent!
