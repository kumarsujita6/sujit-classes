4.1 The Notion of Letter-Numbers (Variables)

A letter-number (variable) is a letter that stands in place of a number whose value can change or vary.
For example:

  • Aftab's age = a years.
  • Shabnam is 3 years older ⇒ Shabnam's age = a + 3 years.
  • If Aftab is 10 ⇒ Shabnam is 10 + 3 = 13.
  • If Aftab is 25 ⇒ Shabnam is 25 + 3 = 28. The rule a + 3 holds true for every year!

4.2 Omitting the Multiplication Sign

In algebra, we write 4 × x simply as 4x, and a × b as ab. Why? Because the symbol ‘×’ looks dangerously identical to the letter ‘x’!
• 1 × x is written as x (not 1x).
• −1 × y is written as −y.

4.3 Like Terms vs. Unlike Terms

The Fruit Basket Rule of Algebra:
• Like Terms: Have the exact same variables with the same powers. (e.g., 3 apples + 5 apples = 8 apples ⇒ 3x + 5x = 8x).
• Unlike Terms: Have different variables. (e.g., 3 apples + 4 bananas CANNOT be combined into 7 applenanas! ⇒ 3x + 4y remains 3x + 4y).

Activity 1: Perimeter Formulas (Page 4)

(a) Equilateral Triangle of side s: All 3 sides are equal to s.
Perimeter = s + s + s = 3s.
(b) Regular Pentagon of side s: All 5 sides are equal to s.
Perimeter = s + s + s + s + s = 5s.
(c) Regular Hexagon of side s: All 6 sides are equal to s.
Perimeter = 6 × s = 6s.

Activity 2: Expression Simplification Drill (Page 14)

(a) p + p + p + p = 4p
(b) p + q + p − q = (p + p) + (q − q) = 2p
(c) p − q + p − q = (p + p) − (q + q) = 2p − 2q
(d) p + q − p + q = (p − p) + (q + q) = 2q
(e) p + q − (p + q) = p + q − p − q = 0
(f) 2d − d − (d − c) = 2d − d − d + c = c

Activity 3: The Snail in the Deep Well (Page 35)

Problem: A snail climbs up u cm during the day, and slips down d cm at night. This continues for 10 days and nights.

Part (a): Write an expression for the net distance climbed in 10 days and nights:
Net gain each 24-hr day = (u − d) cm.
In 10 days = 10(u − d) cm.
Part (b): What can we say if d > u?
If d > u, then (u − d) is negative. The snail slips down further each day and ends up below its starting level!
✓ Answer: (a) 10(u − d) cm; (b) Snail moves downward below starting mark.

Q1. Food Bill at the Restaurant (Page 22)

Problem: One plate of Jowar roti costs ₹30 and one plate of Pulao costs ₹20. If x plates of Jowar roti and y plates of Pulao are ordered, write an expression for the total bill.

Cost of Jowar roti: 30 × x = ₹30x
Cost of Pulao: 20 × y = ₹20y
Total Bill: 30x + 20y
✓ Expression for Total Bill: ₹(30x + 20y).
Q2. Matchstick Squares Rule (Page 24)

Problem: How many matchsticks are required to make 10 connected squares in a row? How many for w squares?

Pattern:
1 square = 4 sticks = 3(1) + 1
2 squares = 7 sticks = 3(2) + 1
3 squares = 10 sticks = 3(3) + 1
General Formula: For w squares, required sticks = 3w + 1.
For 10 Squares: 3(10) + 1 = 30 + 1 = 31 sticks.
✓ Answer: 31 sticks for 10 squares; 3w + 1 for w squares.
Q3. Simplifying (4x + 3y) − (3x + 4y) (Page 14)
Step 1: Open the brackets. The minus sign applies to both terms inside the second bracket:
(4x + 3y) − (3x + 4y) = 4x + 3y − 3x − 4y.
Step 2: Group like terms together:
= (4x − 3x) + (3y − 4y).
Step 3: Simplify coefficients:
= 1x − 1y = x − y.
✓ Final Simplified Form: x − y.

Quick Revision Notes & Common Pitfalls

Master tips to avoid losing marks in algebra tests.

Don't Make This Mistake in Exams:
• Illegal Addition of Unlike Terms: Writing 3x + 4 = 7x is a deadly error! A variable term cannot be added to a constant number.
• Omission of Brackets on Subtraction: When subtracting (x + y), forgetting the bracket gives −x + y instead of the correct −x − y.
• Confusing Powers with Multiples: x + x = 2x, while x × x = x²!
Super Summary of Letter-Numbers:
1. Variable: A letter representing any number.
2. Term: Number × variable part (e.g. 5x).
3. Only Like Terms can be combined: Add or subtract their numerical coefficients.
4. Formulas: General patterns expressed with variables (e.g. Perimeter of square = 4s).
