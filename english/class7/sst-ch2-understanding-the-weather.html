Chapter 2: Understanding the Weather

Atmospheric science made simple: decoding temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind systems, atmospheric pressure, and the science of weather forecasting.

Reading Time: 28 mins Full NCERT Solutions Meteorological Instruments
01

Visual Mind Map & Chapter Overview

Have you ever woken up on a chilly winter morning shivering and looking for a woollen sweater, only to find yourself sweating under a hot afternoon sun just a few hours later? Or have you planned an outdoor cricket match with your friends, only to have sudden grey storm clouds ruin your game?

These rapid, hour-to-hour and day-to-day changes in the air around us are what we call weather. In this chapter, we discover the five primary elements that create weather, the scientific instruments used to measure them, and why modern weather forecasting saves thousands of lives every year.

02

Exhaustive Concept Explanations

1. What is Weather? The Troposphere Theatre

Weather is defined as the state or condition of the atmosphere at a specific location over a short period of time (such as an hour, a day, or a week). It is dynamic and can change within minutes.

All human beings live at the bottom of an ocean of air called the atmosphere. The lowest layer of this atmosphere is the Troposphere (extending from ground level up to roughly 8 km at the poles and 18 km at the equator).

  • Over 75% of the total mass of the atmosphere and almost 99% of all its water vapour is packed inside the troposphere.
  • Therefore, practically all the clouds, rain, snow, hail, lightning, and winds that make up our daily weather happen exclusively inside this lowest layer!

2. Temperature: The Warmth of Our World

Temperature is the measure of how hot or cold the ambient (surrounding) air is. It depends directly on solar radiation (insolation) arriving from the Sun.

  • Unit: Measured in degrees Celsius (°C) in India (and in degrees Fahrenheit, °F, in some countries).
  • Maximum-Minimum Thermometer: The highest temperature of the day usually occurs in the early afternoon (between 2:00 PM and 4:00 PM) after the ground has soaked in hours of sunlight. The lowest temperature occurs in the early morning just before sunrise. A special U-shaped instrument called a Maximum-Minimum Thermometer (Six's Thermometer) contains small metal indices that record both values automatically.
  • Diurnal Range of Temperature: The mathematical difference between the day's maximum temperature and minimum temperature:
    Diurnal Range = Maximum Temperature − Minimum Temperature.
Ambient Temperature vs Human Body Temperature

Never confuse ambient air temperature with your body temperature! Normal human body temperature is around 37°C (98.6°F). If the ambient outdoor air temperature reaches 37°C on a summer afternoon, you feel scorching hot because your body struggles to shed its metabolic heat into the warm air!

3. Precipitation & The Rain Gauge

When water evaporates from rivers, lakes, and oceans, it rises into the cool upper troposphere and condenses into tiny water droplets or ice crystals, forming clouds. When these droplets grow too heavy to float in the air, gravity pulls them down to Earth as precipitation.

Precipitation takes many forms: rain, drizzle, snowfall, sleet (frozen rain), and hailstones (hard balls of layered ice).

Figure 2.2: Cross-sectional diagram of a Standard Symons Rain Gauge.

Rules for Setting Up a Class Rain Gauge

To measure true rainfall accurately, scientists follow strict site rules:

  1. Open Ground: It must be placed in a wide open space, far away from trees, roof overhangs, or walls that could block raindrops or drip extra water into the funnel.
  2. Raised Platform (approx. 30 cm): The rim of the funnel is kept about 30 cm above ground level so rainwater splashing off the mud or grass does not bounce into the jar.
  3. Firm & Level: The gauge must sit perfectly vertical on a level concrete base so it does not tilt in heavy wind.

4. Atmospheric Pressure: The Weight of Air

Did you know that air has weight? Although air seems invisible and weightless, a 1-square-metre column of air reaching from sea level all the way to outer space weighs approximately 10 tonnes!

Atmospheric Pressure is the force exerted by this weight of air pressing down on a unit area of Earth's surface.

  • Instrument: Measured using a Barometer (mercury barometer or dial-based aneroid barometer).
  • Unit: Expressed in millibars (mb) or hectopascals (hPa). Standard sea-level pressure is about 1013.25 mb.
  • Why Does Pressure Drop with Altitude? As you climb higher up a mountain, there is less air above you. The air molecules are spread out, meaning air density and air pressure drop sharply. At the summit of Mount Everest, air pressure is only one-third of sea level! That is why mountaineers and tourists landing in Leh (Ladakh) feel breathless and must rest for 48 hours to acclimatise.

5. Wind: Air on the Move

Air never stays still when there is a pressure difference. Wind is simply the movement of air from areas of high pressure to areas of low pressure.

To understand wind, meteorologists measure two fundamental properties:

  1. Wind Direction: The direction from which the wind blows. It is measured using a Wind Vane (an arrow that swivels on a vertical spindle) or a Wind Sock (a conical fabric tube seen at airports to tell pilots runway wind direction).
    Important Rule: If a wind blows from the east towards the west, it is called an Easterly wind!
  2. Wind Speed: How fast the air is travelling. It is measured using an Anemometer, which has three or four hemispherical cups that rotate around a vertical shaft. Stronger winds spin the cups faster, generating an electronic reading in kilometres per hour (km/h) or knots.
North India's Summer Scorcher: The Loo

In the months of May and June, a powerful, dry, and scorching local wind blows across the Northern Plains from the Thar Desert. Known as the Loo, its temperatures frequently reach 45°C to 48°C. Direct exposure can cause severe dehydration and deadly heatstroke!

6. Humidity: Moisture in the Air

Humidity refers to the concentration of invisible water vapour present in the atmosphere.

  • Relative Humidity (RH): The percentage of water vapour actually present in the air compared to the maximum amount the air could hold at that specific temperature.
    RH = (Actual Water Vapour / Maximum Capacity) × 100%.
  • Warm Air Holds More Water: Warm air expands and can hold vastly more water vapour than cold air. When humid warm air cools down, it reaches 100% saturation (the dew point), forcing excess vapour to condense into dew, fog, or clouds.
  • Why Do Humid Days Feel So Sticky? On a dry day in Jaipur, your sweat evaporates in seconds, cooling your skin down comfortably. But on a humid monsoon day in Mumbai or Cuttack with 85% humidity, the air is already saturated with water vapour. Your sweat cannot evaporate, leaving your clothes soaked and your body feeling uncomfortably hot and sticky!
  • Instrument: Measured using a Hygrometer (often a Psychrometer with two thermometers: a dry bulb measuring ambient air temperature, and a wet bulb wrapped in a damp muslin wick).

7. Weather Stations & The India Meteorological Department

To collect round-the-clock data, meteorological agencies establish Weather Stations across the country.

  • Stevenson Screen: A standard white-painted wooden box with louvered (slatted) sides mounted on four legs 1.25 metres above the ground. The white colour reflects solar heat, and the slats allow free air circulation while keeping the thermometers inside protected from direct sunlight, rain, and wind blasts.
  • Automatic Weather Stations (AWS): Solar-powered robotic towers equipped with electronic sensors that automatically record temperature, pressure, wind, and rain, transmitting data via satellite every 15 minutes. In 2023, India's NDMA installed an AWS beside a high glacial lake in Sikkim at 4,800 metres to detect sudden glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF)!
  • India Meteorological Department (IMD): Founded in 1875, IMD is India's premier national weather agency. Its ancient Sanskrit motto is "Adityat Jayate Vrishti" (meaning: "From the Sun arises rain").
Why Weather Forecasting Saves Lives

1. Farmers: Helps them know when to sow seeds, spray fertilizers, or harvest ripe crops before hailstorms ruin them.

2. Fishermen & Ships: Provides early warnings about rough seas, gale-force winds, and approaching cyclones so boats do not venture out.

3. Aviation: Aircraft pilots rely on wind speed, turbulence, visibility, and thunderstorm reports to take off and land safely.

4. Disaster Preparedness: Supercyclones in the Bay of Bengal can be tracked days in advance by radar and satellite, enabling district administrations to safely evacuate hundreds of thousands of coastal villagers.

Rapid Revision Fact & Technical Term Bank

1. Weather
The day-to-day atmospheric conditions of a specific place at a specific time regarding temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind, and pressure.
2. Troposphere
The lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere (0–18 km) containing 75% of atmospheric mass and 99% of moisture, where all weather phenomena occur.
3. Diurnal Range
The difference between the maximum temperature and the minimum temperature recorded during a single 24-hour day.
4. Precipitation
Any form of liquid or solid water particles that condense in the atmosphere and fall to the ground (rain, snow, sleet, hail).
5. Rain Gauge
A meteorological instrument consisting of a sharp-rimmed funnel and graduated inner cylinder used to measure precipitation in millimetres.
6. Barometer
An instrument designed to measure atmospheric pressure in millibars (mb) or hectopascals (hPa).
7. Anemometer
An instrument with rotating cups used to measure the velocity (speed) of the wind in km/h or knots.
8. Relative Humidity
The ratio of the amount of water vapour present in the air to the maximum amount the air can hold at that temperature, given as a percentage (%).
9. Hygrometer
An instrument (such as a wet-and-dry bulb psychrometer) used to measure the humidity or moisture content of air.
10. Stevenson Screen
A white-painted, louvered wooden shelter that houses delicate meteorological thermometers, shielding them from direct sun while allowing free air flow.

Complete NCERT In-Text Activities & Exercise Solutions

In-Text Activity (Page 4): Talk to elders in your neighbourhood and ask them how they predict weather without instruments.

Traditional Weather Signs Observed by Elders:

  • Ants Carrying Eggs: When black ants are seen moving their eggs to higher, dry ground in large lines, villagers know that heavy rain is coming within hours.
  • Halo around Moon/Sun: A hazy circle or halo around the moon indicates high cirrus clouds containing ice crystals, which often heralds an approaching storm.
  • Frogs Croaking Loudly: Frogs croak intensely when humidity rises before the first monsoon shower because moist air keeps their permeable skin damp.
  • Dragonflies Flying Low: Rising air humidity makes small insects' wings heavy with moisture, forcing them and the dragonflies chasing them to fly close to the ground.
  • Traditional Sayings: In Hindi: "Titli ude aakash, to barsat ki aas" (butterflies flying low means rain is near); In Odia: "Bhadrava megha, nishthe barsha" (dense dark thunderheads mean certain downpours).
In-Text Activity (Page 7): Chart of Temperatures of a city in MP — Maximum 42°C, Minimum 24°C. Calculate the range.

Answer:

  • Maximum Temperature Recorded: 42°C
  • Minimum Temperature Recorded: 24°C
  • Calculation of Diurnal Range:
    Range = Maximum Temperature − Minimum Temperature
    Range = 42°C − 24°C = 18°C.
In-Text Activity (Page 15): Study the IMD Map of 19 May 2024. Which states had heatwave warnings and what were the causes for warnings in Tripura and Lakshadweep?

Answer:

  • Heatwave Warnings: States in northwestern and central India—including Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, and Madhya Pradesh—showed red and orange heatwave alerts as temperatures soared above 45°C under hot dry winds.
  • Cause for Warning in Tripura: Severe pre-monsoon heavy thunderstorms, lightning, and squally winds caused by moisture-laden Bay of Bengal winds meeting hot continental air.
  • Cause for Warning in Lakshadweep: High squally winds, rough sea conditions, and early pre-monsoon rain bands in the Arabian Sea, advising fishermen not to venture into deep sea.

Exercise Q2: Jyotsna is deciding what clothes to pack for Mumbai in June. Forecast: 29°C and 84% humidity. What would be your advice to her?

Teacher's Advice to Jyotsna:

  1. Light, Breathable Cotton Clothes: Although 29°C sounds moderate, an 84% relative humidity means the air is almost completely saturated with water vapour. Sweat will evaporate very slowly, causing heavy perspiration. Pure, loose-fitting cotton or linen clothes will absorb sweat and allow air to circulate.
  2. Strictly Avoid Synthetic Fabrics: She should avoid tight polyester, nylon, or silk clothes because they trap heat and sweat against the skin, causing severe itching and prickly heat rash.
  3. Rain Protection Essentials: June is the start of the heavy South-West Monsoon in Mumbai! She must carry a sturdy umbrella, a lightweight waterproof raincoat, and quick-drying waterproof footwear (like rubber sandals).
  4. Hydration: Carry a reusable water bottle and oral rehydration drinks because the heavy sweating can cause loss of electrolytes.
Exercise Q3: Setting up a rain gauge. Options: (1) Vegetable garden, (2) Terrace, (3) Open ground with elevated platform, (4) Compound wall, (5) Verandah. Finalise the site and give reasons.

Final Choice: Option 3 — Open ground with an elevated platform.

Scientific Reasons for Our Decision:

  • Zero Obstruction: An open ground ensures that no tall trees, building walls, or utility poles block raindrops from falling straight into the funnel rim during windy rain.
  • Elevated Platform (30 cm high): Placing the gauge on a 30 cm raised masonry platform prevents rainwater that hits the muddy ground from splashing back into the funnel, which would give a false, exaggerated rainfall reading.
  • Why other options were rejected:
    • Vegetable garden: Plant leaves and garden bushes might overhang or grow over the funnel.
    • Terrace: Strong swirling rooftop wind currents distort raindrop trajectories, leading to inaccurate readings.
    • Compound wall: High risk of wind turbulence and the gauge falling or getting knocked over.
    • Verandah: The roof of the verandah would block most of the falling rain entirely!

Q1. In which atmospheric layer do almost all clouds, rain, snow, and weather phenomena take place?

Teacher's Concept Explanation
Correct Answer: (a) The troposphere (the lowest layer, 0–18 km) holds 75% of atmospheric mass and almost all water vapour, making it the active engine for all clouds and weather.

Q2. If a wind is blowing from the south-west towards the north-east, what is this wind scientifically called?

Teacher's Concept Explanation
Correct Answer: (b) In meteorology, wind is always named after the direction from which it originates! A wind blowing from the south-west is a South-westerly wind (e.g., India's South-West Monsoon).

Q3. Why is the Stevenson Screen painted bright white with slatted (louvered) wooden sides?

Teacher's Concept Explanation
Correct Answer: (c) Thermometers must measure true shade ambient air temperature. White paint prevents the shelter from heating up, while louvered slats allow outdoor air to circulate freely.

Q4. Why does atmospheric pressure decrease as we climb higher up into the mountains?

Teacher's Concept Explanation
Correct Answer: (a) Air pressure is the weight of the air column above. At high elevations, the column of air above is shorter and less dense, so the pressure is much lower.

Q5. What is the motto of the India Meteorological Department (IMD), established in 1875?

Teacher's Concept Explanation
Correct Answer: (d) From Manusmriti, "Adityat Jayate Vrishti" emphasizes the scientific truth that solar heat drives evaporation and creates all rain!

Exam Hall Traps & 15 High-Yield Revision Points

Common Exam Hall Traps to Avoid!
  • Trap 1: Confusing Weather with Climate: Weather is the atmospheric condition of a day or week. Climate is the average weather pattern calculated over at least 30 to 35 years!
  • Trap 2: Wind Naming Confusion: Always remember: wind is named after where it comes from, NOT where it is going! A wind blowing from the North is a Northerly wind.
  • Trap 3: Humidity vs Temperature: 85% humidity does not mean the temperature is 85°C! Relative humidity is a percentage of saturation. A coastal city can be 28°C and have 90% humidity.
  • Trap 4: Rain Gauge Location: Never place a rain gauge next to a building wall or under a tree—overhangs drip extra water and walls block rain, giving totally incorrect measurements.

15 High-Yield Revision Points

  1. Weather Definition: Short-term atmospheric condition of a specific place at a specific moment.
  2. Troposphere: Lowest 0–18 km layer where 99% of atmospheric water vapour exists and all weather happens.
  3. Five Weather Elements: Temperature, Atmospheric Pressure, Wind, Humidity, and Precipitation.
  4. Diurnal Temperature Range: Maximum daily temperature minus Minimum daily temperature.
  5. Precipitation Types: Rain, drizzle, snow, sleet, and hailstones.
  6. Rain Gauge Standard: Funnel with sharp rim, graduated cylinder reading in mm, elevated 30 cm in open ground.
  7. Atmospheric Pressure: Force of the air column pressing down on Earth's surface (normal sea level = 1013.25 mb).
  8. Barometer: Measures atmospheric pressure; pressure drops rapidly with increasing elevation.
  9. High Altitude Breathlessness: Lower air density at high mountain altitudes causes reduced oxygen intake.
  10. Wind Cause: Movement of air from regions of high atmospheric pressure to low atmospheric pressure.
  11. Wind Vane vs Anemometer: Wind vane measures direction; Anemometer (rotating cups) measures velocity in km/h.
  12. The Loo: Hot, dry, scorching summer wind blowing across North India during May and June.
  13. Relative Humidity (RH): Actual water vapour present as a percentage of maximum water vapour air can hold at that temperature.
  14. Stevenson Screen: Louvered white wooden shelter elevated 1.25 m to protect thermometers from direct radiation while permitting airflow.
  15. IMD Foundation: Established in 1875 with the motto "Adityat Jayate Vrishti" (From the Sun arises rain).
