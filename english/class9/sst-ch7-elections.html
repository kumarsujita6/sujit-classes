Section 2 Exhaustive Topic-by-Topic Notes

1. Why Do Elections Matter in a Democracy?

In a direct democracy with a small population, all citizens can assemble in a town square to deliberate and vote on every law. However, in modern continental nations with millions or billions of inhabitants, direct governance is physical impossibility. Therefore, citizens govern indirectly by electing trusted representatives.

Elections serve four foundational functions in democratic societies:

  • Equality: Every citizen aged 18 and above possesses exactly one vote of equal legal weight (‘one person, one vote, one value’), eliminating feudal privileges based on birth, gender, caste, or wealth.
  • Representation: Voters choose legislators who mirror their socio-cultural aspirations, debate public issues, and enact laws on their behalf.
  • Accountability: Regular, periodic elections (every 5 years in India) compel elected politicians to perform. If an elected representative fails to fulfil promises or abuses power, voters can peacefully eject them from office at the next election.
  • Legitimacy: Laws and tax policies command voluntary obedience because the government derives its authority directly from the consent of the governed.

2. Electoral Systems: First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) vs Proportional Representation (PR)

The method by which popular votes are translated into legislative seats defines an electoral system. The framers of the Indian Constitution evaluated multiple models and deliberately selected the First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) system for direct elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies, while reserving Proportional Representation with Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV) for indirect elections.

Territory: Country divided into single-member constituencies. • Voter Choice: Voter casts a ballot for a single candidate. • Winning Rule: Candidate securing the highest votes wins, even without securing an absolute majority (>50%). Candidate A: 38% (WINNER) Candidate B: 30% Candidate C: 20% Candidate D: 12% Used in: Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha (India, UK, USA) Strength: Simple, stable executive, local MP accountability. Proportional Representation (PR) • Territory: Large multi-member constituencies or entire country. • Voter Choice: Voter votes for a political party or ranks candidates. • Winning Rule: Parties win seats in direct proportion to their popular vote share (e.g., 40% votes = 40% legislative seats). Party X: 40% Votes → 40 Seats Party Y: 32% Votes → 32 Seats Party Z: 18% Votes → 18 Seats Others: 10% Votes → 10 Seats Used in: Rajya Sabha, President (India), Israel, Netherlands Strength: Mathematically fair, eliminates wasted votes.

3. Statutory Foundation: RPA 1950 & RPA 1951

The constitutional mandate under Articles 324 to 329 is operationalized through comprehensive parliamentary legislation:

  • The Representation of the People Act, 1950 (RPA, 1950):
    • Governs the delimitation of parliamentary and assembly constituencies.
    • Sets voter qualification criteria (Indian citizen, 18+ years of age, ordinarily resident in constituency, sound mind).
    • Governs the preparation, revision, and publication of the Electoral Roll.
  • The Representation of the People Act, 1951 (RPA, 1951):
    • Regulates the actual conduct of elections, notifications, candidate nominations, and security deposits.
    • Prescribes disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Assemblies (e.g., conviction for specific criminal offences resulting in 2+ years imprisonment).
    • Explicitly defines and penalizes Corrupt Practices and Electoral Offences:
      1. Bribery and Inducement: Distributing cash, liquor, gifts, or promising government jobs in exchange for votes.
      2. Appeals to Religion or Caste: Seeking votes or discouraging voting on grounds of religion, race, caste, community, or language (Section 123(3)).
      3. Booth Capturing: Seizing polling booths, intimidating presiding officers, or stuffing ballots.
      4. Misuse of Official Machinery: Utilizing government transport, state helicopters, or official personnel to advance the campaign of the ruling party.

4. The Election Commission of India (ECI): Powers and Independence

Established on 25 January 1950 (celebrated nationwide as National Voters’ Day), the ECI is an autonomous constitutional authority safeguarding electoral purity.

  • Composition: Currently functions as a multi-member body headed by the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and assisted by two Election Commissioners (ECs). Decisions are made by majority consensus.
  • Constitutional Shield of Independence:
    • The CEC cannot be removed arbitrarily by the government. The CEC enjoys the same security of tenure as a Supreme Court judge and can only be removed through a rigorous parliamentary impeachment address supported by a two-thirds majority in both Houses.
    • Conditions of service of the CEC cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.
  • Quasi-Judicial Authority: The ECI adjudicates disputes concerning the recognition of political parties, splits/mergers, and allotment of reserved election symbols under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.

6. Recognition Criteria: National vs State Political Parties

Political parties articulate public grievances, present policy manifestos, and contest elections. Under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, the ECI classifies registered parties into State Parties and National Parties based on empirical electoral performance:

Condition 1: 6% valid votes in State Assembly election + at least 2 MLAs in that Assembly. Condition 2: 6% valid votes in Lok Sabha election from state + at least 1 MP elected to Lok Sabha. Condition 3: At least 3% of total seats in State Assembly or 3 seats, whichever is greater. Condition 4: At least 1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 seats allotted to the state. Condition 5: Secures 8% of total valid votes polled in state (Assembly or Lok Sabha), even with zero seats. Privilege: Reserved exclusive symbol inside that State. National Party Recognition (Any ONE Condition) Condition A: Four-State Vote Share • Secures not less than 6% of valid votes polled in 4 or more States (Lok Sabha / Assembly), • AND wins at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha. Condition B: Lok Sabha Seat Spread • Wins at least 2% of total Lok Sabha seats (i.e., 11 seats out of 543), • AND these MPs are elected from at least 3 States. Condition C: Four-State Recognition • Recognized as a State Party in at least 4 States. Privilege: Exclusive symbol reserved across entire India.

8. Contemporary Electoral Challenges and Reforms

  • Excessive Money Power: Wealthy individuals and corporations funding massive campaigns, distorting the democratic playing field. (Countered by statutory ceiling on candidate expenses and expenditure observers).
  • Criminalization of Politics: Candidates with pending criminal cases contesting elections. (Countered by Supreme Court mandates requiring candidates to publish criminal affidavits in newspapers and TV).
  • Disinformation and Social Media Botnets: Targeted fake news on messaging platforms. (Countered by ECI’s C-VIGIL app, where citizens upload geo-tagged photo/video evidence of MCC violations, requiring officials to act within 100 minutes).
  • Voter Indifference: Low turnout among urban educated youth. (Addressed by ECI’s SVEEP — Systematic Voters’ Education and Electoral Participation programme).

Page 4: Comparative Exercise — Electoral Outcomes in Haritbhumi, Ratnadweep, and Swarnalok

Answer:

  • Haritbhumi (FPTP System): The candidate with the highest votes in each single-member constituency wins, even if they get only 35% of the total vote. It produces a clear, decisive governing majority and strong local representative accountability.
  • Ratnadweep (Majority System): To win, a candidate must secure an absolute majority (more than 50% of valid votes). If no candidate achieves 50% in the first round, a run-off election is held between the top two candidates, ensuring the winner has broad majority support.
  • Swarnalok (Proportional Representation System): The entire territory acts as one multi-member constituency. Parties submit candidate lists and receive seats matching their exact vote share, preventing any votes from being wasted.

Part B: End-of-Chapter Exercises & Activities (Questions 1 to 14)

Question 1: Inclusive Voting Reforms Introduced by the ECI for Specified Groups

Answer:

  • a. People with Disabilities (PwDs):
    1. Accessible Infrastructure: Compulsory ramps at all polling stations, wide doorways, and free wheelchair facilities.
    2. Braille Balloting: EVM balloting units feature embossed Braille numbers beside candidate buttons, accompanied by Braille-printed voter guide slips.
    3. Saksham App: A dedicated mobile app allowing PwD voters to book free wheelchair transport and request volunteer assistance at the booth.
  • b. Service Voters (Armed Forces & Paramilitary):

    Introduction of the Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System (ETPBS). Service personnel stationed in remote border areas receive password-protected encrypted ballots online, cast their vote secretly, and mail them back via registered post.

  • c. Senior Citizens (Above 85 Years of Age):

    Home Voting Facility: Elderly voters aged 85+ can submit Form 12D. A dedicated polling team (accompanied by a videographer and police security) visits the voter’s residence to conduct secret postal balloting at home.

  • d. Prisoners:

    Under Section 62(5) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, any person confined in prison (whether serving a criminal sentence or as an undertrial) is legally disqualified from voting in any election.

  • e. Persons in Preventive Detention:

    Unlike ordinary criminal convicts, individuals held under preventive detention laws (e.g., to preserve public order) are explicitly permitted to vote through postal ballots under Section 62(5) proviso.

Question 2: Functions of the Election Commission of India & Most Critical Function

Answer:

Key Functions of the ECI:

  1. Delimitation & Polling Station Setup: Demarcating constituency borders and setting up accessible polling booths within 2 km of every voter.
  2. Electoral Roll Revision: Annual continuous updates and Special Intensive Revisions (SIR) to enroll new 18-year-old citizens and remove deceased/migrated names.
  3. Election Notification & Candidate Scrutiny: Receiving nomination papers, checking candidate affidavits (assets, criminal records), and deciding on invalid papers.
  4. Symbol Allotment & Party Recognition: Allotting reserved symbols to recognised National and State parties.
  5. Enforcement of Model Code of Conduct (MCC): Ensuring ministers and candidates do not misuse government resources or incite communal hatred.
  6. Counting of Votes and Result Declaration: Overseeing transparent vote counting with 100% VVPAT slip matching for five randomly selected polling booths per assembly segment.

Which Function is Most Important?

The Enforcement of the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) and Supervision of a Level Playing Field is the most critical function. Without strict enforcement of the MCC, the ruling government could misuse public helicopters, police machinery, state advertisements, and treasury funds to unfairly overpower opponents. The MCC guarantees that elections remain a contest among equals, preserving the very soul of democracy.

Question 3: “Elections are the soul of a democracy.” Do you agree? Why or why not?

Answer:

Yes, I completely agree that elections are the soul of a democracy.

  1. Expression of Popular Sovereignty: A constitution may state that power belongs to the people, but it is only through elections that citizens convert this theoretical ideal into real political authority.
  2. Peaceful Renewal and Correction: Elections provide a peaceful, orderly mechanism to replace corrupt or non-performing governments without requiring street violence or military coups.
  3. Universal Dignity: On election day, the vote of a poor daily-wage laborer carries the exact same value as that of a billionaire corporate magnate, actualizing true civic equality.
  4. Consent of the Governed: Rulers who lack an electoral mandate are autocrats. Regular elections ensure that government policies enjoy legitimate public backing.

Question 6: Special Intensive Revision (SIR) Initiative — Objectives and Necessity

Answer:

Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is an exhaustive, door-to-door electoral roll verification campaign conducted periodically by the ECI.

  • Core Objectives:
    1. 100% Voter Enrolment: Ensure that every eligible citizen who has turned 18 is enrolled via Form 6.
    2. Purging Ineligible Entries: Systematically remove names of deceased citizens, permanently migrated families, and duplicate/ghost voter entries.
    3. Error Correction: Rectify spelling errors in names, addresses, or photos on EPIC cards.
  • Necessity for Free and Fair Elections:

    An erroneous or inflated voter list allows bogus voting and impersonation. Conversely, missing names disenfranchise legitimate citizens. An accurate, purified electoral roll is the prerequisite for democratic integrity.

Question 10: What Are the Major Challenges to Conducting Free and Fair Elections?

Answer:

  1. Role of Black Money: Astronomical election expenses encourage the use of unaccounted cash to bribe voters, distribute gifts, and hire crowds.
  2. Criminalization of Politics: Parties often award tickets to candidates with criminal records who possess ‘winnability’ through intimidation and muscle power.
  3. Communal & Caste Polarization: Partisan appeals dividing citizens along religious, regional, or caste identities, violating the spirit of secularism.
  4. Misinformation & Digital Echo Chambers: Viral fake news, deepfake videos, and hate speech disseminated through social messaging apps right before polling day.
  5. Urban and Youth Voter Apathy: Lower voter turnout in wealthy urban constituencies compared to rural districts.

A. Multiple Choice Questions (5 Questions with Conceptual Traps)

MCQ 1: Electoral Systems 1 Mark

Question: In a Lok Sabha constituency, Candidate X gets 32% votes, Candidate Y gets 31%, Candidate Z gets 25%, and others get 12%. Under the First-Past-The-Post system, who is declared elected?

A. Candidate X, because they secured more votes than any other individual candidate.
B. Nobody, because no candidate crossed the 50% absolute majority mark.
C. A run-off election must be conducted between Candidate X and Candidate Y.
D. Candidate X and Candidate Y share the legislative seat.

Correct Answer: A. Candidate X, because they secured more votes than any other individual candidate.

Trap & Explanation: Conceptual Trap: Students confuse the plurality system (FPTP) with the absolute majority system. Under FPTP, whoever gets the highest number of votes wins, even if it is only 32%.

MCQ 2: Electoral Disqualifications 1 Mark

Question: Under Section 62(5) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, who among the following is permitted to vote in an election?

A. A convicted felon serving 5 years in central jail.
B. An undertrial prisoner awaiting judgment in district jail.
C. A person held under lawful preventive detention.
D. An accused person out on police remand.

Correct Answer: C. A person held under lawful preventive detention.

Trap & Explanation: Conceptual Trap: Most students assume undertrials can vote because they are not yet convicted. Section 62(5) of RPA 1951 bars all persons in lawful police or judicial confinement, explicitly exempting ONLY those under preventive detention.

MCQ 3: Model Code of Conduct 1 Mark

Question: The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) comes into legal effect:

A. Exactly 48 hours before polling begins.
B. Immediately when the Election Commission announces the election schedule at a press conference.
C. On the day candidates file their nomination papers.
D. Only after the final list of contesting candidates is published.

Correct Answer: B. Immediately when the Election Commission announces the election schedule at a press conference.

Trap & Explanation: The 48-hour rule applies to the silence period (banning rallies and campaigning). The MCC is triggered the exact minute the ECI announces the poll dates.

MCQ 4: National Party Qualifications 1 Mark

Question: A political party can be recognized as a National Party if it is recognised as a State Party in at least:

A. 2 States
B. 3 States
C. 4 States
D. 5 States

Correct Answer: C. 4 States

Trap & Explanation: Under the Election Symbols Order, 1968, being recognized as a State party in at least four states automatically qualifies a party for National Party status (Condition C).

MCQ 5: Assistive Polling 1 Mark

Question: How long does the printed paper slip remain visible through the transparent window of a VVPAT machine before dropping into the sealed ballot compartment?

A. 3 seconds
B. 5 seconds
C. 7 seconds
D. 10 seconds

Correct Answer: C. 7 seconds

Trap & Explanation: The ECI technical specifications prescribe exactly 7 seconds for the voter to verify the candidate’s serial number, name, and party symbol.

B. Assertion & Reasoning Questions (4 Questions)

Directions: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. (b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A. (c) A is true but R is false. (d) A is false but R is true.

A&R 1: Independence of the CEC

Assertion (A): The Chief Election Commissioner cannot be removed from office at the arbitrary discretion of the Prime Minister or ruling cabinet.
Reason (R): The Indian Constitution grants the CEC security of tenure equivalent to a Supreme Court judge, requiring a parliamentary impeachment address supported by a two-thirds majority in both Houses.

Correct Option: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.

Explanation: To safeguard electoral impartiality, Article 324 shields the CEC with rigorous impeachment protections identical to those of Supreme Court judges.

A&R 2: Nature of the FPTP System

Assertion (A): Under the First-Past-The-Post system, a party that wins 38% of the popular vote can secure over 60% of seats in Parliament.
Reason (R): In single-member constituencies, votes cast for defeated candidates are not counted toward national seat allocation.

Correct Option: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.

Explanation: Because the winner takes all in each constituency, popular votes for opponents do not translate into legislative seats, creating ‘manufactured majorities’.

A&R 3: Offline Architecture of EVMs

Assertion (A): Indian EVMs cannot be hacked remotely via Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or foreign internet servers.
Reason (R): EVMs are standalone, battery-operated machines possessing zero wireless antennae, network ports, or internet hardware connectivity.

Correct Option: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.

Explanation: Total physical isolation from any telecommunication network protects EVMs against remote digital manipulation.

A&R 4: Model Code of Conduct Legal Status

Assertion (A): The Model Code of Conduct is an Act passed by the British Parliament in 1947.
Reason (R): The Model Code of Conduct originated as a voluntary consensus among political parties in Kerala in 1960 and is enforced through ECI’s constitutional powers under Article 324.

Correct Option: (d) A is false but R is true.

Explanation: The MCC is not an act of the British Parliament; it evolved from voluntary consensus among political parties in Kerala and is now enforced by the ECI under Article 324.

C. Short Answer Questions (5 Questions • 2M / 3M)

SA 1: What is the Role of a Returning Officer (RO)? 2 Marks

Answer:

  • Statutory In-Charge: The RO (usually a District Magistrate or Sub-Divisional Magistrate) oversees the entire conduct of elections in a specific parliamentary or assembly constituency.
  • Key Duties: Receives nomination papers, scrutinizes candidate affidavits, allots election symbols, supervises polling, manages vote counting, and officially declares the winning candidate.
SA 2: How Does the ETPBS System Function for Service Voters? 3 Marks

Answer:

  1. Digital Transmission: The Returning Officer electronically transmits encrypted ballot papers to registered service voters (e.g., soldiers stationed along borders).
  2. Secret Marking: The service voter downloads the postal ballot using a secure OTP, records their choice in secret, and places it in a sealed envelope.
  3. Postal Dispatch: The sealed postal ballot is mailed back free of cost to the RO before the counting deadline.
SA 3: Explain the Silence Period of 48 Hours 2 Marks

Answer:

  • Statutory Ban: Under Section 126 of RPA 1951, all public meetings, roadshows, loudspeakers, and television campaign broadcasts must end 48 hours before voting concludes.
  • Democratic Rationale: Provides voters a peaceful, unpressured ‘cooling-off’ window to reflect calmly and make an independent choice free from campaign clamour.
SA 4: What is the Purpose of Candidate Affidavits (Form 26)? 3 Marks

Answer:

  1. Criminal Background: Discloses pending criminal charges framed by courts and past convictions.
  2. Financial Assets & Liabilities: Details movable and immovable assets, bank balances, and unpaid government dues of the candidate, spouse, and dependents.
  3. Educational Qualifications: Lists school and university degrees, enabling voters to make well-informed choices.
SA 5: How Does the C-VIGIL App Empower Citizens? 2 Marks

Answer:

  • Real-Time Reporting: Ordinary citizens can record photos or 2-minute videos of Model Code violations (cash distribution, liquor supply, hate speech) and upload them instantly.
  • Time-Bound Resolution: The app automatically tags GPS location; flying squads are dispatched to the spot, and officials must resolve the complaint within 100 minutes.

D. Long Answer Questions (3 Questions • 5M with Marking Breakdown)

LA 1: Evaluate the Powers, Independence, and Limitations of the ECI 5 Marks

Marking Breakdown:

  • Constitutional basis & composition [1 Mark]
  • Autonomous powers (MCC, scheduling, transfers) [1.5 Marks]
  • Constitutional safeguards guaranteeing independence [1.5 Marks]
  • Limitations / challenges faced [1 Mark]

Comprehensive Answer:

  1. Constitutional Basis: Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction, and control of all elections to Parliament and State Legislatures in the ECI, established on 25 January 1950.
  2. Comprehensive Powers:
    • Enforces the Model Code of Conduct, censuring political leaders, banning provocative speeches, and stopping transfer of bureaucrats during elections.
    • Commands the entire civil and police administration during elections; all government officials on election duty function under ECI deputation.
    • Cancels polling or orders fresh repolling in booths afflicted by violence or machine defects.
  3. Constitutional Independence:
    • The CEC can only be removed through parliamentary impeachment address equivalent to a Supreme Court judge.
    • Salary and allowances are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
  4. Limitations & Challenges:
    • ECI does not possess permanent police forces of its own; it relies on state police and central paramilitary forces.
    • Cannot deregister corrupt political parties directly; must refer cases to courts.
    • Struggles to police encrypted social media messaging apps spreading misinformation.
LA 2: Compare First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) with Proportional Representation (PR) 5 Marks

Marking Breakdown:

  • Core definition & mechanics of both systems [1.5 Marks]
  • Key advantages of FPTP [1 Mark]
  • Key advantages of PR [1 Mark]
  • Why India adopted FPTP for Lok Sabha and PR for Rajya Sabha [1.5 Marks]

Comprehensive Answer:

  1. Core Distinction:
    • FPTP: Country is divided into single-member constituencies. The candidate who secures the most votes wins, regardless of whether they cross 50%.
    • PR: Seats in the legislature are allocated in strict mathematical proportion to the percentage of popular votes polled by each party.
  2. Why India Chose FPTP for Lok Sabha and Assembly Elections:
    • Simplicity: In 1950, when the majority of Indian voters were illiterate, FPTP offered an uncomplicated method: pick one preferred local candidate and stamp their symbol.
    • Clear Legislative Majority & Stable Government: FPTP tends to give a decisive parliamentary majority to the leading party, preventing chaotic multi-party instability.
    • Direct Local Link: Every citizen has a specific local MP/MLA answerable for constituency roads, hospitals, and grievances.
  3. Why PR-STV Was Chosen for Rajya Sabha & President:
    • The Rajya Sabha represents the federal interests of States; PR ensures that minority political parties in state assemblies gain fair representation in the Upper House.
LA 3: Detail the Assistive Voting Measures Ensuring ‘No Voter Left Behind’ 5 Marks

Marking Breakdown:

  • Concept of inclusive democracy [0.5 Mark]
  • Reforms for Persons with Disabilities (PwDs) [1.5 Marks]
  • Home voting facility for senior citizens (85+) [1.5 Marks]
  • Reforms for Service Voters & remote geography [1.5 Marks]

Comprehensive Answer:

  1. The Vision: The ECI operates under the motto “No Voter to Be Left Behind”, ensuring every eligible citizen can exercise their franchise with dignity.
  2. PwD Empowerment Measures:
    • Universal Physical Access: Mandatory concrete ramps, non-slip flooring, and wheelchairs at every polling station.
    • Braille Technology: EVM balloting units feature embossed Braille numerals for visually impaired citizens.
    • Saksham App: Allows disabled voters to request pick-up and drop-off transport and special assistance.
  3. Home Voting for Seniors (85+) and Benchmark PwDs:
    • Eligible voters submit Form 12D before election notification deadlines.
    • A mobile polling team (two polling officers, a videographer, and police escorts) visits the voter’s home, sets up a secure voting compartment, and conducts secret postal balloting.
  4. Reaching Extreme Remote Frontiers:
    • Polling booths are established in high-altitude Himalayan snow peaks, dense rainforests, and offshore islands (such as a dedicated booth for a single voter in Gir Forest, Gujarat).
    • Service personnel on frozen Himalayan borders vote securely via the Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System (ETPBS).

E. Competency-Based Case Studies (2 Real-World Scenarios)

Case Study 1: The Model Code of Conduct in Sundarnagar 4 Marks

Case Narrative: Three days after the ECI declared general elections, the Minister for Public Works in Sundarnagar organized a lavish ceremony to lay the foundation stone for a multi-crore highway. During the event, government transport buses were used to ferry spectators, and the Minister promised free electricity connections to all attendees if his party was re-elected. An alert local citizen used the C-VIGIL app to upload live photos and video footage of the event. Within 45 minutes, an ECI flying squad arrived on the scene.

Questions & Step-by-Step Solutions:

1. Identify two distinct violations of the Model Code of Conduct in this incident. (1.5 Marks)
Answer:

  1. Laying the foundation stone and announcing new welfare promises (free electricity) after the election schedule was announced.
  2. Misusing government transport buses and public resources to benefit the ruling party’s campaign.

2. How did digital technology enable swift accountability in this scenario? (1 Mark)
Answer: The C-VIGIL app allowed the citizen to upload geo-tagged photo/video proof instantly, alerting the District Election Officer and enabling flying squads to intervene within 45 minutes.

3. What immediate punitive actions can the ECI take against the Minister? (1.5 Marks)
Answer: The ECI can issue a show-cause notice, bar the Minister from campaigning for 48 to 72 hours, order the seizure of government vehicles used, and direct police to register an FIR under the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

Case Study 2: Election Petition in High Court 4 Marks

Case Narrative: In a closely fought state election, Candidate A won by a razor-thin margin of 24 votes against Candidate B. Candidate B discovered that the local Returning Officer had improperly rejected 85 valid postal ballots without providing written justification. Candidate B filed an Election Petition challenging the declaration of results.

Questions & Step-by-Step Solutions:

1. Which legal forum has the jurisdiction to entertain an Election Petition under Indian law? (1 Mark)
Answer: Under Article 329 and the Representation of the People Act, 1951, election petitions must be filed directly before the State High Court.

2. Under what statutory ground can an election outcome be declared void? (1.5 Marks)
Answer: An election can be set aside if the court finds that there was improper rejection of valid votes, improper acceptance of void votes, non-compliance with the Constitution/RPA 1951, or commission of corrupt practices (bribery, booth capturing).

3. What remedy can the High Court grant if Candidate B’s claims are substantiated? (1.5 Marks)
Answer: The High Court can order a recount of the 85 rejected postal ballots, declare the election of Candidate A void, and declare Candidate B duly elected if the recounted tally shows Candidate B received more valid votes.

Section 6 Exam Hall Common Mistakes & 15 Crisp Revision Points

Top 5 Conceptual Pitfalls to Avoid

⚠ Mistake 1: Confusing Absolute Majority with Plurality in FPTP

The Trap: Writing that a candidate must score more than 50% of total votes to win a Lok Sabha seat.
The Correction: Under FPTP (plurality system), whoever secures the highest number of votes among contestants wins, even if their vote share is only 30%.

⚠ Mistake 2: Assuming Undertrial Prisoners Are Permitted to Vote

The Trap: Believing undertrials can vote because they have not been proven guilty.
The Correction: Under Section 62(5) of RPA 1951, all persons in lawful prison custody (both convicts and undertrials) are legally barred from voting. ONLY persons under preventive detention can vote.

⚠ Mistake 3: Believing the CEC Can Be Fired by the Prime Minister

The Trap: Thinking the government can dismiss an uncooperative CEC at will.
The Correction: The CEC enjoys constitutional security equivalent to a Supreme Court judge; removal requires a two-thirds majority impeachment address in both Houses of Parliament.

⚠ Mistake 4: Confusing RPA 1950 with RPA 1951

The Trap: Mixing up the subject matter of the two election statutes.
The Correction: RPA 1950 governs constituency delimitation and voter roll preparation; RPA 1951 governs the actual conduct of elections, corrupt practices, and candidate disqualifications.

⚠ Mistake 5: Assuming EVMs Store Results on the Cloud

The Trap: Writing that Indian EVMs send results to online central servers.
The Correction: Indian EVMs are 100% standalone, offline, battery-operated machines with zero internet, Bluetooth, or wireless hardware.

15 Ultra-Crisp Revision Points (Read Before Entering Exam Hall)

  1. Constitutional Mandate: Article 324 vests superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the Election Commission of India.
  2. Founding Date: ECI established on 25 January 1950; observed annually as National Voters’ Day.
  3. Electoral Plurality: Lok Sabha and State Assemblies follow First-Past-The-Post (FPTP); candidate with the highest votes wins.
  4. Proportional Representation: Rajya Sabha and Presidential elections use PR with Single Transferable Vote (STV).
  5. Statutory Twins: RPA 1950 governs delimitation and rolls; RPA 1951 governs election conduct, corrupt practices, and offences.
  6. CEC Protection: Can be removed only through parliamentary impeachment with two-thirds majority in both Houses.
  7. Model Code of Conduct: Activated the moment election dates are announced; prevents government resource misuse.
  8. Silence Period: Campaigning, rallies, and loudspeakers must stop 48 hours before polling closes.
  9. EVM Standalone Integrity: Indian EVMs are offline, battery-operated, and contain no wireless hardware.
  10. VVPAT Audit: Displays candidate serial, name, and symbol on paper for 7 seconds before dropping into a locked box.
  11. Home Voting (Form 12D): Senior citizens aged 85+ and benchmark PwDs can vote from home via videographed secret ballot.
  12. ETPBS System: Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System enables armed service personnel to vote remotely.
  13. Prisoner Voting Ban: Section 62(5) of RPA 1951 disqualifies all prisoners except persons under preventive detention.
  14. National Party Rule: Recognized as State party in at least 4 states, OR 6% votes in 4 states + 4 MPs, OR 2% Lok Sabha seats across 3 states.
  15. C-VIGIL App: Enables citizens to report MCC violations with GPS location; resolved by flying squads within 100 minutes.
